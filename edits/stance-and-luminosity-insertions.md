# Insertions: the stance paragraph (all four books) and a luminosity passage (*Nāgārjuna's Middle Way*)

Edits to apply to the four book manuscripts, in line with `canon/mind-phenomena-and-luminous-clarity.md`.
Each one gives the file, the exact place, and the text to insert. Anchor sentences are quoted verbatim
from the current manuscripts.

Only *The Transition from Anatta to Emptiness* and *The Observer and the Taste of Presence* have a section called
"Note on Method". For the other two books the paragraph goes in the section that does the same job:
"Lineage and Conversation" in *Substrateless World*, and "The translation and the reading" in *Nāgārjuna's Middle Way*.

The paragraph is the same in all four books, adjusted only for each book's voice (first person in *Anatta to
Emptiness*) and for what each book covers.

**File:** `substrateless-world-v67-A4.docx` (and `substrateless-world-v67.docx`)
**Section:** Introduction → "Lineage and Conversation"
**Place:** a new paragraph after the first paragraph of that section, which ends:
> "…These sources are introduced where they are used; the book does not assume familiarity with them."

**Insert:**

> Within that lineage, the view of this book is a rangtong reading of Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*:
> everything, mind included, is empty of its own nature, and that emptiness is a non-implicative negation, which
> removes what was never there and puts nothing in its place. On luminous clarity the book follows Nyingma
> Dzogchen, which takes the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* as its view. Luminosity is the knowing clarity of appearing

> own nature, as the shentong ("empty of other") reading holds.

**Insert:**

### 3b. The luminosity passage (new section in Chapter 18)

**File:** `mmk/mmk-18.html` (then rebuild the book and the site page)
**Chapter:** 18, Examination of Self and Entities
**Place:** a new `h2` section at the end of the explanation, after the explanation of 18.9 (which ends "…Nāgārjuna does
not install a positive absolute behind appearances.") and the explanation of 18.10. Put it before "What is left
standing".

**Insert:**

> ## Is reality luminous?
>
> Readers who know the later traditions will ask a question the verses do not raise. Tibetan Buddhism, and Dzogchen
> above all, speaks of the nature of mind as luminous (*'od gsal*), and an early discourse already says "luminous,
> monks, is this mind" (Aṅguttara Nikāya 1.51–52). Verse 18.9 describes reality only by what is absent from it. Is
> something luminous left over when the elaborations stop?
>
> The word does not appear in the verses, so the answer has to come from how Nāgārjuna argues. Suppose luminosity
> were a radiant awareness behind appearances, existing from its own side. Then it would be one more thing with
> own-nature, and every chapter of this book would apply to it. It would have to be found either as one with what it
> lights up or as other than it, and it is found as neither (18.10). Calling it empty of everything else but not of
> itself only moves the own-nature to a new place. That is the reading called "empty of other" (*gzhan stong*), and
> it is not the one this book follows.
>
> There is another reading. The Nyingma Dzogchen tradition, which takes the Middle Way as its view, holds it, and
> this book follows it. Luminosity is not a light behind appearing. It is the knowing clarity of appearing itself:
> a sound heard as sound, a cold felt as cold, with no one standing apart to receive them. Nothing blocks this
> showing. The Tibetan word is *ma 'gags pa*, unobstructed, and it does not mean that anything lasts. The clarity is
> empty in the same way as everything else, because it too arises dependently. The clarity of a sound is not found
> apart from the sound, the ear and the hearing, and whatever arises dependently is empty (24.19). Clarity and
> emptiness are not two, and neither is the ground of the other.
>
> Read this way, verse 18.9 and the luminosity teachings describe one seeing from two sides. The verse names what
> is absent: own-nature, elaboration, plurality. The Dzogchen word names how appearing is when nothing is added:
> vivid, clear and unowned. "At peace" does not mean dim. When elaboration stops in emptiness (18.5), nothing goes
> dark, and nothing new is installed to shine.

**Add to "Words to know" (Chapter 18), and so to the Glossary:**

> **Luminosity** (Tibetan *'od gsal*): in Dzogchen, the clarity of appearing. In this book, the knowing clarity of
> appearing itself, empty like everything else; not a light or an awareness behind appearances. (Ch. 18)

**Add to "In the books" (Chapter 18):**

> - *The Logical Necessity of a Substrateless World*, §7.10, "Objection: What of the Luminous Mind?"
> - *The Transition from Anatta to Emptiness*, "Luminous Clarity Without Background Awareness"
> - *The Observer and the Taste of Presence*, Chapter 2 (luminosity as the knowing clarity of the display) and
>   Chapter 19 (the objection from self-awareness)

## 4. *The Observer and the Taste of Presence*

**Status: applied** (2026-10-22) in GitHub `JohnTLM/EBooks`, branch `claude/confident-planck-tirt7e`, together with the three glossary fixes below; PDF rebuilt (90 pages).

**File:** `ebook-combined.md` (Drive `website/bonus-articles/final-2026-10-22`; also GitHub `JohnTLM/EBooks`) and
`ebook-assembled-1-front-matter-and-part-I.md`

**Place:** a new paragraph after the paragraph that begins "The reader should also know that the traditions do not
agree on the synthesis." and ends:
> "…as a reading that can be defended, not as a settled point."

That puts it before "The book's position is best described as a Madhyamaka reading of Dzogchen…".

**Insert:**

> The view itself can be stated in one sentence. It is a rangtong reading of the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*:
> everything, mind included, is empty of its own nature (MMK 24.19), and that emptiness is a non-implicative
> negation, which removes the fixing and puts nothing in its place. On luminous clarity the book follows Nyingma
> Dzogchen, which takes the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* as its view. Luminosity is the knowing clarity of appearing
> itself (Chapter 2), as empty as everything else. It is never an ultimate that is empty of other things but not of
> its own nature, as the shentong ("empty of other") reading holds.

**Related glossary fixes in Appendix B.** Without these, the new paragraph would contradict the glossary.
- **Rigpa**: after "the pristine awareness that is the basis itself", add: "The book does not take awareness as a
  basis; see the Note on Method."
- **Lhun grub**: after the Higgins sentence, add: "The book does not read this as an awareness from which luminosity
  issues; luminosity is the clarity of appearing itself."
- **Luminosity**: replace "and 'the luminosity of emptiness.'" with "The book uses the first sense only: luminosity
  is empty, and emptiness has no luminosity of its own (see Emptiness)."
