**Structure**

Front matter
- **Preface:** the book follows one line of argument: observer → substrates → two modes of seeing → "beyond cause and effect". It names its Madhyamaka–Dzogchen reading as a synthesis.
- **Note on Method:** separates classical material from eight kinds of authorial synthesis. Lists four departures from standard Dzogchen presentation (basis, unproduced, openness/"nature", recognition).
- **How to Read This Book:** four parts, exercises, the Appendix C path, the appendices.

Part I: The Observer
- **Ch 1, The Witness Behind Experience:** the chariot analysis, then the same looking applied to the seer. "The one who sees is not found apart from the seeing."
- **Ch 2, Vividness Without a Viewer:** without the witness, experience is more vivid, not blank. The red patch shows dependence. Luminosity is read as the knowing clarity of the display, and the Bahiya sutta is introduced.

Part II: The Logics and the Substrates
- **Ch 3, Ignorance and Its Habit (new in the 23-chapter edition):** ignorance (*avidyā* / *ma rigpa*) is a habit of not seeing, not a missing belief. The pattern runs before reasoning, and insight gets absorbed by it. The felt contraction is the eighth synthesis. The habit has four supports, and the practice is to see the pattern, not fight it.
- **Ch 4, Two Logics:** the logic of substance against the logic of dependency. The chapter shows substantial thinking in space, time and grammar. "Logic" means the necessity of a consequence, and the conclusion is a negation without a thesis.
- **Ch 5, The Given:** Locke and Sellars; Dignāga and Dharmakīrti's two-storey epistemology; Candrakīrti's three moves; Tsongkhapa's refusal of own-characteristic even conventionally. Efficacy is a relation.
- **Ch 6, Conceptual All the Way Down?:** McDowell against Dreyfus. Appearance and concept are co-designated. "There is no down."
- **Ch 7, Energy:** energy is a frame-dependent, Noether-derived conserved quantity, not a substrate.
- **Ch 8, Potentiality and Plasticity:** potentials are designated relationally. Plasticity is bounded by conditions. *Ma 'gags pa* (unobstructed) is preferred to "potential".
- **Ch 9, Continuity Without a Carrier:** Nāgasena's lamp, the melody. Continuity is designated, not carried.
- **Ch 10, The Candle and the Weight of Watching:** anaesthesia, Dōgen's firewood and ash, the fear of death without a possessor.

Part III: Two Modes and the Middle Way
- **Ch 11, Change Without a Changer:** walker and walking (MMK 2.18–21). Noun and verb are designated together.
- **Ch 12, The Eight Extremes:** the extremes as eight ways of asking where a thing is. Dependence and unfindability are one fact.
- **Ch 13, Identity, Convention and the Criteria of Truth:** identity as reliable picking-out, and Tsongkhapa's three criteria. "There is one appearance, seen two ways."
- **Ch 14, Analysis and the Dividing Mind:** *vikalpa*, analysis turned against itself, and MMK 24.18–19.
- **Ch 15, Reification and Svabhāva as a Way of Seeing:** abstraction followed by reification. Svabhāva lies in the seeing, not in the content.
- **Ch 16, Non-Dividing Seeing:** *avikalpa*, the duck-rabbit and reading. The two modes are not stages.

Part IV: Beyond Cause and Effect
- **Ch 17, The Three Assumptions and the Fourth:** the author's scheme (essence, causal power, foundation, then "why"). Two claims, the flame, three senses of "cause", "no why and because".
- **Ch 18, Spontaneous Presence:** *lhun grub* without causelessness. The two questions, flat non-hierarchy, and the insubstantial reading of self-appearing.
- **Ch 19, Two Traditions, One Risk:** *rang rig* and the svasaṃvedana debate (blade and lamp), Mipham via Pettit, Higgins, Longchenpa's ground, *rang grol* against suppression.
- **Ch 20, The Gap and Its Absence:** the subject–object gap was never load-bearing. Its absence is a stopping, not an achievement.
- **Ch 21, The Taste of Pacified Reification:** Bahiya (Udāna 1.10), and open against contrived seeing.
- **Ch 22, Painted Food, Painted Hunger:** Xiangyan, Dōgen's *Gabyō*, the meal exercise.
- **Ch 23, Recognition, Not Effort:** recognition is decisive but not produced. Non-implicative negation, the three recognitions, openness also empty, and clear knowing as the practice that remains.

- **C:** the Beginner's Companion in eleven rounds.

## 2. Core theses

1. **The observer is not found apart from the seeing.** "The one who sees is not found apart from the seeing." (Ch 1) This is a narrow claim: "This does not mean that nobody is seeing."
2. **Absence of the witness leaves vividness, not blankness.** "What changes is not the vividness. What changes is the architecture." (Ch 2)
3. **Luminosity is the knowing clarity of appearing, not a substance behind it.** "the clarity is not behind the display. It is the knowing clarity of the display itself." The book labels this as its own reading of the luminosity tradition. (Ch 2)
4. **Ignorance is a habit of not seeing, not missing information.** It runs before reasoning and absorbs insight. "The framework does not merely survive the argument. It produces the argument." (Ch 3)
5. **Two logics share one vocabulary.** The logic of substance starts with things. The logic of dependency asks how anything is identified as a thing and finds it "identified only in dependence: on parts, causes, contrasts and the designation under which it is picked out." (Ch 4)
6. **Madhyamaka logic is ordinary logic used as reductio.** "It means the necessity of a consequence." It relies on non-contradiction. Its conclusion is "a negation that removes inherent existence and installs nothing in its place." The tetralemma refusals refuse a presupposition: "The question had nothing to be about." (Ch 4)
7. **There is no Given.** Prāsaṅgika denies establishment by own-characteristic "not even conventionally". Valid cognition is kept. "Causal efficacy is itself a relation." (Ch 5)
8. **There is no conceptual or non-conceptual floor either.** Appearance and concept are "designated together, like long and short". "There is no down." (Ch 6)
9. **Energy, potentiality and continuity are designated regularities, not substrates.** "Conservation is a designated regularity of the highest reliability. It is not a passenger." (Ch 7) "Appearances are exactly as plastic as their conditions allow, and no more." (Ch 8) Continuity is "designated across the moments, not carried by something that runs through them." (Ch 9)
10. **Change has no changer, and the extremes are questions about whereabouts.** "The extremes are not eight separate errors. They are eight ways of asking where a thing is." "Dependence and unfindability are one fact, seen from the side of convention and from the side of analysis." (Ch 12)
11. **Convention is fully valid and is the only validity.** "conventional truth is the only kind of validity there is, and it is fully valid." "Ultimate truth is not a higher world. It is the emptiness of this one." "There is one appearance, seen two ways." (Ch 13)
12. **Analysis is a response to the dividing habit, not a standing activity.** "It shows that the dividing mind is a formation, and not a discovery." (Ch 14) "It works when there is a habit to reify, and it reaches its end when there is nothing left to examine." (Ch 16)
13. **Svabhāva is a way of seeing, not a content.** "The difference lies entirely in the seeing." (Ch 15)
14. **Non-dividing seeing is not a stage or a second reality.** "It is appearing as it is when no fixing occurs." (Ch 16)
15. **The two claims describe one event.** Analysis leaves no foundation, and appearances arise when conditions align. "They are two statements about one appearing." "Cause" in the valid sense is conditional alignment or a designated relatum, never an intrinsic producer. (Ch 17)
16. **"Why do appearances spring forth?" is the last reification.** "It searches for permission to stop searching." "No why and because" marks "the point where the question of cause no longer has a referent". It is the author's step. (Ch 17)
17. **Spontaneous presence is the same appearing when reification stops.** "Spontaneous presence, then, does not name an additional ingredient sitting on top of emptiness." "Neither is a higher description, and neither grounds the other." (Ch 18)
18. **Flat, ownerless appearing.** "The alignment of conditions is the appearing, shown without a layer or an intermediary." "Self" in self-appearing "names only that the appearance is not borrowed from another place". (Ch 18)
19. **The gap's absence is a stopping, not an accomplishment.** "If the gap is a construction, its absence is a stopping rather than an accomplishment." (Ch 20)
20. **Open and contrived are two ways of meeting the same appearing.** "The difference lies in the seeing, not in emptiness, and not in a seer standing behind it." Recognising it "takes clear knowing". (Ch 21)
21. **Designation is not escaped, it is seen through.** "Realisation did not escape designation. It saw through the demand that there be something unpainted behind it." (Ch 22)
22. **Recognition is decisive but not produced.** "Freedom is not produced, and practice does not make it. What practice does is undo the habit that obscures it." (Ch 23)
23. **Negation must stay non-implicative.** "If 'the nature of each appearance' becomes a positive ground beneath appearances, the disease of reification has returned in a new form." (Ch 23)
24. **Openness is also empty.** "It is not something one has. It is the absence of what one was holding." (Ch 23)
25. **Clear knowing is the practice that remains.** "Undoing the habit that fixed them requires clear knowing, and that clear knowing is the practice that remains." (Ch 23, closing)

## 3. Key terms

### 3a. Appendix B glossary, captured in full

Each entry gives the term as spelled in the book, followed by the gloss in the book's wording. Some entries are lightly condensed, but every substantive clause is kept.

- **Abandonment.** "The ending of the afflictions, possible only because they are empty."
- **Afflictions** (*kleśa*). "States that disturb the mind and drive suffering: craving, aversion and delusion."
- **Apoha.** "Exclusion. On Dharmakīrti's account, a universal is understood by excluding what it is not, rather than by pointing to a common essence."
- **Avikalpa.** "Non-conceptual awareness: registering what presents without the names, categories and part-whole structure that thought adds."
- **Basis** (*gzhi*).
  - "In Dzogchen, the primordial state of any sentient being, from which path and fruition are said to unfold; knowledge of it is *rigpa*."
  - "Longchenpa treats it as a ground only from a relative perspective, empty of being a ground in the true nature of reality."
  - "This book reads the basis as the emptiness of appearing, not as a ground behind appearances. See the Note on Method."
  - Source: Wikipedia, "Ground (Dzogchen)", summarising Longchenpa.
- **Clarity** (*gsal ba*).
  - "In Dzogchen, the clear nature (*rang bzhin*) of the basis, one of its three aspects together with its empty essence (*ngo bo*) and its unobstructed compassion (*thugs rje*)."
  - "In this book, the knowing clarity of appearing itself, not a substance behind it. See Luminosity."
  - Source: Rangjung Yeshe Wiki, "primordial ground" and "ngo bo rang bzhin thugs rje gsum". The entry says: "The book's own gloss is stated as a gloss."
- **Conditions** (*pratyaya*). "What something arises in dependence on. The Abhidharma listed four kinds."
- **Contrivance** (in this book). "The same unfixed appearing, met with the habit of fixing still operating. Its counterpart is openness. This is a term of the book's own, not a standard Dzogchen term."
- **Conventional truth** (*saṃvṛti-satya*). "The level of valid designation, in which causal explanation and the words 'why' and 'because' are usable."
- **Dependent arising** (*pratītyasamutpāda*). "Things appear only in dependence on other things, and on the way they are picked out."
- **Dependent designation.** "Things are named and known in dependence on the conditions and the naming itself. Dependent designation is not a compromise between existence and non-existence."
- **Dharmatā** (*chos nyid*).
  - "In the standard sources, the true nature of phenomena, often glossed as the intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*) of dharmas, which is constant and transcends discrimination."
  - "This book uses 'nature' for the way things are, when they are not reified, in a narrower sense than that gloss. It avoids the term for emptiness itself. The difference is stated where the term is first used."
- **Elaboration** (*prapañca*). "The mind's spinning-out of 'is' and 'is not' around something it takes to be there."
- **Eight extremes.** "Arising and ceasing, permanence and annihilation, coming and going, identity and difference."
- **Emptiness** (*śūnyatā*, *stong pa nyid*). "The lack of intrinsic nature. Not a separate reality behind phenomena, and not itself a hidden substrate. Emptiness has no intrinsic nature of its own, and this book does not call it open, closed or luminous in itself."
- **Implicative and non-implicative negation.** "Non-implicative negation (*prasajya-pratiṣedha*) denies without installing a replacement. Implicative negation (*paryudāsa*) denies and installs a positive ground in its place."
- **Lhun grub.** "Spontaneous presence. What dependent arising looks like once no producer is sought. Not an uncaused ground. In Higgins's account (via KMaps), spontaneous presence is the form in which the basic openness of awareness surges forth as luminosity."
- **Luminosity** (*od gsal*; note the spelling, which lacks the leading apostrophe of *'od gsal*).
  - "In Dzogchen, a term that is categorised under the clarity aspect of the nature of mind, and related to *lhun grub*."
  - "In this book, the knowing clarity of appearance, not a substance behind it."
  - "Rangjung Yeshe gives *stong pa'i 'od gsal* as 'empty luminosity' and 'the luminosity of emptiness.' See Clarity."
- **Non-arising** (*anutpāda*). "No arising found from a thing's own side. This is not the same as no causes."
- **Openness** (in this book).
  - "The way unfixed appearing meets when it is seen without fixing. It is not a property of emptiness or of the basis, and it does not explain why appearing occurs. Its counterpart is contrivance."
  - "This is a departure from the sources. In the sources, openness appears as a characterisation of the ground and of awareness: *rig stong* (cognitive intensity and openness) and *gsal stong* (luminosity and openness, or the unity of clarity and openness), both linked to *lhun grub*. The book's usage is narrower."
  - Confirmed: Higgins via KMaps; Rangjung Yeshe for *gsal stong*.
- **Own-nature** (*svabhāva*).
  - "A nature a thing would have from its own side, apart from everything else. Defined in MMK 15.2 as non-contingent and not dependent on anything other than itself. Madhyamaka argues that nothing has it, and that emptiness is not one either."
  - "Distinguish from nature as the way things are (dharmatā). The Tibetan *rang bzhin* is used for both, and in Dzogchen also for the ultimate nature, unaltered because the basis is spontaneously accomplished. The book distinguishes these by context."
- **Primordial purity** (*ka dag*).
  - "The Dzogchen description of the basis as pure from the beginning, and a way of signifying emptiness. In this book, read as the unfixed character of appearing, not as an uncaused source."
  - Confirmed: Higgins (via KMaps), "a term signifying primordial purity as a way of signifying emptiness," unique to rNying ma tantric literature.
  - Namkhai Norbu: "uncreated, ever pure, and self-perfected, not something that has to be constructed," which supports "unproduced" in this sense.
  - The Buddha-Nature site glosses it as the empty nature of phenomena. *Tshig mdzod chen mo*: pure from the beginning, free from stain.
- **Rang grol.** "Self-release: thought dissolves when it is not followed or fixed." Standard in Dzogchen and Mahāmudrā (Wikipedia, "Dzogchen"; Studybuddhism glossary).
- **Rang rig.** "Self-cognizing awareness (Sanskrit *svasaṃvitti*)." The Dzogchen position on it is debated: Mipham's *Beacon of Certainty* (Pettit 1999) defends Dzogchen in Madhyamaka terms, and Higgins (2013) reads Longchenpa as rejecting self-reflective awareness. Chapter 19 presents these readings.
- **Recognition** (*ngo shes*; *rang ngo shes* for self-recognition).
  - "In Dzogchen, the central term of the path. Knowledge of the basis (*rigpa*) comes from recognising it, and recognition is often identified with awakening."
  - "The book does not dispute that recognition is decisive. Its chapter title, 'Recognition, Not Effort' (Chapter 23), means only that recognition is not produced by effort or accumulated as a state." See the Note on Method, fourth departure.
- **Reification.** "Taking an abstraction, or an appearance, to exist as a thing in its own right, standing beneath the particulars."
- **Rigpa** (*rig pa*).
  - "In Dzogchen, the knowledge of the basis, and the pristine awareness that is the basis itself. Its opposite is *ma rigpa*, ignorance, the failure to recognise the basis. Sanskrit *vidyā* ('knowledge')."
  - "The book's 'knowing clarity' is a gloss on this, not a translation."
  - Ācārya Malcolm Smith translates rigpa as *vidyā*, prefers to leave it untranslated, and holds that "awareness" is wrong.
- **Svalakṣaṇa.** "The particular, what characterises itself alone, in Dignāga's and Dharmakīrti's epistemology." Candrakīrti answers "a view closely resembling Dignāga's" (Arnold 2003, JIABS).
- **Ultimate truth** (*paramārtha-satya*). "Not a higher world, but the emptiness of the conventional one."
- **Unobstructed** (*ma 'gags pa*). "Nothing blocks the display, and there is no store behind it from which the display comes. Not that something lasts for ever."
- **Vikalpa.** "Conceptual construction: the mode of mind that divides, names and asks which part carries identity."

### 3b. Terms used in a fixed sense in the body but not in the glossary

- **Logic of substance / logic of dependency** (Ch 4). These are the book's master pair. The logic of substance "starts with things and then asks what happens to them". The logic of dependency starts with how a thing is identified.
- **Designation / designated relatum** (Ch 17). "A cause is a designated relatum within a web, and it is nothing over and above its designation." The other two senses of cause are **intrinsic producer** (rejected) and **conditional alignment** (affirmed conventionally).
- **Standing-behind** (Ch 15, 20): the ground produced by reifying an abstraction.
- **The Given** (capital G), and the **two-storey epistemology** (Ch 5).
- **Vividness / immediacy** (Ch 2, 20). "Vividness here is not intensity added to ordinary perception. It is the absence of the mediation that normally makes perception feel distant."
- **Knowing clarity** (Ch 2; Rigpa and Clarity entries): the book's gloss on luminosity and rigpa.
- **Taste of presence / taste of pacified reification** (Ch 15, 21): "This taste is the mark that the reifying habit has been seen through. It is not a higher stage on a path."
- **Open seeing / contrived seeing** (Ch 21, 23).
- **Clear knowing** (Ch 21, 23): required for recognition and for undoing.
- **Self-appearing, insubstantial reading** (Ch 18). Its "flat" arrangement means no hierarchy.
- **The fourth assumption / the last spell / last reification** (Ch 17).
- **Non-dividing seeing / dividing mind** (Ch 14, 16).
- **Seeing-as** (Ch 16, citing *Substrateless World* §10.4).
- **Felt contraction** (Ch 3): "the seer-seen split is felt as contraction, and contraction is felt as a self."
- ***Avidyā* / *ma rigpa*** (Ch 3). "*Ma rigpa* is not a translation of *avidyā*. The two name the same root… and the overlap should not be taken for identity."
- **Negation without a thesis** (Ch 4), which equals non-implicative negation (Ch 23).
- ***Kalpanā*** (Ch 14), paired with *vikalpa*.
- **Means of knowledge** (Ch 5): perception, inference, testimony, analogy as the world uses them. ***Pramāṇasamuccaya***, ***kalpanāpoḍha*** and "non-erroneous" appear in Appendix D.
- ***Svasaṃvedana*** (Ch 19), set against ***svasaṃvitti*** (glossary Rang rig). Two Sanskrit forms for self-awareness are used.
- **Śamatha** (Ch 19).
- ***Yujyate*** ("fits") for MMK 24.14 (Appendix A).
- **Spelling conventions:** Mūlamadhyamakakārikā; Candrakīrti; Dharmakīrti; Dignāga; Nāgārjuna; Prāsaṅgika; Prajñākaragupta; Madhyamakāvatāra; svabhāva (lower case, italic); Bahiya sutta (lower-case "sutta", no diacritic on Bahiya); Udāna; Ṭhānissaro; Dōgen; Genjōkōan; Shōbōgenzō; *Gabyō*; Xiangyan (pinyin); *lhun grub*, *rang grol*, *rang rig*, *ka dag*, *gzhi*, *ngo shes* (Wylie, lower case, italic); rigpa (anglicised, roman) alongside *rig pa*; Nyingma.

## 4. Rejected views

- **A witness or observer standing behind experience.** It is looked for and not found. Its sense "is itself something that appears, a thought or an impression that comes and goes" (Ch 1). In Ch 21: "If the witness is a separate subject standing behind experience, it becomes one more *svabhāva*."
- **Luminosity as a substance behind appearances.** "It sounds as if there were a bright substance behind the display… Looked at carefully, the clarity is not behind the display" (Ch 2).
- **Any carrier: soul, stream of consciousness, neural core, store of potentials, conserved energy.** Each candidate "is itself something that appears… So it is designated like everything else, and it would need a carrier of its own" (Ch 2; Ch 9).
- **The Given / svalakṣaṇa as an undesignated floor.** Candrakīrti's three moves (characteristic and characterised are co-designated; perception is of the pot; the means and objects of knowledge are co-defined) and Tsongkhapa's "not even conventionally" (Ch 5).
- **Conceptual idealism ("conceptual all the way down") and a non-conceptual floor (Dreyfus's coping).** "Each makes one term prior." Idealism, panpsychism and Mind-Only are rejected via *Substrateless World* (Ch 6).
- **Energy as substance.** It is frame-dependent, its conservation follows from a symmetry, and it has no defined total in an expanding universe. "A true substance could not simply fail to add up" (Ch 7).
- **Potentiality as a reservoir, and unlimited plasticity.** Unlimited plasticity "would itself be a kind of own-nature… That is the idealist extreme" (Ch 8). Making emptiness the home of possibilities "turns emptiness into a new substance" (Ch 8).
- **Nihilism / disconnection.** "The logic of dependency rejects both pictures, the carrier and the disconnection" (Ch 9). In Ch 14, nihilism "concludes from the absence of intrinsic nature that nothing exists".
- **Eternalism.** "Eternalism treats things as having a fixed, self-standing nature that persists regardless of conditions" (Ch 14).
- **Process, flow or energy as a replacement subject.** "The logic of dependency does not replace the subject with a better subject, such as a process, a flow or an energy" (Ch 11).
- **Transcending logic.** "A view that claims to transcend logic can say anything, and so says nothing" (Ch 4).
- **A second, deeper truth.** "There is no second, deeper truth for convention to fall short of" (Ch 13).
- **Convention as demotion.** "Convention is not the opposite of truth. It is where truth, in every usable sense, lives" (Ch 13).
- **An intrinsic producer cause (MMK 1.1), and its opposite, uncaused arising.** The dilemma "either a producer, or no reason at all" shares a false assumption (Ch 17).
- **An uncaused ground or source (lhun grub as causelessness).** "an uncaused source is exactly the *svabhāva* that Madhyamaka rejects" (Ch 18). The second departure in the Note on Method holds that freedom "is not an uncaused base".
- **Emptiness as a substrate.** "emptiness must not become a hidden substrate of emptiness either, because emptiness is itself empty" (Ch 18).
- **Hierarchy between conditions and appearance.** "Neither is above the other, because neither has a place from which it could rule" (Ch 18).
- **Conditions as agents with a viewpoint, and "own" as possession** (Ch 18).
- **Suppression / forced stillness.** "Stillness forced by effort can become a new object of holding" (Ch 19).
- **Non-duality as a new possession.** "'No gap, no ground' can become a new ground" (Ch 20).
- **The Bahiya teaching as atomism or blankness** (Ch 21).
- **An unpainted reality, and idealism ("only pictures").** "There is no blank canvas under the painting, and no painter standing apart from it" (Ch 22). Awakening in "some unpainted, non-conceptual ground" is rejected as "the two-storey picture" (Ch 22).
- **Recognition as achievement, effortlessness as accomplishment, and emptiness as a view** (MMK 13.8, Ch 23).
- **Implicative negation, a "true nature" beneath the seer** (Ch 3, Ch 23).

## 5. Teaching devices

- **Chariot in a courtyard** (Ch 1; referred to in Ch 14, 17, 18, 22): no chariot-itself over and above the parts, yet the word works. It is told in full only in Ch 1.
- **Tree and rain** (Ch 1–2): look for the seer or hearer apart from the seeing or hearing.
- **Red patch and simultaneous contrast (grey)** (Ch 2, 15): vividness and dependence are visible together. In Ch 15 the same red is seen as svabhāva or as dependent origination.
- **Dull grey wall against a sunset** (Ch 2): vividness is not a mark of greater reality.
- **Unkind remark next morning** (Ch 3): argument does not reach where the habit works.
- **Candle / flame** (Ch 4, 11, 17, 10): change without a changer. In Ch 17 it is the book's own illustration of the two claims. In Ch 10, "What changes is the weight with which it is watched."
- **Space as a room, time as a river or money, subject–predicate grammar** (Ch 4): where substantial thinking hides.
- **King of France** (Ch 4): the tetralemma as presupposition failure.
- **Snake in the rope, corrected by a lamp** (Ch 5, 6, 13): valid convention and Tsongkhapa's criteria.
- **Key, lock, hand and door** (Ch 5): efficacy is relational.
- **Ice sting; shadow that cannot be a bridge** (Ch 6, 8): appearance constrains designation.
- **Bicycle riding** (Ch 6): a skill is co-designated, neither rule nor reflex.
- **Match, ball and runner** (Ch 7): energy is a designated, frame-relative quantity.
- **The dress, duck-rabbit, electron** (Ch 8, 16): plasticity, and seeing-as.
- **Salt and water, seed and sprout** (Ch 8): potentials are relational. The salt example is the book's own, not MMK.
- **Nāgasena's lamp and a melody** (Ch 9): continuity without a carrier.
- **Anaesthesia** (Ch 4, 10): no river of time. Continuity is designated on waking.
- **Firewood and ash** (Ch 10): each abides in its own position, with no lost sequence.
- **Cracked phone, ship of Theseus, child in a photograph, forged passport** (Ch 13): identity by criteria.
- **Cup divided into handle, rim and body** (Ch 14): the dividing mind.
- **Reading a word, an unfamiliar script, a musician's cadence** (Ch 16): no undesignated floor.
- **Lamp switching** (Ch 12): light came from nowhere and went nowhere.
- **Blade that cannot cut itself, and lamp** (Ch 19): the svasaṃvedana debate.
- **Painted rice cake, painted hunger, the meal exercise; stone and ant** (Ch 22): designation is all the way through, and it still feeds.
- **Xiangyan's pebble and bamboo** (Ch 22): burning notes as a cure for misuse of concepts, not for concepts as such.
- **Anger reconfiguring** (Ch 23): conditions are what work.

## 6. Practice and realisation

- **Looking over believing.** "It asks the reader to look… The looking is more useful than the reading" (Preface; How to Read).
- **First step: see the pattern as a pattern.** "The aim is not to replace it with a better picture, and it is not to suppress the feelings." "Nothing is done to it. The looking is enough" (Ch 3).
- **Insight is absorbed by the habit.** "'There is no self' becomes something to understand, to hold and to defend, and the one who understands it is the seer again" (Ch 3). Logic still has a role: it "help[s] keep it steady while the habit tries to take it back" (Ch 3).
- **Embodied check.** Contraction and self are offered "for inspection… it should be tested before it is believed" (Ch 3).
- **Analysis and non-dividing seeing are not stages.** "It would be tempting to treat the two as stages, first analysis and then recognition. That reading is mistaken" (Ch 16).
- **The taste is not a stage or an attainment.** "It is recognised, and the moment one tries to keep it as an accomplishment, the fixing has returned" (Ch 21).
- **Stopping, not achieving.** "its absence is a stopping rather than an accomplishment" (Ch 20).
- **Self-release over suppression.** "Suppression pushes thoughts down… Self-release lets thoughts dissolve when they are not followed or fixed." Candrakīrti accepts śamatha conventionally, but "the decisive step is analysis" (Ch 19).
- **Recognition.**
  - It is decisive, and it is not produced: "recognition is the seeing that the undoing makes possible" (Ch 23).
  - "Thought is recognised as already empty, already not fixed, and in that recognition it stops fixing."
  - Recognition "does not negate. It sees that appearances are already not fixed."
- **Effortlessness.** "The moment one treats effortlessness as an accomplishment, one has made it another fixed object. The word 'effortless' needs watching" (Ch 23).
- **Undoing is still practice and needs clarity.** "What needs doing is undoing the habit that fixes them, and that undoing is itself a practice." Closing line: "that clear knowing is the practice that remains" (Ch 23). See also Ch 21: "Recognising the difference does not happen by itself. It takes clear knowing."
- **Gradual against sudden.** "Gelug describes a graduated path… Dzogchen describes a recognition that is not an accomplishment and happens at once. That is a difference in method, not in whether freedom is made" (Ch 23).
- **Three recognitions** (Ch 23): conditions are what work; appearing is open because it is dependent; there is nothing to hold and no one apart from the holding.
- **The trap.** Openness, non-holding and emptiness can become "a state to reach, an attitude to maintain, a view to defend" (Ch 23).
- **Death.** The view "asks nothing of the dying about what comes after", and does not "make the loss of someone we love painless" (Ch 10).
- **No I AM, non-dual, anatta or emptiness stage scheme appears in this book.** The arc is observer not found (anatta-like), then vividness without a viewer, then emptiness of substrates, then the two modes, then recognition. Ch 3 names "Anatta insight" as "a real breakthrough" that the habit can absorb.

## 8. Tensions

### 8a. With the nine-point canon

1. **"Basis" re-read as emptiness (canon 1, 6).** Note on Method, first departure: the book "reads *gzhi* as the emptiness of appearing, not as a ground behind it. Where the traditional language of basis is used, the book means this reinterpreted sense." The Basis glossary entry says the same. Canon 1 says emptiness "is not called… ground in itself". Equating the basis with emptiness hands the ground-word to emptiness, even with the disclaimer.
2. **"Nature" for emptiness (canon 1), with a self-contradiction.**
   - Note on Method, third departure: "Its use of 'nature' for emptiness is also narrower than the standard usage."
   - Glossary, Dharmatā: the book "uses 'nature' for the way things are, when they are not reified… It avoids the term for emptiness itself. The difference is stated where the term is first used."
   - The two statements contradict each other. No first-use definition of "nature" appears in the body. The body uses "nature" only negatively (e.g. "no fixed nature", "the true nature" in scare quotes in Ch 3, "the nature of each appearance" in Ch 23 as the thing that must not become a ground).
3. **Openness as a property, in reported glosses (canon 2, 3).**
   - Glossary, Lhun grub: "spontaneous presence is the form in which the basic openness of awareness surges forth as luminosity" (Higgins). Attributed, but it sits inside the book's own definition entry without being marked as a departure. It treats openness as a property of awareness and lhun grub as an emergence, close to a ground that produces.
   - The Openness entry, by contrast, flags the departure.
4. **"Luminosity of emptiness" (canon 1).** Glossary, Luminosity: "Rangjung Yeshe gives *stong pa'i 'od gsal* as 'empty luminosity' and 'the luminosity of emptiness.'" It is reported without comment. The Emptiness entry says the book does not call emptiness luminous, so the Luminosity entry should flag the departure as the Openness entry does.
5. **Rigpa as "the pristine awareness that is the basis itself" (canon 1, 6).** This is the reported Dzogchen sense in the glossary. The book's own position ("knowing clarity… a gloss") is stated, but the entry does not mark this sense as one the book does not adopt. That leaves awareness-as-ground standing in a definition.
6. **"Its clarity by its own being clear" (Ch 2) (canon 1, 9).** "The reliability of the appearance is accounted for by its conditions, and its clarity by its own being clear. Nothing further is needed." "Its own being" edges toward own-nature language for luminosity. Compare Ch 19: "self-knowing… is that flow's luminosity", where "flow of awareness" is close to a stream-type carrier, though it is presented as the lamp reading.
7. **"Both are established by emptiness" (Ch 16) (canon 1, 3, 6).** "Both are established by emptiness, and emptiness is not a further thing." "Established by" casts emptiness as the establisher or ground of the two modes. The second half of the sentence corrects it, but the verb conflicts with "not a ground that produces".
8. **"The habit that obscures it" (Ch 23) (canon 5, 8).** "Freedom is not produced… What practice does is undo the habit that obscures it." Freedom as something already present and hidden can read as the "uncaused base" canon 5 rejects. Canon 4 frames undoing as release, not uncovering.
9. **"Recognition… does not negate" (Ch 23) (canon 9).** It reads oddly beside Ch 3 and the same chapter's insistence that the guide is non-implicative negation. The intended sense (recognition does not negate appearances) is clear from context, but a canon statement should say "does not negate appearances".
10. **"The presenting basis" (Ch 17) (canon 6).** "the presenting basis is itself one of them, with no web drawn over it". Using "basis" for the appearing risks ground language. It is undefined.
11. **Openness glossed as an absence (Ch 23) (canon 2; terminology).** "Openness, too, is empty… It is the absence of what one was holding." Canon 2 makes openness a mode of seeing, and canon 1 makes emptiness "the absence of any fixing". Defining openness as an absence blurs it with emptiness.
12. **Lhun grub and dependent arising: equal or not? (canon 6).** The Note on Method, third synthesis, says they "are not two equal descriptions. Any attempt to divide appearing belongs to dependent arising, and when the reifying habit stops, the same appearing is spontaneous presence." Ch 18: "Neither is a higher description, and neither grounds the other." "Not equal" against "neither higher" invites a hierarchy reading. The glossary's "What dependent arising looks like once no producer is sought" is cleaner.
13. **Non-dividing seeing "without names" against designation all the way (canon 7).** Ch 16 defines the second mode as registering "without the names, categories and part-whole structure that thought adds" (also the Avikalpa glossary entry). The same chapter, Ch 6 and Ch 22 hold that designation is never escaped ("never an undesignated floor"; "Realisation did not escape designation"). Ch 6's "designation is wider than language" is the implicit reconciliation, but it is not stated in Ch 16 or the glossary. Canon 7 (division conventionally stands) would want this explicit.
14. **"Rest in non-dividing seeing" (Appendix C, Round 9) (canon 4, 8).** Makes non-dividing seeing sound like a state entered by doing, against Ch 16's "not a stillness reached by effort".
15. **Second departure: "The correction is to the self-description of the tradition, not an interpretation of it"** (Note on Method). This openly corrects Dzogchen self-description ("freedom… is not an uncaused base"). It agrees with canon 5, but readers should know the book frames it as a correction.

## 9. Candidate canon points

1. **The observer is not found apart from the seeing.** Looking for the seer finds seeing and what is seen, never a viewer standing apart. Nothing is denied: seeing happens, persons act. What goes is the expectation of a witness behind experience.
2. **Without a viewer, experience is more vivid, not blank.** Vividness is the absence of mediation, not intensity added. It does not make an appearance more real: the dull and the vivid are equally empty.
3. **Luminosity is the knowing clarity of appearing, not a light behind it.** The knowing and the known are not two. This is a gloss on the luminosity tradition and on rigpa, not a translation. Clarity is never a carrier, store or substance.
4. **Ignorance is a habit of not seeing, not a lack of information.** It arranges seer, seeing and seen before reasoning begins, and it absorbs insight and analysis into new seers. Argument steadies the seeing but does not replace it. The first step is to see the pattern as a pattern.
5. **Madhyamaka logic is ordinary logic used against the opponent's premises.** It relies on non-contradiction and concludes in a negation that installs nothing. The tetralemma refusals reject a presupposed bearer, not logic.
6. **There is no floor: neither a Given nor concepts all the way down.** Appearance and designation are co-designated, like long and short, and neither is prior. Designation is wider than language. Valid cognition and the perception–conception distinction stand conventionally.
7. **Efficacy, energy, potential and continuity are relations, not substrates.** Each is a designated regularity of high reliability. None is a passenger carried through change. Plasticity is exactly as wide as conditions allow.
8. **Dependence and unfindability are one fact.** The negations describe dependent arising itself. A thing that could be found arising would not need to depend. The eight extremes are eight ways of asking where a thing is.
9. **Conventional truth is fully valid, and ultimate truth is not a higher world.** Convention has standards (Tsongkhapa's three criteria). Ultimate truth is the emptiness of the conventional, so there is one appearance seen two ways.
10. **Svabhāva lies in the seeing, not in the content.** The same red can be met as ground or as dependent. A ground is produced by reifying a useful abstraction.
11. **Analysis and non-dividing seeing are not stages.** Analysis answers the dividing habit and ends when nothing is left to examine. Non-dividing seeing is appearing when no fixing occurs, not a second method or a state reached by effort.
12. **"Cause" is conditional alignment or a designated relatum, never an intrinsic producer.** Analysis leaving no foundation, and appearances arising when conditions align, are one event described two ways. The dilemma "producer or no reason" shares one false assumption.
13. **The question "why does appearing occur?" is the last reification.** It has force only while a ground seems to stand behind appearing. Seen through, it is not answered but loses its object. "No why and because" does not deny conventional causal regularity.
14. **Spontaneous presence is dependent appearing when reification stops, not an uncaused source.** Lhun grub adds nothing on top of emptiness and grounds nothing. Self-appearing is ownerless: "self" means only "not borrowed from elsewhere".
15. **Recognition stays within non-implicative negation.** Recognition does not negate appearances. It sees them already unfixed. If "the nature of appearances" becomes a positive ground, reification has returned. Openness, too, is empty, and is not something one has.

## 11. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity

**What the book does not contain.** I searched the full text. It never uses the terms *rangtong*, *shentong*, *rang stong*, *gzhan stong*, "empty of other", *prabhāsvara* or "luminous clarity". It does not use "nature of mind" as a doctrinal term of its own. The phrase appears only in the title of Longchenpa's work and in the Luminosity glossary entry ("the clarity aspect of the nature of mind"). The book uses "Nyingma" once, "Dzogchen" often, and spells kadag and lhundrub in Wylie (*ka dag*, *lhun grub*). Its stance is implicitly rangtong: emptiness is "the lack of intrinsic nature" and is non-implicative (see §3, §9). On luminosity it presents itself as reading Nyingma Dzogchen through Madhyamaka.

### (a) Nature of mind and nature of phenomena

The book never sets up a mind/phenomena contrast explicitly. Its one direct statement makes emptiness the same for all phenomena and "not confined to mind":

- Ch 18, quoted exactly: "Emptiness is not an intrinsic nature that appearances possess. It is the absence of any fixing, and it is not confined to mind. There is not one phenomenon that has not arisen dependently, and so there is not one phenomenon that is not empty (MMK 24.19)."
- Ch 18 note: "MMK 24.19 is confirmed in the Tsadra glossary as 'Because there are no phenomena that are not dependently arisen, there are no phenomena that are not empty.'"
- Ch 14: "Emptiness is therefore not a separate reality behind phenomena. It is what dependent arising amounts to when its ontological status is examined. There is not one phenomenon that has not arisen dependently, and so there is not one phenomenon that is not empty."
- Ch 20: "The emptiness that applies to the dull applies equally to the vivid."
- Ch 2: "A dull grey wall is as vivid as a sunset in the sense that matters here."
- Ch 15: "Redness, sound and the feeling of presence can be exactly as vivid whether or not they are held as svabhāva."
- Mind is not privileged as the ground of dependence. Ch 2: "Does the red depend on the mind, and is the mind then the ground? That question leads back to the witness."
- Ch 6 rejects "idealism, panpsychism and Mind-Only". Ch 8: a plasticity independent of conditions is "the idealist extreme".
- Ch 19 states Candrakīrti's critique of "the self-awareness (*svasaṃvedana*) of cognition, the Yogācāra view of self-knowing mind".
- **Reading:** the nature of mind and the nature of phenomena are one emptiness, with no special status for mind. This fits a rangtong reading.

### (b) Luminosity, clarity, rigpa, awareness, presence, vividness: how each is framed

Each passage is quoted exactly.

- **Ch 2, framed as a mode of appearing, explicitly not a substance or ground:** "What, then, is the vividness? The Dzogchen tradition uses the word luminosity for the clarity of an appearance, and the word can be misread. It sounds as if there were a bright substance behind the display, shining through it the way a lamp shines through a window. Looked at carefully, the clarity is not behind the display. It is the knowing clarity of the display itself. A sound is heard as sound. A cold is felt as cold. The knowing and the known are not two things, and the clarity is what the appearance is when it is not being held at a distance. This identification of appearing with its knowing clarity is the book's reading of the luminosity tradition. Chapter 19 sets out the objection it faces."
- **Ch 2, framed as not a carrier and not an object:** "Luminosity is not that kind of object. It is the clarity by which an appearance is known as it appears. The reliability of the appearance is accounted for by its conditions, and its clarity by its own being clear. Nothing further is needed. A reader who tries to say what the clarity requires beyond these will find that each attempt turns the clarity into a thing, and each thing is again an appearance."
  - **Flag (mild):** "its clarity by its own being clear" can be heard as self-standing clarity, an own-nature of luminosity. It does not lean shentong, since there is no ground and no "empty of other", but the wording is loose.
- **Ch 2, radiance:** "It is simply the radiance of hearing, heard as it is. The warmth of the sun is the immediacy of warmth." This frames radiance as the appearance itself.
- **Ch 2, vivid and dependent:** "The red is dependent on its conditions, and it is vivid in itself." Read in context ("It needs the light, the eye, the surround and the seeing"), "in itself" means complete as an appearance. It is not intrinsic.
- **Ch 2, Bahiya:** "It names the vividness this chapter has described, with no one standing behind it to receive it."
- **Ch 5:** "Vividness and designation are mingled through and through."
- **Ch 6:** "The constraint that keeps designation from being arbitrary comes from the vividness itself."
- **Ch 10:** "The dread of death… presupposes that the vividness of experience was inherent." Also: "The luminosity of this appearance, its clarity, its colour and the sound in the next room, is available now, including to the one who is dying." Also: "what remains, the vividness of this moment, has never needed a bearer to be complete."
  - **Flag (mild):** "what remains" could suggest a residual ground. In context the passage frames it as bearerless.
- **Ch 15, presence explicitly not a foundation:** "This is why the taste of presence cannot be described as a foundation the analysis finally reaches. Appearance seen without a standing-behind is dependent through and through, and its immediacy is that of something relating, not of something standing on its own." Ch 15 also treats "presence" as an abstraction that can be reified: "The sameness of 'chariot'… or of 'presence' across many experiences, is drawn out."
- **Ch 16, avikalpa as non-conceptual awareness:** "The second is non-conceptual awareness, *avikalpa*." It is "appearing as it is when no fixing occurs". It is not a ground.
- **Ch 19, the lamp reading of self-awareness:** "Dharmakīrti takes a lamp as the model of self-awareness, and Prajñākaragupta argues that a cognition arises with the essence of shining, so that the blade comparison does not apply to it. On this reading self-knowing is not a second act performed on the flow of awareness. It is that flow's luminosity."
  - **Flag:** this is presented as one interpretation ("to be weighed"). Still, "the flow of awareness" and cognition arising "with the essence of shining" frame luminosity as a property of an awareness-stream. That is closer to awareness as a continuum than anything else in the book, and it is the nearest the book comes to luminosity as a property.
- **Ch 19, Rang rig risk:** "Dzogchen speaks of *rang rig*, self-cognizing awareness. The difficulty is that a nature that is not produced but is the ground of appearances sounds very close to the *svabhāva* that Madhyamaka refuses." The book names the shentong-type risk and does not endorse it.
- **Ch 20, vividness as absence of mediation, not a property:** "Vividness here is not intensity added to ordinary perception. It is the absence of the mediation that normally makes perception feel distant… The immediacy is not a quality that makes appearances more real. It is a quality of how they are not being held at a distance."
- **Ch 21, presence and vividness as the result of seeing through the witness:** "Once the designation stops, experience has a vividness that is hard to describe." Also: "If the witness is a separate subject standing behind experience, it becomes one more *svabhāva*. Seen without fixing, the seeing is simply vivid, and no witness is posited as doing the seeing." Also: contrived seeing makes "stillness, vividness and a sense of freedom become objects to hold."
- **Ch 1, awareness as a name for the reified witness:** "This centre has many names: the self, the I, the awareness, the one who knows… it is pictured as a stable witness standing quietly behind experience." Here "awareness" is listed among the witness-names that the book rejects.
- **Glossary, Clarity (exact):** "**Clarity** (*gsal ba*). In Dzogchen, the clear nature (*rang bzhin*) of the basis, one of its three aspects together with its empty essence (*ngo bo*) and its unobstructed compassion (*thugs rje*). In this book, the knowing clarity of appearing itself, not a substance behind it. See Luminosity. *Source for the three aspects: Rangjung Yeshe Wiki, 'primordial ground' and 'ngo bo rang bzhin thugs rje gsum', where Longchenpa's three factors appear as an empty essence, a clear nature and an unobstructed radiance of compassion. The book's own gloss is stated as a gloss.*"
  - **Note:** the Dzogchen sense reported here makes clarity a property ("clear nature") of the basis. The book's sense makes clarity a mode of appearing. The entry separates the two correctly.
- **Glossary, Luminosity (exact):** "**Luminosity** (*od gsal*). In Dzogchen, a term that is categorised under the clarity aspect of the nature of mind, and related to *lhun grub*. In this book, the knowing clarity of appearance, not a substance behind it. Rangjung Yeshe gives *stong pa'i 'od gsal* as 'empty luminosity' and 'the luminosity of emptiness.' See Clarity."
  - **Flag:** "the luminosity of emptiness" is reported without the departure being flagged. That conflicts with canon 1 and the book's own Emptiness entry ("does not call it… luminous in itself"). It is not shentong as such, because no "empty of other" ground is stated, but the reported gloss sits unqualified.
- **Glossary, Lhun grub (exact):** "Spontaneous presence. What dependent arising looks like once no producer is sought. Not an uncaused ground. In Higgins's account (via KMaps), spontaneous presence is the form in which the basic openness of awareness surges forth as luminosity."
  - **Flag (strongest lean):** "basic openness of awareness surges forth as luminosity" gives a basic awareness from which luminosity emanates. That is a source-and-emanation picture. It is attributed to Higgins, but it is unmarked as a departure inside the book's own definition. This is the closest the book comes to awareness as ground.
- **Glossary, Rigpa (exact, first sentence):** "In Dzogchen, the knowledge of the basis, and the pristine awareness that is the basis itself."
  - **Flag:** "the pristine awareness that is the basis itself" states awareness as the basis. It is reported Dzogchen usage, and the next sentences give the book's gloss ("The book's 'knowing clarity' is a gloss on this, not a translation"). The entry does not say the book declines the awareness-as-basis sense, though the Note on Method's first departure implies it.
- **Note on Method, third departure:** "In Dzogchen sources, openness (*stong*, as in *rig stong* and *gsal stong*) is one of the characterisations of the ground, paired with clarity and luminosity, and Longchenpa's ground-awareness is described as an openness that is at once empty and radiant. The book instead treats openness as the way unfixed appearing is seen, not as a property of emptiness or of the ground." This is flagged as a departure.
- **Glossary, Avikalpa:** "Non-conceptual awareness: registering what presents without the names, categories and part-whole structure that thought adds."
- **Glossary, Avidyā / ma rigpa (Ch 3):** "In Dzogchen the term is *ma rigpa*, non-recognition: the nature of appearing is there, and it goes unrecognised."
  - **Flag (mild):** "the nature of appearing is there… unrecognised" can read as a pre-existing nature awaiting discovery. Compare Ch 23: "undo the habit that obscures it" (see §8a item 8). Both lean toward a hidden-presence model, though neither calls it a ground.

### (c) Rangtong/shentong, Nyingma, Dzogchen, Mipham, Longchenpa, ka dag / lhun grub

- **Rangtong / shentong:** not named anywhere. No sentence asserts "empty of other". The nearest structural markers are below.
  - Rangtong-type: "Emptiness has no intrinsic nature of its own" (glossary, Emptiness); "emptiness must not become a hidden substrate of emptiness either, because emptiness is itself empty" (Ch 18); non-implicative negation (Ch 4, Ch 23).
  - Potential shentong lean: only the reported glosses flagged above (Lhun grub "basic openness of awareness surges forth"; Rigpa "pristine awareness that is the basis itself"; Luminosity "the luminosity of emptiness").
- **Nyingma:**
  - Note on Method: "The synthesis is that Prāsaṅgika Madhyamaka, as Candrakīrti and Tsongkhapa read it, and Nyingma Dzogchen share the view of appearing set out here. Gelug is a school within Prāsaṅgika, not a third party to the claim. No single tradition has stated this exact formulation, and the claim that the two share a view is the author's synthesis."
  - "rNying ma tantric literature" appears in the Primordial purity entry.
  - **Note against the coordinator's stance:** the book's Prāsaṅgika is explicitly "as Candrakīrti and Tsongkhapa read it". It names Tsongkhapa, not Mipham, as the Madhyamaka anchor, and it treats the Gorampa–Mipham contrast as undecided ("This book does not decide between Gorampa's and Mipham's readings").
- **Dzogchen as read through Madhyamaka:**
  - Note on Method: "The book's position is best described as a Madhyamaka reading of Dzogchen, not as a plain account of Dzogchen's own self-understanding."
  - Note on Method: "Some Dzogchen lineages hold that the view is its own resultant vehicle, not reducible to Madhyamaka analysis" (repeated in Ch 19).
  - Note on Method: "Some Dzogchen authors, Longchenpa among them, state that the ground is empty and not a subtle existent. The book's reading draws on that statement."
- **Longchenpa:**
  - Note on Method: "Longchenpa describes the ground as a ground only from a relative perspective, empty of being a ground in the true nature of reality. This book follows that line. It rejects any foundation that appearances are produced from or rest on, and reads *gzhi* as the emptiness of appearing, not as a ground behind it."
  - Ch 19: "Longchenpa's own account of the ground is careful. He does not treat the ground as a foundation: it is called a ground only from a relative perspective, and in the true nature of reality it is empty of being one."
  - Ch 19: Higgins "reads Longchenpa as rejecting self-reflective awareness".
  - Ch 20: *The Natural Freedom of the Nature of Mind* "takes self-release (*rang grol*) as its theme."
  - Clarity entry: "Longchenpa's three factors appear as an empty essence, a clear nature and an unobstructed radiance of compassion."
- **Mipham:**
  - Ch 19: "Mipham's *Beacon of Certainty*, in John Whitney Pettit's translation and study, defends Dzogchen in the terms of Madhyamaka logic, and it argues with Gelug positions, including Tsongkhapa's eight points of difficulty. Its treatment of the relation between Dzogchen view and Madhyamaka is one of the main sources for the synthesis this book offers."
  - Note on Method: "Gorampa (1429–1489) read the Madhyamaka critique of intrinsic nature more absolutely than Mipham (1846–1912) did… Mipham wrote four centuries later and drew on Gorampa's work."
  - Rang rig entry: Mipham "defends Dzogchen in Madhyamaka terms".
  - **Deleted (must not return):** Mipham's lamp reply, and "rigpa's luminosity is not a svabhāva because it is not an independent object".
- ***Ka dag*:**
  - Note on Method: "in some systems it speaks of an unproduced primordial purity (*ka dag*)… Dzogchen calls the ground primordially pure and unproduced, which can sound like an uncaused source. This book holds that freedom is not produced and is not a result of causes, but also that it is not an uncaused base. The correction is to the self-description of the tradition."
  - Glossary: "read as the unfixed character of appearing, not as an uncaused source."
- ***Lhun grub*:**
  - Ch 18: "The Dzogchen term for this appearing is *lhun grub*, spontaneous presence. The word can slide into causelessness, and an uncaused source is exactly the *svabhāva* that Madhyamaka rejects."
  - Ch 18: "Spontaneous presence, then, does not name an additional ingredient sitting on top of emptiness… Neither is a higher description, and neither grounds the other."
  - Ch 23: "*Lhun grub* means already complete. It is not produced by effort."
  - For the glossary entry, see (b) above.
- ***Ma 'gags pa*:** Ch 8 says: "The Dzogchen word *ma 'gags pa*, unobstructed, fits better than 'potential'. Nothing blocks the display, and there is no store behind it from which the display comes."

### Summary of shentong-lean flags, strongest first

1. Glossary Lhun grub: "the basic openness of awareness surges forth as luminosity" (Higgins, unmarked).
2. Glossary Rigpa: "the pristine awareness that is the basis itself" (reported, not explicitly declined).
3. Glossary Luminosity: "the luminosity of emptiness" (reported, departure not flagged).
4. Ch 19: "a cognition arises with the essence of shining… It is that flow's luminosity" (presented as one reading, framing luminosity as the property of an awareness-flow).
5. Ch 3 "the nature of appearing is there, and it goes unrecognised" and Ch 23 "the habit that obscures it" (hidden-presence model).
6. Ch 2 "its clarity by its own being clear" (loose own-nature wording).

None of these asserts a truly existing ground or "empty of other". The body chapters consistently frame luminosity as the knowing clarity of dependent appearing: empty, not a substance, ground, witness or carrier.
