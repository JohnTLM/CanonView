**Structure**

PART 1: THE TRANSITION FROM ANATTA TO EMPTINESS
- **Preface.** The seven phases were posted about 25 years ago and the journey spans about 40 years. AI helped with the writing. "Why Deconstruct the View?": deconstruction frees energy. Two failure modes are named: counterfeit deconstruction (relentless negation) and spiritual bypass.
- **A Note on Method.** Lays out the four parts and the Appendix. Calls the book "a personal philosophical synthesis". Flags five distinctions as the author's own: conceptual vs dependent designation; designation by relation; the logic of the single stroke; spontaneous presence = dependent arising; desynchronization.
- **The Starting Point: The Journey Through Presence.** Presence matures into non-dual luminosity and then becomes "the new observer". The turning question is "Does Presence itself inherently exist?" The sub-section "The Cumulative Peak of Anatta" covers Phase Five, "no seer, no seeing, and nothing seen", and the non-conceptual register.
- **Introduction: The Architecture of a Direct Realization.** Anatta, total exertion, Nāgasena, Candrakīrti and Nāgārjuna are presented as "one coherent non-substantialist logic", which the book calls the Logic of the Single Stroke.
- **The Collapse of the Observer.** Anatta completes Phase Five. It removes the subjective pole but not the objective framework. This is where post-anatta desynchronization begins.
- **The Taste of Total Exertion.** Seven tastes of zenki. Covers Dōgen's boat, firewood/ash and dharma-positions (hōji), seamlessness without fusion, verbs without nouns, and luminous clarity without background awareness.
- **The Great Desynchronization.** Experience has become non-substantialist while the framework stays substantialist. Covers the migration of substantialism, noun grammar, "Essencelessness Changes What Is Reasoned About, Not the Rules of Reasoning", the fluid substrate trap, spiritual bypass, and why the MMK seems to contradict anatta.
- **The Chariot Comes Alive.** Dependent designation and the birth of conventional reality. Covers Nāgasena, Candrakīrti's sevenfold search, exhaustive conditionality, functioning as the criterion of conventional validity, "No Residue: The Testimony of the Senses", the apple, the physical/felt "gap", and "Where Does Not Apply".
- **The Last Refuge of Ontology: Dependent Arising Dismantles Itself.** Substantialism migrates from things to process. Dependent arising turns on itself. Covers non-implicative negation, niṣprapañca, "An Unaccountable World", and "Beyond Cause and Effect: Why Spontaneous Presence Is Not a Hidden Ground".
- **The Logic of the Single Stroke & The Eye as a Living Chariot.** States the Central Theorem and walks the eye through five lenses.
- **The Disease of Relentless Negation & The Principle of Formation.** Four critical insights (with a table) and the Triad of Formation.
- **Ye Shes: When Analysis Becomes Transparent & Freedom from Elaboration.** Two registers, "Thought Is Not the Veil" (rtsal, rang grol), and prajñā completing itself as ye shes.
- **Resonances and Open Questions.** Explicitly flagged as provisional. Covers unfindability vs non-locality, emergence/epiphenomenalism, the eight extremes and nirvāṇa, spontaneous presence and actualization (with sufficiency left open), and a closing caution (Feynman).
- **Epilogue: The Synchronization of Experience and Understanding.**

PART 2: THREE FORMATIONS
- **The Formation of a Mountain.** The search for the mountain; "not a boundary drawn on a field"; different bodies, different mountains; agreement is not evidence; "still there" vs "independently real".
- **The Formation of a Flower: Three Levels Down.** Qualia, imputation and the conventional shared world. Also covers radiance as neither internal nor external, effortless binding, the inclusion of the mind side, simultaneity rather than sequence, and "Emptiness Is Always Emptiness of Phenomena".
- **Space Is Also a Formation.** Space and object arise together.
- **The Painted Rice Cake: Designation Is Also a Formation.** Dōgen's *Gabyō*, Xiangyan, painted hunger, and a meal exercise.
- **Beyond the Extremes: Why Life and Death Were Never the Right Words.** Death-fear needs a bearer, which is never found. Rejects both survival (eternalism) and annihilation.
- **The Taste, Not the Argument.** Corelessness tasted as openness. Mutual verification of view and taste, and embodiment down to the reflexes.

PART 3: SCIENCE AND THE DHARMA: FROM SUBSTANCE TO RELATION
- **Independent Paths: What Science Gave Up.** Mach, Einstein, Bohr, Rovelli, Vedral, Bell, Wheeler, QBism, Barad and enactivism. Every link is framed as "resonance, not proof".
- **At the Edges of Science.** "Ordinary Magic" (Stevenson and rebirth evidence), Faggin, "Function Without Substance", "Beyond Science".

PART 4: NOTHING TO FIND, NOTHING MISSING: A BEGINNER'S COMPANION. The two label stories, five sceptic rounds (the fourth and fifth deal with Dharmakīrti), "What This Doesn't Mean", "Functioning Was Never Evidence of Essence", two faults (relentless negation; substantialist non-duality), and "the path runs in layers".

## 2. Core theses

1. **Anatta is complete but is not yet emptiness.** Anatta removes the subjective pole and leaves the objective framework standing (Collapse of the Observer): "This marks the completion of Phase Five. It is a decisive liberation, but it is not yet emptiness." "The observer has disappeared, but the world has not."
2. **Desynchronization is the engine of the book.** The experience can arrive complete while the view stays substantialist. The book closes the mismatch; it does not deepen the experience (Starting Point; Note on Method): "The experience had become free; the view through which it was interpreted had not." "the analytical genius of Madhyamaka does not deepen the non-conceptual register, but liberates the conceptual register."
3. **Substantialism migrates rather than disappears.** It moves from the observer to space, time, conditions, matter, energy and awareness, and then to process, relation and dependent arising itself (Great Desynchronization; Last Refuge): "It is not committed to any particular ontology. It is committed to ontology itself."
4. **Presence and awareness must also be examined.** "The observer may disappear, while Presence quietly becomes the new observer… The centre has disappeared, yet the background remains." (Starting Point)
5. **Dependent designation, not conceptual designation.** A thing and its label arise together. There is no thing first and label second (Chariot; Companion). Central Theorem (Single Stroke): "dependent designation is not the conceptual labelling of a pre-existing world, but the simultaneous articulation of dependent arising itself."
6. **There is no pre-given world, not even a pre-given process.** "There is no pre-given world—not even a pre-given, ever-changing world." (Single Stroke). "Pre-given" means "already carved and existing from its own side". Conventionally, "the mountain is there before anyone names it".
7. **Relation and relata are designated together, and neither is prior.** (Great Desynchronization; Chariot; Appendix 1.7): "Relation and relata are designated together; neither is prior." The relating "too, is designated".
8. **Exhaustive conditionality.** Each condition is required in full. Remove one and you get a different thing or nothing, not a lesser version. This does not install conditions as a ground (Chariot): "Exhaustive conditionality shows only that no shared residue is found; it does not install the conditions as a new explanatory ground."
9. **No residue.** The sense modalities share no common substance (awareness, presence) (No Residue): "Here, even the substance the observer was once thought to observe turns out to have never been there." The seamlessness between qualia "is made of there being nothing underneath."
10. **Functioning is the criterion of conventional validity, not correspondence.** (Chariot): "Convention succeeds because it works, not because it mirrors an underlying ontology." "Ultimate analysis removes intrinsic existence, but it does not remove functioning."
11. **Functioning is never evidence of essence. Showing that something functions already shows that it is empty.** (Companion): "Valid functioning and independent existence aren't just different; they aren't even correlated." "showing that something functions already is showing that it's empty."
12. **Dependent arising dismantles itself.** It has no ontological privilege, and conventional functioning is undiminished (Last Refuge): "its explanatory success does not grant it ontological privilege." "Dependent arising never required rehabilitation; only ontology did."
13. **Negation is non-implicative.** Openness and the other qualities are not positive results inferred from emptiness (Last Refuge): "Such an interpretation would convert a non-implicative negation into an implicative one." "Negation neither produces openness nor creates a new reality."
14. **Freedom from elaboration (niṣprapañca) is the nature of dependent arising**, not a meditative suspension (Last Refuge): "Its emptiness and its functioning have always been inseparable. Neither grounds the other."
15. **The "why" and the "where" are retired, not answered.** (Apple; "Where Does Not Apply"): "The question is not left unanswered. It is retired, for lack of anywhere left to stand." "Appearance does not originate from somewhere, and it does not originate from nowhere either."
16. **The physical/felt gap is dissolved, not bridged.** No medium is needed, but the hard problem is not solved (Chariot): "It does not explain why there is something it is like to hear at all… What it dissolves is… the demand for a connector."
17. **Intrinsic causal power and substrates explain nothing. "Beyond cause and effect" means beyond intrinsic cause** (Relentless Negation, Four Insights; Beyond Cause and Effect): "'power' adds a name, not an explanation." "a substrate only moves the question down a level." "Beyond cause and effect does not mean beyond dependent arising."
18. **Spontaneous presence (lhun grub) is not a hidden ground.** It is dependent arising "from the inside" and the same nature under two descriptions (Beyond Cause and Effect; Spontaneous Presence and Actualization). If it were taken as a ground, "it would be the demand for a producer, granted one final, unquestionable exemption."
19. **Relentless negation is a disease. The cure is the question of formation**, meaning how an empty world appears vivid, stable and efficacious (Principle of Formation): "The mistake is to assume that nothing remains to be understood once nothing remains to be negated." "That the world forms at all, and so precisely, is what shows it to be empty."
20. **Prajñā completes itself as ye shes.** Ye shes is not a second attainment (Ye Shes): "Ye shes is not a mystical higher state or another knowledge added after prajñā. It is prajñā no longer burdened by the compulsion to establish reality ontologically." The last seam is "the analysing itself".
21. **Two registers, one reality. Thought is display, not a veil.** (Ye Shes): "One tastes, and the other understands. Neither produces the reality that both disclose." "Seeing thought as display is… the end of needing to."
22. **Logic is kept and its premises are changed.** (Great Desynchronization): "it is not logic that must change, but the premises logic has been applied to." The looser view "is an immunity from being wrong".
23. **Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are identical in nature and distinct in cognition.** This is the most important guard against bypass (Eight Extremes): "The nature was never different. The recognition was."
24. **Emptiness is always the emptiness of phenomena.** (Flower): "No phenomenon, no emptiness… Emptiness is a way of correctly seeing phenomena, not a separate phenomenon-free reality."
25. **Death: no bearer, so neither annihilation nor survival.** Conventional birth and death stand (Beyond the Extremes): "there was never a bearer, on either side of the word 'death', for anything to happen to — not destruction, and not survival."

## 3. Key terms (spelling as in the book)

- **anatta**: Pali, lowercase, no diacritics. "the complete collapse of the independently existing observer". Corresponds to Phase Five. Formula: "no seer, no seeing, and nothing seen".
- **Phase Five / Phase Six**: capitalised, from the author's seven phases of enlightenment. Five is anatta; Six is emptiness/dependent arising. The book is about "the transition between Phase Five and Phase Six".
- **Presence / presence**: capitalised when it functions as a reified ground ("Does Presence itself inherently exist?"). The book also capitalises "Awareness, Being, or Presence" as candidate ultimate substances.
- **non-conceptual register / conceptual register**: "the cumulative experiential peak of Phase Five realization" versus the register that "understands why that very presence has never required an ontological foundation".
- **total exertion (zenki)**: zenki is glossed "literally 'total dynamic working'". It is "each event completely exhausted itself in being exactly what it was". The book presents it as a taste that arrives post-anatta, before the view.
- **dharma-position (hōji)**: from the Genjōkōan. "the positions themselves are designated, not found"; "a dharma-position, conventional and empty, not a locus in which some reality establishes itself".
- **(Great) Desynchronization / post-anatta cognitive desynchronization**: the author's own framing. The mismatch of view, experience and insight.
- **substantialism / substantialist**: the habit of positing independently existing entities. "inherent view" and "'inherent' thought" (Preface) name the same thing felt as a "spell" or "matrix".
- **inherent existence / intrinsic existence / existing from its own side / independently existing**: used interchangeably. **svabhāva** appears once (Science, Vedral).
- **conceptual designation**: "thing first, label second", the "sticker picture". This is a pedagogical device; classical Madhyamaka holds that all designation is dependent.
- **dependent designation**: "thing and label arising together"; "the mode by which empty conventional phenomena are conventionally established and articulated", "not fundamentally a theory of naming".
- **designation by relation / designation-by-thought**: the author's distinction. "structural, non-conceptual individuation operates even prior to explicit conceptual labelling". Also the name of the Appendix notation, **Designation by Relation**.
- **dependent functioning**: "the conventional coordination through which phenomena mutually establish one another"; "itself conventionally designated, not another hidden ontological layer".
- **dependent arising / dependent origination / pratītyasamutpāda**: used interchangeably.
- **exhaustive conditionality / exhaustive dependency**: every condition is required in full.
- **no residue**: no shared substance across modalities.
- **sevenfold search / sevenfold analysis / sevenfold reasoning**: Candrakīrti, *Madhyamakāvatāra* 6.151 ff. Not identical, not different, does not possess, not in them, they not in it, not mere collection, not shape.
- **Logic of the Single Stroke / single stroke**: capitalised as the name of the framework. "'single' because nothing is ontologically prior"; "the single stroke, too, is empty."
- **Central Theorem**: dependent designation is the simultaneous articulation of dependent arising.
- **pre-given world**: "already carved and existing from its own side".
- **fluid substrate trap / hidden substrate view**: replacing solid things with a flowing medium (awareness, energy, consciousness).
- **spiritual bypass**: "using a correct view of nature to excuse the actual work of recognition".
- **relentless negation**: "a non-implicative negation performed incompletely — mistaking exhaustion for completion".
- **epistemological bypass**: "the cessation of conceptual proliferation is mistaken for the completion of wisdom".
- **non-implicative / implicative negation**: non-implicative "negates and leaves nothing standing in its place"; implicative "negates X, but leaves the mind holding an implicit Y".
- **niṣprapañca / freedom from elaboration**: "the very nature of dependent arising itself".
- **prajñā**: "analytical wisdom". It "works by search and negation" and is "object-directed".
- **ye shes**: Tibetan, lowercase, two words. "immediate wisdom"; "prajñā no longer burdened by the compulsion to establish reality ontologically".
- **spontaneous presence (lhun grub)**: "names nothing more than appearing that owes its completeness to nothing hidden".
- **rtsal** (display, "expressive energy"); **rang grol** (self-liberation, "like a drawing on water"); **ma 'gags pa** (unobstructed; "ceaseless meaning unobstructed… not a series of instants").
- **formation / principle of formation**: "the intelligibility of how a world empty of intrinsic existence nevertheless appears as the vivid, stable, objective, causally efficacious, and conventionally valid reality".
- **Triad of Formation**: "1. Luminous Clarity… 2. Stable Causal Efficacy… 3. Dependent Designation", described as "none prior to the others".
- **luminous clarity / luminosity / radiance / vividness**: "the vividness of the event itself"; "luminous meaning nothing more than the plain, undeniable vividness of appearing itself" (Flower).
- **beyond cause and effect**: "beyond intrinsic cause and intrinsic effect".
- **the last spell**: the demand for a "why" behind appearing that has already aligned.
- **substantialist non-duality**: "an implicative negation wearing non-dual clothing". The non-substantialist kind sees "even the ground-nature, the luminosity, the awareness itself… as dependently designated".
- **eight extremes**: cessation, arising, annihilation, permanence, coming, going, unity, plurality (MMK dedication). **śivam** is glossed as "nirvanic language".
- **svalakṣaṇa**: Dharmakīrti's "momentary, causally efficacious particular".
- **Prāsaṅgika**: Tsongkhapa's mark, "nothing is established by its own characteristic, not even conventionally".
- **painted rice cake / painted hunger**: from Dōgen's *Gabyō*. Designation is also a formation.
- **ordinary magic**: what gets called ordinary "was never explained", only made familiar.
- **Rheomode**: Bohm's verb-first language, which "risks reifying process itself".
- **Appendix operators**: `~>` Relational Co-Emergence, `::` Specific Conditionality, `[@]` Manifest Identity, `{O}` Unmodified Entirety, `=!=` Invalidation Protocol, `[~]` Spontaneous Expression, `[*]` Non-Referential Luminosity, `<>` Horizontal Dependency, `<~>` Horizontal Co-Emergence, `[><]` Direct Mediumless Contact, `[~v~]` Conventional Validity. Together they are called "The Eleven Axioms of the Coreless Matrix".
- **Diacritics and capitals as used:** Nāgārjuna, Nāgasena, Candrakīrti, Dōgen, Mūlamadhyamakakārikā (MMK), Madhyamakāvatāra, Milindapañha, Shōbōgenzō, Genjōkōan, Zenki, Gabyō, saṃsāra, nirvāṇa, Dignāga, Dharmakīrti, Prāsaṅgika, Middle Way (capitalised), Dharma (capitalised), Madhyamaka.

## 4. Rejected views

- **Witness or observer as an entity.** "not because it has disappeared, merged with the universe, or become infinite, but because it was never present as an independently existing entity." The book also denies a "background awareness": "Luminosity belongs to the event itself."
- **Presence, awareness or consciousness as the new Self or ground.** The book applies the same investigation to them: "If inherent existence cannot be granted to the individual, on what basis should it be granted to consciousness itself?"
- **Fluid substrate.** This covers an ocean of awareness, universal consciousness, energetic flow and invisible continuum: "the substantialist framework… survive[s] in liquid form."
- **Energy as substrate.** Included in the fluid-substrate list ("awareness, energy, consciousness, or a universal process"). In the Preface, energy release is described only as an effect of deconstruction.
- **Holism and monism.** "This should never be interpreted as holism, nor… a universal substance expressing itself through many forms. Nothing has merged, and nothing has become 'one'." The book's positive claim here is "Seamlessness without fusion".
- **Process ontology and relational ontology** (process philosophy, systems thinking, dynamic holism, Rovelli's network, Rheomode): "Static ontology quietly becomes process ontology. Substance becomes relation." The answer is that the relation "too, is designated".
- **Reified dependent arising.** Exempting it "would become the very foundation that all previous analysis had sought to avoid."
- **Spontaneous presence as an uncaused ground.** "the last and subtlest place substantialism can hide."
- **Nihilism.** "What remains is not nihilism, but the complete freedom of conventional functioning." The book also rejects fictionalism ("Nāgasena is not replacing realism with fictionalism") and "the mountain is an illusion".
- **Eternalism.** Survival of "some deeper radiance, presence, or awareness" after death "is eternalism, arrived at by the most sophisticated possible route."
- **Physicalism and idealism.** "Both keep exactly one substrate standing and demote the other side." Redescribing red as "really" wavelength "answered a functional question… as though it were an ontological one".
- **Emergence and epiphenomenalism.** Both keep "the base-then-derivative sequence" and require production.
- **Intrinsic causal power** (a tautology) and **substrates** (field, matter, consciousness, which only relocate the question). **Information as the new substance** (on Vedral). **Consciousness as fundamental** (Faggin): "to make consciousness fundamental is to install one more ground."
- **Absolute space and time as containers.** "Here and now arrive with it." The book applies this to the question of where one goes at death.
- **A raw field with a boundary drawn on it** (the mountain), and a blank canvas under the painting (the rice cake).
- **Dharmakīrti's efficacy-as-the-mark-of-the-real.** "efficacy is itself a relational fact… 'Causally efficacious' and 'independently existing' aren't the same claim." Locke's substratum is also rejected.
- **Overturning logic.** The loose view "is an immunity from being wrong".
- **Thought as veil and non-conceptuality as ground.** This is "the two-storey picture… with meditation as the lift between floors".
- **"Comes from nowhere".** It still answers "where" with "a blank instead of a place".

## 5. Teaching devices

- **Nāgasena's chariot**: the seed of the whole book. It shows "no residue", exhaustive conditionality, no sequence and full function. "Walking is the chariot. Hearing is the chariot."
- **Candrakīrti's sevenfold search**: closes the escape routes of owner, container and arrangement.
- **Dōgen's boat** (Zenki fascicle): "the boat carries the rower"; there are no participants prior to the activity.
- **Firewood and ash** (Genjōkōan): dharma-positions with before and after cut off; applied to life and death.
- **Wind blowing, fire burning, river flowing**: shows noun grammar putting a bearer behind activity. Also used to show the risk of a verb-substrate.
- **Coldness of ice**: a quality is designated together with body, air and feeling, not owned by a substrate.
- **The apple**: qualities and the imputation "apple" arrive together; here and now arrive with it. It is vivid and unfindable, "one fact, seen twice".
- **Mirage and moon in water**: "what full, vivid appearing looks like when nothing is holding it up from beneath".
- **Rabbit from a hat with no hat**: an unaccountable world.
- **Canyon "a zillion miles wide"**: the physical/felt gap, which "only existed because I had first assumed both banks into being".
- **Bell struck**: what arises (MMK 1). Sound is not found in bell, air or ear. Also used in "ordinary magic".
- **Birth as also the beginning of death**: shows verbs as designations.
- **The eye as a living chariot**: examined through anatta, Dōgen, Nāgasena, Candrakīrti and Nāgārjuna. "The eye that sees is… physically different as well."
- **Seed and growth power**: intrinsic causal power is a tautology.
- **Medicine, money, teacher**: designation validated by function.
- **The mountain**: the hardest case. Covers human, bat and bee; agreement as matching instrument calibration; "still there" vs "independently real".
- **The red flower, three levels down**: qualia (red by contrast), flowerness unfound, and shared convention. The radiance is neither internal nor external.
- **Space as a formation**: object and location arise together.
- **Painted rice cake and painted hunger**: Xiangyan, Guishan and the pebble on bamboo. Ends with a meal exercise. "the lucid dreamer's knowledge, not an escape from the dream."
- **Long/short rope, parent/child**: the fastest way into dependent designation (Companion).
- **Coffee cup subtraction and Locke's "he knows not what"**: the substrate is a placeholder.
- **Sleep**: continuity without a supervising witness. The book offers it as "a useful phenomenological analogy… though not a proof".
- **"He is not tall"**: illustrates implicative negation.
- **Drawing on water**: self-liberation of thought.
- **Lunch thought**: a thought does its work and is not found.
- **Feynman on quantum mechanics**: comfort with a formulation of emptiness "is the tell".
- **Train in the dark, Newton's bucket, lightning on a train, Wheeler's curvature, the forest seen by botanist, bird, deer and engineer**: science resonances only.

## 6. Practice and realisation

- **Sequence:** maturation of presence (I AM, then non-dual, "single field of luminous immediacy") → turning question about Presence → anatta, Phase Five ("no seer, no seeing, and nothing seen") → intermittent total exertion → Great Desynchronization → chariot recognition ("they have been living as the chariot all along") → Nāgārjuna turns analysis on dependent arising → freedom from elaboration, prajñā → ye shes. The book insists these are "successive clarifications of one indivisible realization", not stages that each add something.
- **Insight vs experience:** the experience may be complete while the view lags. "View is what lets an opening become a place to live rather than a place once visited." Wisdom is "the inseparable union of direct realization and right view."
- **Anatta needs no philosophy:** "This realization requires no prior understanding of dependent arising… It is not the product of philosophical reasoning." Total exertion "is not first understood conceptually… one suddenly finds oneself living within it post anatta."
- **Deconstruction as practice:** "seeing through the construction… releases what was being spent to keep the construction standing." In the author's experience this was "more effective than long hours of formal sitting practice, and in a specific sense safer". The book warns that its counterfeit "consumes energy and multiplies thought".
- **Two failure modes:** endless conceptual gymnastics on one side, premature closure (bypass) on the other. "the corrective in both cases is the same: not a bigger experience, but a view precise enough to notice which of the two is happening."
- **Mutual verification:** "the view corrects experience… Conversely, the direct taste corrects the view." Realisation "ceases to be an expansive state entered periodically". It must reach the somatic reflex, so that the reflex "no longer secretly confirms a solid, enduring subject".
- **Effortlessness and recognition:** freedom "belongs to dependent arising itself". Nirvāṇa "is not a different place arrived at later. It is this, correctly seen, standing against this, still misread." "the path was never adding anything ontologically new."
- **Sufficiency left open:** the book holds the equivalence of nature "with full confidence" but leaves open whether analysis alone leads to recognition. Recognition may need "direct introduction by a qualified teacher".
- **Layers:** "a finer layer only becomes visible after a coarser one has already been seen through". Negation and the question of how things still function must alternate.
- **End point:** "seamlessness — rather than an altered state — marks the culmination of insight." "Even emotional contraction is seen as empty; even fear is recognized as radiant appearance." "Eventually, even that formulation falls away."

## 8. Tensions

**Against the nine-point canon**

- **Canon 1 and 2 (openness and luminosity as properties).**
  - Last Refuge, "The Final Reversal": "Precisely because appearances lack any inherent substrate, they are already primordially open, non-abiding, effortless, infinitely responsive, and inexhaustibly capable of manifestation". This is hedged straight away with "not as a positive remainder".
  - Last Refuge: "dependent arising… already primordially open, non-abiding, effortless, and free from elaboration". Also: ye shes "directly recognizes the primordially open… character of dependent arising". This treats openness as a character of appearing, where canon 2 treats it as a mode of seeing.
  - Last Refuge: "Reality has not become open. It is recognized as open". "Reality… is recognized as having always been light."
  - Ye Shes: "Freedom belongs to dependent arising itself… rather than to a particular mode of cognition". This places freedom or openness in dependent arising, not in the seeing. That conflicts with canon 2's "The difference lies in the seeing".
- **Canon 3 (openness as consequence or explanation).**
  - The Taste, Not the Argument: "Openness is not an attribute belonging to a hidden subject, but the direct consequence of a world devoid of fixed essence."
  - Same chapter: "Radiance is immediate precisely because it is completely empty."
  - Triad of Formation: "We see the flourishing dependent arising of ethics, medicine… precisely because dependent arising is empty."
  - Appendix Axiom 11: "the conventional world works well not despite being empty, but precisely because it lacks an obstructive, rigid core."
  - Chariot: "they function flawlessly precisely because they are not fixed into self-existing substances."
  - Companion: Nāgārjuna's dictum, "emptiness is what allows functioning".
  - All of these use "because" or "allows" in an explanatory way.
- **Canon 1 (no intrinsic nature).** Last Refuge: niṣprapañca "is recognized as the very nature of dependent arising itself". Note on Method: "the same nature under two descriptions". "Nature" is used positively and is never defined.
- **Luminosity.**
  - Total Exertion: "Luminosity belongs to the event itself"; "The clarity belongs to the manifesting itself".
  - Triad: "Luminous Clarity" is one of three aspects of formation.
  - Flower: "insubstantial and luminous". This one is hedged as "nothing more than the plain, undeniable vividness".
  - These are property-ascriptions to appearances, not to emptiness, so the conflict is partial.
- **Canon 6 (no foundation, no layer).** Several phrases risk a field or matrix:
  - Appendix: "The Eleven Axioms of the Coreless Matrix"; "a pure framework of dependency"; Synthesis 1: "it arises seamlessly as a relational matrix"; Axiom 2: "what is designated as the relational field". This last is hedged: "itself a designation, not a substrate".
  - Insight 3 itself names "a field" as a failed substrate.
  - Synthesis 2: "the ultimate state is entirely devoid of an inherent core… resting in Unmodified Entirety ({O})"; "forbids any independent essence from separating these two domains". The phrases "ultimate state" and "two domains" are in tension with Axiom 4's "not a positive perfection state".
- **Canon 9 (non-implicative negation).**
  - Appendix Axiom 5: "absolute structural negation… completely zeroed out". This is close to annihilatory phrasing.
  - Companion: "A truly non-implicative negation… [is] achieved by seeing, clearly and positively, how causal efficacy… operate". The book means positive clarity of knowing, not a positive ground. This is compatible with canon 4 but needs careful wording.
- **Canon 4 (analysis as a habit of division).** The book puts heavy weight on analysis and deconstruction, which it calls "more effective than long hours of formal sitting practice". It does, however, treat the analysing itself as the last seam ("the analysing itself… stops being exempted"), which matches canon 4. Canon 4's "habit of analysis… is a habit of division" is not stated in the book.
- **Canon 5 (freedom not produced).** The Preface describes freedom and energy opening as following from deconstruction ("Seeing through the construction… releases…"). Ye Shes: "Prajñā… naturally maturing into immediate wisdom". This is mild, process-like causal language.
- **Total exertion and holism.**
  - Total Exertion: "the whole universe appears to be completely occupied with this one event"; "exhaustive of the entire cosmos"; "the entire cosmos is spent within the immediate relational configuration". This is in tension with the same chapter's denial of holism.
  - Also: firewood "structurally severed from linear continuity" sits against the later "continuity itself is a designation".
- **Appendix Axiom 3:** "the individual is the fluid manifestation itself". This echoes the "fluid substrate" language the book rejects, and the identity claim reads as positive.

**Internal tensions**

- **"Why" retired vs "dependent arising must explain formation".** The Apple and Beyond Cause and Effect retire the why. The Principle of Formation says "Dependent arising must explain formation" and "the cure for relentless negation is… the question of formation". The book reconciles these only by defining formation as "intelligibility".
- **"Not consequences" vs "consequence".** Last Refuge says "These qualities are not consequences produced by emptiness". The Taste chapter calls openness "the direct consequence of a world devoid of fixed essence".
- **"Resting point" vs no rest.** The Closing Caution says no formulation is to be "rested upon". The Companion says "Only the non-substantialist version is the actual resting point".
- **The mountain.** "Conventionally, the mountain is there before anyone names it" (Single Stroke) sits against "'mountain' and its boundary arise together with the designating" and "There are as many mountains… as there are ways of sensing". The book reconciles them through its definition of "pre-given" and the "still there" vs "independently real" line.
- **The lhun grub table** (Relentless Negation, row 4): "spontaneous presence (lhun grub) operates effortlessly without an ontological anchor". This gives spontaneous presence an agent-like role, which the prose elsewhere refuses.

**Terminology inconsistencies**

- "dependent arising", "dependent origination", "dependent functioning", "relational functioning", "relational configuration", "relational participation" and "emptiness/dependent arising" all name overlapping ideas.
- "inherent", "intrinsic", "independent" and "from its own side" are interchangeable.
- "luminous clarity", "luminosity", "radiance", "vividness", "clarity" and "self-luminous" are used side by side.
- "conceptual designation" is used for the sticker picture. Yet in the Note on Method "designation-by-thought" is contrasted with "designation-by-relation", so "conceptual" carries two senses.
- **zenki** is glossed as "total exertion". Dōgen's usual term for total exertion is *gūjin*; *zenki* is "total dynamic working". The book's own gloss acknowledges the second.
- "Two Truths" is capitalised in Starting Point and lowercase ("the two truths") in the Single Stroke chapter.
- "Phase Five/Six" (book) vs "stages" (general).
- "nature" is used for the shared nature of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa and also for the "nature of mind". Elsewhere the book denies intrinsic nature.
- Spelling is mainly British with -ize ("realization", "centre", "sceptical"). There are American slips: "fulfillment" (Ye Shes), "labor" (Flower, Effortless Binding), "flavor" (No Residue).

## 9. Candidate canon points

1. **Anatta is complete but not yet emptiness.** Anatta removes the inherent observer, the subjective pole. It does not by itself remove the reification of space, time, conditions, causality or relation. What remains is a lag in view, not a lack in the experience.

2. **View and experience must be synchronised.** Realisation can run ahead of view, and view can run ahead of realisation. Madhyamaka does not deepen the non-conceptual taste. It frees the conceptual register from the same reification. Each corrects the other until the distinction between them is artificial.

3. **Designation is dependent, never thing-first.** A thing and its designation arise together, and neither is prior. There is no pre-given world, static or changing, that labels are later attached to. "Conceptual designation" (thing first, label second) is a teaching contrast, not a classical category.

4. **Relation and relata are designated together.** Dependence is not a network, field or process standing behind things. The relating is itself designated. Replacing substance with process or relation is substantialism migrating.

5. **Dependent arising has no ontological privilege.** The principle that removes intrinsic existence is not itself intrinsically established. Analysis must turn on dependent arising, while conventional functioning continues undiminished.

6. **Functioning is the criterion of convention and is never evidence of essence.** A designation is valid because it works, not because it corresponds to something existing from its own side. Reliability is not independence.

7. **There is no residue and no substrate.** No shared substance (awareness, presence, energy, information, matter) runs beneath the modalities or beneath appearances. A substrate only relocates the question. Intrinsic causal power is a tautology.

8. **The why and the where are retired, not answered.** "Why this, now" and "where does appearance come from" assume a gap and a location that are never found. "From nowhere" still answers the question of where. The questions lapse for lack of anywhere to stand.

9. **Spontaneous presence is not a ground.** Lhun grub names appearing that owes its completeness to nothing hidden. "Beyond cause and effect" means beyond intrinsic cause, not an exception to dependent arising.

10. **Relentless negation is an incomplete negation.** Negating harder does not finish a non-implicative negation. It is finished when it becomes clear how appearance functions without essence. The cure is the question of formation, not more negating.

11. **Prajñā completes itself as ye shes.** Ye shes is not a second attainment. It is prajñā once the analysing itself is no longer exempted from what it has seen. Whether analysis alone suffices for recognition is left open.

12. **Thought is display, not a veil.** Neither the conceptual nor the non-conceptual register is the ground of the other. Thought liberates itself when it is not read as standing on its own. Practice is not a campaign against thinking.

13. **Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa share one nature and differ in recognition.** Ordinary experience shares nirvāṇa's emptiness now. That does not make recognition unnecessary. Treating shared nature as accomplished liberation is spiritual bypass.

14. **Emptiness is always the emptiness of phenomena.** Emptiness is not a free-standing void or a phenomenon-free reality behind things. It is a way of correctly seeing phenomena, which remain exactly as vivid.

15. **Death has no bearer, so there is neither annihilation nor survival.** Conventional birth, ageing and death stand. What fails is an intrinsically existing self undergoing intrinsic annihilation. A surviving awareness or radiance would be eternalism.

## 11. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity

The coordinator asked for this section partway through. There is no section 10. I searched the full text (PDF up to p. 75, then the docx) for: nature of mind, nature of phenomena, 24.19, 'od gsal, prabhāsvara, rigpa, rangtong, shentong, gzhan stong, Nyingma, Mipham, kadag, lhundrub, "empty of other", Longchenpa, Dzogchen, luminous/luminosity, clarity, radiance, awareness, self-luminous.

**Terms that never occur:** MMK 24.19, 'od gsal, prabhāsvara, rigpa, rangtong, shentong, "empty of other", Nyingma, Mipham, kadag (as a Tibetan word). The book writes lhun grub, never "lhundrub". Primordial purity appears once, in English only.

### (a) Nature of mind vs nature of phenomena

The book never argues directly that the emptiness of mind and the emptiness of phenomena are the same emptiness, and it does not cite MMK 24.19. The relevant passages are these:

- Total Exertion, "When the Universe Becomes a Single Event": "While it is not yet the mature realization of the nature of mind/phenomena, it is already a complete departure from ordinary perception". This is the only place both are named together. The slash treats them as one.
- Ye Shes, "Thought Is Not the Veil": "Thoughts are not obstacles in front of the nature of mind; they are its display (rtsal), its own expressive energy."
- Resonances, "Spontaneous Presence and the Actualization of the View": "Dzogchen's direct introduction to the nature of mind can be understood as the experiential actualization of exactly what Madhyamaka's analysis was always pointing towards — reasoning and direct recognition converging on one nature, approached from two directions." Also: "the disagreement is about method and sufficiency, not about the nature being pointed to." Also: "Someone could, in principle, exhaust the analysis perfectly and still not have the nature of mind pointed out directly".
- Flower, "The Mind Side Must Be Included, Not Assumed". The same analysis applies to the perceiver: "There is no independently existing 'mind' or 'consciousness' sitting behind the eye… This is anatta, applied here specifically to the perceiving side". Also: "neither physicalist nor idealist… neither side needs to be kept standing."
- Ye Shes, "Two Registers": "There is only one empty conventional reality, free from elaboration from the very beginning." Also: "Freedom belongs to dependent arising itself."
- Science, Faggin section: "When the relational picture of physics is carried over to the nature of mind and matter, the rigid wall between the 'subject' and the 'object' crumbles." This is followed by: "to make consciousness fundamental is to install one more ground."

**Assessment.** In effect the book gives mind and phenomena one emptiness. The same search is run on the observer (anatta), on objects (chariot, flower) and on the perceiving apparatus, and all of it is "one empty conventional reality". It reaches this by the method of searching, not by citing scripture. It does not separate a "nature of mind" from phenomena as a distinct object of realisation, except in the phrase "direct introduction to the nature of mind".

### (b) Luminosity, clarity, awareness, presence and vividness: how each is framed

**Framed as reification (warned against):**
- Starting Point: "The observer may disappear, while Presence quietly becomes the new observer. The self may dissolve, while awareness itself becomes the new Self. Duality may collapse, while luminosity quietly assumes the role once occupied by substance. The centre has disappeared, yet the background remains."
- Starting Point: "The language of pure awareness, luminous presence, mirror-like clarity, primordial knowing, or boundless consciousness all point towards this transformation. Such descriptions are neither mistaken nor fabricated." The experience is affirmed; only the reified reading is denied.
- Starting Point: the investigation asks whether "awareness, presence, luminosity, space, time… or even dependent arising itself" could be established as inherently existing.
- Great Desynchronization: "matter, energy, and even awareness itself may quietly become a subtle metaphysical ground."
- Fluid Substrate Trap: "reality becomes an ocean of awareness, universal consciousness, energetic flow, luminous presence, or some invisible continuum from which appearances emerge."
- Spiritual Bypass: "Centreless presence, boundless openness, luminous clarity, and total exertion are genuine contemplative taste. Yet… awareness becomes an ultimate ground, presence becomes a universal substance, or luminous clarity becomes an independently existing field from which appearances emerge."
- No Residue: "call it awareness, or consciousness, or presence, as I once quietly assumed… I found nothing."
- Taste, Not the Argument: "Intimacy does not depend on an ultimate substance such as Awareness, Being, or Presence." Also: "A powerful taste of openness or radiance can easily be misidentified… as an ultimate Awareness, an underlying Presence, or an enduring substrate."
- Beyond the Extremes: survival of "some deeper radiance, presence, or awareness… is eternalism".
- Companion, "substantialist non-duality": "The substantialist version treats the openness or awareness disclosed in that experience as itself a subtle, findable ground… the real thing… is a non-duality in which even the ground-nature, the luminosity, the awareness itself, is seen as dependently designated rather than independently findable, while remaining vividly present." Also: "a subtle Y — the ground, the luminosity, taken as ultimately real — gets implicitly reinstated".

**Framed as the vividness or mode of appearing itself, not a witness or ground (the book's settled position):**
- Total Exertion, list of tastes: "Luminous immediacy. Experience becomes vividly self-present and unmistakably clear, yet no observer, background awareness, or hidden ground can be found illuminating it. Luminosity belongs to the event itself."
- Total Exertion, "Luminous Clarity Without Background Awareness": "This luminosity is not a light behind appearances, nor an awareness standing apart to illuminate them. It is the vividness of the event itself." Also: "Every appearance is lucid in its very appearing, yet nothing behind the appearance can be found as its owner, witness, source, or ground… The clarity belongs to the manifesting itself."
- Triad of Formation: "Luminous Clarity… is simply the self-presenting character of appearance itself, requiring no hidden ground."
- Ye Shes: "'display' must not be heard as the display of something standing behind it—a luminous ground that projects thoughts as a lamp projects light. Longchenpa is explicit that the ground is empty. Display here means only this: dependent arising, vivid and unobstructed (ma 'gags pa), with nothing behind it."
- Ye Shes, prajñā to ye shes: "The same clarity, no longer needing an object apart from itself to be clear about". Here clarity is the clarity of knowing, not of a ground.
- Flower: "insubstantial and luminous — luminous meaning nothing more than the plain, undeniable vividness of appearing itself, not a hidden metaphysical light or a subtle consciousness illuminating the flower from behind, which would simply be the substrate view returning in its most disarming disguise".
- Flower, "Taste of Radiance": "That radiance was never added afterwards… It is not on top of anything. It is the whole of what is there." Also: radiance is "not external, not internal".
- Appendix, Axiom 7, Non-Referential Luminosity: "the clarity of a happening, vividness and designation mingled rather than a pre-given content later named… luminous and fully functioning without any background infrastructure or stage." The table version reads: "a self-luminous, translucent wireframe event".

**Framed in property or explanatory language (canon tension, see §8):**
- "Luminosity belongs to the event itself" and "The clarity belongs to the manifesting itself" ascribe luminosity to appearances as a belonging. They do not ascribe it to emptiness.
- Taste, Not the Argument: "Radiance is immediate precisely because it is completely empty." Emptiness is used here as an explanation.
- Last Refuge: dependent arising "already primordially open, non-abiding, effortless, and free from elaboration". Ye shes "directly recognizes the primordially open… character of dependent arising".
- Preface: "a full opening of radiant presence". This is experiential report.

### (c) Dzogchen, Longchenpa, primordial purity, lhun grub

- Note on Method: "This book draws extensively on… the Dzogchen tradition… a personal philosophical synthesis, developed in continuity with Madhyamaka and Dzogchen".
- Note on Method: "Spontaneous presence equals dependent arising… Dzogchen's spontaneous presence (lhun grub) and Madhyamaka's dependent arising are the same nature under two descriptions… This reading has real support within the tradition — Longchenpa's insistence that the ground is empty, not a subtle existent thing, points in the same direction — but it is not a settled cross-tradition consensus. Other lineages and commentators hold that Dzogchen constitutes its own resultant vehicle, not reducible to or guaranteed by Madhyamaka analysis alone."
- Resonances, Spontaneous Presence: "lhun grub and pratītyasamutpāda belong to different systems… the claim is that they refer to the same nature, not that they are the same teaching… Longchenpa is explicit that the ground is empty, not a subtle existent thing standing behind appearances, and later commentators have argued at length that appearance-emptiness inseparability and Dzogchen's primordial purity and spontaneous presence are identical in what they refer to". This is the only mention of primordial purity (kadag), in English. The "later commentators" are not named; this is probably Mipham.
- Beyond Cause and Effect: spontaneous presence is "the last and subtlest place substantialism can hide… It calls itself spontaneous presence." Also: "It names, descriptively, how appearing was never otherwise".
- Relentless Negation, Insight 4: "spontaneous presence (lhun grub) names nothing more than appearing that owes its completeness to nothing hidden." The table version reads: "spontaneous presence (lhun grub) operates effortlessly without an ontological anchor."
- Ye Shes: "The Dzogchen tradition refuses that picture. Thoughts are not obstacles… they are its display (rtsal)… (rang grol)".
- Appendix, Synthesis 1: "SP ~> ( [*] <> [~] )". It is restated as "spontaneous presence and dependent arising are the same nature under two descriptions, with no producer required".

**Assessment against the stated stance.** This matches a rangtong reading of the MMK combined with Nyingma Dzogchen on luminous clarity. The ground is "empty, not a subtle existent thing". Display has "nothing behind it". Luminosity is "the vividness of appearing itself". Primordial purity and spontaneous presence are treated through appearance-emptiness inseparability. Ye shes is prajñā completed, not a separate gnosis. The book never uses the rangtong/shentong vocabulary.

### Sentences that lean shentong, or could be read that way

No sentence asserts luminosity as a truly existing ground or as "empty of other". Every use of "ground" is either negated or hedged. The following are wording risks:

1. Companion: "even the ground-nature, the luminosity, the awareness itself, is seen as dependently designated… while remaining vividly present". The term "ground-nature" is kept and then emptied. Read quickly, it suggests a ground that persists.
2. Ye Shes: "Thoughts are not obstacles in front of the nature of mind; they are its display (rtsal), its own expressive energy." The possessive "its" implies a nature that owns the display. The very next paragraph corrects this.
3. Taste of Total Exertion: "Luminosity belongs to the event itself" / "The clarity belongs to the manifesting itself". This is property language. It is not shentong as such, but it is close to treating clarity as an intrinsic quality.
4. Last Refuge: "already primordially open, non-abiding, effortless" and ye shes "directly recognizes the primordially open… character of dependent arising". "Primordially" together with "character" can be read as an innate positive nature that was always there, which is the shape of a shentong-style claim. The book hedges this as "not as a positive remainder".
5. Resonances: "reasoning and direct recognition converging on one nature". "One nature" is never defined. A reader could take it as a single positive nature of mind.
6. Appendix Synthesis 2: "the ultimate state… resting in Unmodified Entirety ({O})" and "these two domains". This reifies an ultimate state that is distinct from the conventional.
7. Appendix Axiom 7 table: "self-luminous… event". "Self-luminous" (rang gsal) is traditional Dzogchen language, but here it lacks the main text's hedge "vividness of appearing, not a light".
8. Preface: "a full opening of radiant presence" and Starting Point: "single field of luminous immediacy". These are reports of earlier experience, but they are not marked as superseded at the point where they appear.
