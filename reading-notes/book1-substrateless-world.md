## 2. Core theses

1. **Intrinsic nature is logically impossible, not merely absent.** "Intrinsic nature is not merely absent from the world as a matter of fact; it is logically impossible for anything that appears in dependence—and nothing can be posited that does not." (Intro, The Claim.) It is "not an extra posit for which evidence is lacking, but a requirement that nothing could meet" (§2.4, the square circle).
2. **The argument is consequential, not inductive.** "Logical necessity" means "the necessity of such a consequence… a demonstration that the opponent's own proposal cannot be stated without undermining itself" (Intro). "The argument needs no premises of its own about being; it works with the opponent's" (§2.6).
3. **To posit anything is already to depend.** "To propose a phenomenon… is to pick it out. Picking out requires contrast… and it requires a designation under which it is singled out" (§2.5). This is Tsongkhapa's third sense of dependence, dependence on designation.
4. **The negation is non-implicative.** "a non-implicative negation (prasajya-pratiṣedha) that refutes intrinsic nature without putting anything in its place" (Intro). It "does not affirm that everything possesses the property of dependence" (§2.5). Conclusion: "The negation has been non-implicative throughout: it removes inherent existence and puts nothing in its place."
5. **Dependence too is designated and empty.** Dependent arising is an instrument, not a ground. "Throughout, dependent arising is used to demonstrate emptiness, never to assert a new ground" (Intro). "the argument does not make dependence a universal law of being, a new ground that everything obeys" (§2.5). Conclusion: "Nor is it a field of interdependence standing in their place: dependence, too, is empty."
6. **'Substrateless' names an absence, not a kind of world.** "'substrateless' names an absence, not a kind of world: the book describes no positive structure that reality has instead" (Intro).
7. **Relation is not the new foundation.** "Relation and relata are designated together, within the conceptual framework that distinguishes them; neither is prior, and neither is a ground" (Intro; §4.2, §4.6). "'Relation is primary' is useful as a corrective… and must be let go once it has done its work" (Intro; §4.5).
8. **Dependent arising and emptiness are one appearance seen from two directions** (§2.8). "Dependent arising can be equated with emptiness precisely because there is no nature to connect" (Interlude). "The dependence is the unfindability, seen from the side of convention" (§3.3).
9. **Unfindability is not non-existence.** "vivid appearance does not imply ontological solidity" (§3.1). Unfindability "is not non-existence" (Intro, Vocabulary).
10. **The eight extremes share one premise, whereabouts.** "All eight presuppose something with whereabouts… When it goes, the eight do not have to be refuted one by one; they lose what they were about" (§3.3). "The middle way does not select a middle position" (§3.9).
11. **Present without being produced; non-arising.** "Production and cessation are kept, as designations that work. Only the producer and the annihilated thing are let go" (§3.4). Non-arising is "not a denial of causal regularity, but the recognition that causal regularity, like everything it describes, is empty" (§3.5). Replacing a producer with "conditions" "would only exchange one producer for many" (§3.5).
12. **The two truths are non-dimensional.** "They are two aspects of one appearance, not two levels of reality. Non-dimensional names exactly this: there is no deeper level beneath appearances—no backstage, substrate or ground—and no higher level above them" (Intro; Ch 5).
13. **Appearance demonstrates emptiness.** "the formation of appearances is itself what demonstrates their emptiness" (§5.5). "This formation is not a residue left over once emptiness has done its work; it is the very way emptiness is shown" (§5.5).
14. **Inherent existence would make function impossible.** "Inherent existence is not merely unnecessary for feeling; it would make feeling impossible" (§5.5). "An anchor would stop it working" (§14.3). MMK 24.14 is the hinge.
15. **Absolute nothing is substantialism in reverse.** The void "rests on the exact same substantialist fallacy" (§6.1). "Absence is always relative" (§6.4). "Emptiness names only the fact that no ground, of being or of nothing, is found" (§6.11).
16. **Consciousness is designated together with its objects.** The Hard Problem is "an artifact of substantialist framing" (§7.1). "the subject, the act of experiencing, and the experienced object are designated together, none prior to the others" (§7.2). Consciousness is "not found apart from its objects" (§7.4).
17. **No invariant bearer; continuity is designated.** "identity across time is designated, not found" (§9.2). The person is the "mere I" (nga tsam), "valid only as designated" (§9.4).
18. **Designation is not a sticker.** "Designation is then not laid on top of an appearance; it is in the very appearing" (§13.3). "Designation and vividness are therefore mingled through and through, with no seam between them and no hierarchy" (§13.3). "There is no down" (§13.8).
19. **Designation is constrained, not arbitrary.** "constrained at every step by what holds up under further experience" (§13.1). Tsongkhapa's three criteria apply (§13.6). "Reality does not depend on language" (§13.7).
20. **Function needs no intrinsic power.** "action is designated in dependence on its relations, not produced by a relational process standing behind it" (§14.1). "The tremendous power of the world… is the power of what is dependent and empty, and therefore free to act" (§14.3).
21. **Science refines designations; it does not uncover a substrate.** Science is "the rigorous, empirical modelling of regularities within frameworks… regularities that, like the frameworks, are designated" (§15.1). Physics does not prove the view: "a reading compatible with physics, not one physics compels" (Conclusion).
22. **Emptiness must not be given a substrate** (Hilbert space, potentia, field, net). "To place the openness in a mathematical space behind them is to explain emptiness by giving it a substrate—the one explanation emptiness cannot have, and the one it does not need" (§15.6).
23. **No view in need of a cure.** "What remains is not a final picture of reality but freedom from needing one—appearances vivid and functional, and nothing found beneath them, not even emptiness" (Conclusion).
24. **Boundaries are designated, neither abolished nor merged.** "Boundaries lack inherent existence… They are not thereby abolished, and the things they separate are not thereby merged" (§4.8). "intimate without merging, distinct without walls" (§4.11).

## 3. Key terms (with the book's spelling)

All are from the Glossary unless marked (Text).

- **Acquired grasping** (Tib. *kun brtags*): "Learned belief in inherent existence, such as philosophical theories of substance." Contrast innate grasping.
- **Innate grasping** (Tib. *lhan skyes*): "The unlearned habit of perceiving and thinking things as existing from their own side." It is "present in animals and infants, and in philosophers too once they put their books down" (§1.2). The book's "real subject is the habit."
- **Basis of designation** (Tib. *gdags gzhi*): "What a designation is made upon… It is itself designated, never an undesignated layer beneath labels."
- **Co-definition**: "The identification of two or more terms only through each other, with neither prior." **Co-arising** (Text, Intro): "it means only that the terms are designated together, neither prior to the other—not that some process brings them forth."
- **Concordant and discordant experience**: "Mipham's second model of the two truths: the same appearance met without reification (concordant, authentic) or with it (discordant, mistaken)."
- **Condition** (Skt. *pratyaya*): "designated as such only in relation to its effect; it is not a producer standing on its own."
- **Conventional** (Skt. *saṃvṛti*, *vyavahāra*): "The world as lived, spoken of and acted on. Valid and correctable, not arbitrary and not second-rate, yet concealing its own emptiness." *Vyavahāra* = transaction, everyday usage. *Saṃvṛti* = concealment (Interlude). Candrakīrti's three senses: concealment, mutual dependence, worldly convention (§5.2).
- **Conventional truth** (Skt. *saṃvṛti-satya*): "Appearance as it functions, with its own standards of correct and mistaken… not a lower level of reality."
- **Dependence** (Text, Intro): "a thing's being identified only through what it is not—its parts, causes, contexts and contrasts, and the designation under which it is picked out."
- **Dependent arising** (Skt. *pratītyasamutpāda*): "The identification of anything only in dependence… It demonstrates emptiness; it is not interconnection between things, and not a ground."
- **Dependent appearing** (Text, used without a glossary entry; §6.8, §6.9, title of §7.4 "Consciousness as Non-Dual Dependent Appearing"): "Dependent appearing is not a continuum underlying them; it is how their emptiness is shown."
- **Designation** (Skt. *prajñapti*): "The picking out of something as this rather than that, by perception, concept or language. Constrained by what holds up under further experience; mingled with vividness, not stuck on a pre-given object." It is wider than language and includes pre-verbal discrimination (§13.7). Contrast **mere naming**, "an unconstrained label" (§13.1).
- **Dharma position** (Jpn. *jū hōi*): "each appearance abiding fully in its own position, with before and after cut off. The positions are designated, not found." Related: **being-time** (*uji*), "being and time are not two" (Interlude; §5.5).
- **Eight extremes**: "Arising and ceasing, permanence and annihilation, coming and going, identity and difference." **Extreme** (Skt. *anta*), "an end or edge… a place for a thing to be" (§3.3).
- **Emptiness** (Skt. *śūnyatā*): "The absence of inherent existence. Not a void, not a hidden essence and not a view to be held; itself empty." "always the emptiness of something, never a something on its own" (Interlude).
- **Inherent existence, own-nature** (Skt. *svabhāva*), **existence from its own side**: three names for one thing. "what it would have if it were what it is independently of everything else, including the mind that picks it out. The object of negation; never found." "It is the object of negation throughout, and nothing else is negated" (Intro).
- **Intrinsic nature** (Text): used interchangeably with the above throughout.
- **Mere I** (Tib. *nga tsam*): "Tsongkhapa's term for the person as merely designated in dependence on body and mind: fully able to act, yet not found under analysis." "Nor is the mere I a thin self left over when the thick one has been refuted" (§9.4).
- **Non-arising** (Skt. *anutpāda*): "under ultimate analysis, nothing is found to arise. Not a denial of conventional causal regularity."
- **Non-dimensional**: "This book's term for the absence of any deeper level beneath appearances, and of any higher level above them."
- **Non-implicative negation** (Skt. *prasajya-pratiṣedha*): "affirms nothing in place of what it negates. Contrast implicative negation (*paryudāsa*)."
- **Object of negation** (Tib. *dgag bya*): "inherent existence, not appearances themselves. Identifying it correctly is the first step."
- **Particular** (Skt. *svalakṣaṇa*): the Dignāga/Dharmakīrti self-characterised momentary object. "Prāsaṅgika refuses it at every level: nothing is established by its own characteristic, not even conventionally." Related (Text, §13.4): *sāmānyalakṣaṇa* (universal); *kalpanāpoḍha* (free from conceptual construction); *arthakriyā-samartha* (capable of causal efficacy); *apoha* (exclusion); *rang gi mtshan nyid kyis grub pa* (established by its own characteristic); *lakṣaṇa* / *lakṣya*.
- **Prāsaṅgika**: "proceeds by consequence (*prasaṅga*), drawing out the unwanted consequences of a position without asserting a thesis of its own."
- **Two truths**: "two aspects of one appearance, not two worlds or two levels."
- **Ultimate truth** (Skt. *paramārtha-satya*): "The emptiness of conventional appearance. Not another world behind it." In §5.3: "simply the total unfindability of an intrinsic nature."
- **Unfindability**: "What results when something is sought apart from its dependencies and nothing is found. Not non-existence."
- **Unobstructed** (Tib. *ma 'gags pa*): "Said of appearance: nothing blocks its showing. It does not mean a stream that lasts for ever." §3.5 gives *ma 'gags pa* as "unceasing", glossed as "unobstructed, not enduring". See Tensions.
- Other Sanskrit and Pali terms in the text: *prapañcopaśama* (quieting of elaborations, §3.9); *bhavasaṃtati* (continuum of becoming, MMK 21.21, §8.6); *ātman* (§9.1); *kṣaṇavāda* (momentariness, Interlude); *ākāśa* (space, Interlude); *catuṣkoṭi* (tetralemma, §15.6); *yujyate* ("is fitting", "holds together", "works", §15.6); *ousia* (§1.6); *nirvāṇa*, *saṃsāra*.
- **Spelling conventions:** full IAST diacritics (Nāgārjuna, Candrakīrti, Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, Madhyamakāvatāra, Prasannapadā, Vigrahavyāvartanī, śūnyatā, svabhāva, prajñapti, Śālistamba, Mādhyamika). Wylie for Tibetan in italics-style lower case (dgag bya, nga tsam, ma 'gags pa). Japanese with macrons (Dōgen, Genjōkōan, Shōbōgenzō, Gabyō, jū hōi, uji). The text writes "Shōbōgenzō" and the references write "Shōbō Genzō". The book's English uses -ize spellings (recognize, conceptualization, modelling, colour), i.e. Oxford spelling, not plain British -ise.

## 4. Rejected views

- **Substantialism, theoretical and innate** ("entities first, relations second", §1.1). Aristotle's *ousia*, Descartes' independent substance, Abhidharma *svabhāva* ("The self was emptied, but its components were left full", §1.6). Argued against by exhaustive search (the six questions, §2.2), by the incoherence of an independently identifiable ground (§2.1), and by MMK 15.1–2.
- **Intrinsic nature as the "fact that these dependencies obtain"** (§2.1). Either it is "a redundant re-description" or it is "eternally inaccessible (performing zero explanatory work)" (§2.2).
- **Realism of a determinate referent** ("dependence in being known ≠ in being", §2.6). The ordinary mind-independence of the mountain is kept. What is denied is that it "comes with its edges and its oneness already drawn". Locke's "something I know not what" is "the empty place where one was expected."
- **Grounding foundationalism / vicious regress** (Schaffer). The regress "has nothing to defer". "It is the grounding picture, not the Madhyamaka analysis, that needs a first link" (§2.7).
- **Relational foundationalism**: network, field, structure, "net of interactions" (Intro; §4.1, §4.7, §4.13; Ch 15). "'field' is itself a designation, as unfindable as the entities it replaces" (§4.7).
- **Ontic structural realism** (Ladyman & Ross): "Structure without relata is relation without relata" (§15.8).
- **Barad's agential realism as ontology**: "the phenomenon the primary ontological unit… repeats, at the level of relation, the substantialism it set out to overcome" (§15.7).
- **Priority monism / "all is one" / holism**: "a single whole that exists from its own side is a substance, however large" (§4.8). Holism "reverses the direction of priority rather than removing it" (§4.12).
- **Nihilism about distinctions** ("no boundaries at all", §4.8).
- **Nihilism / annihilationism / the absolute void** (Ch 3.10, Ch 6). "Nihilism and substantialism are the same mistake made from two sides" (§3.10). The void is "substantialism in reverse" (§6.1). Bergson's being-as-full is also refused (§6.2).
- **Eternalism / permanence** and "it exists" (MMK 15.10–11; §3.7).
- **Emptiness as void or as hidden ground**: "a vast spacious ground from which all things arise, sometimes equated with pure awareness… a new substance with a quieter name" (Interlude, Emptiness).
- **Emptiness as a view** (MMK 13.8; Conclusion).
- **Two-tier ontologies** (physicalism, emergentism, backstage/frontstage) (§5.1). Also the ultimate as a "higher realm" (Interlude).
- **Production / producer causation** ("a cause is a thing with power", Interlude). Also "conditions" as the new producer (§3.5, §4.2). "Conditions align" "is itself a way of speaking" (§3.4).
- **"Ongoing" appearance as a lasting stream**: "Going-on is a form of abiding" (§3.5).
- **Time as river; space as container; rebirth as traveller** (§1.3). Also Sarvāstivāda three-times existence and space as unconditioned (Interlude).
- **Materialism + representational dualism** generating the Hard Problem (§7.1). **Idealism** and **panpsychism** (§7.7: panpsychism is "if anything, the opposite of this book's view"). **Brain as producer** (§7.8). **Cartesian certainty of a thinker** (§7.9).
- **Luminous mind as "a radiant substance behind experience… an awareness that exists from its own side"** (§7.10). This is the book's witness/awareness-as-ground rejection.
- **"Ontological off-switch"** of consciousness in sleep and death (§8.1). Epicurus' annihilation premise (§8.7).
- **Invariant bearer**: soul (*ātman*), neural core, Cartesian ego (§9.1). **Parfit's reductionism**, which leaves events "full" (§9.4).
- **Internal representation model / Cartesian theatre / container model of mind**, and **naive realism** (Ch 10, Ch 12). **Predictive processing read as brain-as-ground** (§10.10). **Perceiver makes the world** (§10.11).
- **Extended mind as a bigger container** (§11.3). **Autopoietic organism as bedrock** (§11.8). **Homunculus / central executive** (§11.5). **Hard determinism or fatalism**, and **the uncaused will** (§11.6).
- **Buddhaghosa's asymmetry** (doing kept, doer removed), corrected by MMK 8.12 (§11.7).
- **Yogācāra / Mind-Only**: "to deny objects while keeping mind is to make mind the last substance" (§12.10).
- **Naive nominalism; designation as sticker; social constructivism** (Ch 13). **The Myth of the Given**, including Dignāga and Dharmakīrti's particular (§13.4). **McDowell "conceptual all the way down"** and **Dreyfus "non-conceptual at bottom"** (§13.8).
- **Intrinsic causal powers / dispositional essentialism** (Bird), and Searle's brute-fact layer (Ch 14).
- **Building-block reductionism; wavefunction realism; Heisenberg's potentia; consciousness-collapse (Wigner)**. Also Bohr's unanalysed classical framework (§15.6).
- **"Quantum mysticism"**: superposition is not the tetralemma (§15.6).
- **Krauss's "universe from nothing"** (§6.10). **Leibniz's question** is dissolved, not answered (§6.9).

## 5. Teaching devices

- **Whirlpool** (Intro; §13.2; Conclusion): vivid, timeable, yet "nothing can be found" apart from the water passing through. It frames the whole book.
- **Cube of ice / coldness** (Intro; §1.2; §5.5; recurring): the central device. Not in the ice, the hand, their meeting, or apart. Ice, coldness, feeler and felt are co-defined. "an inherent coldness could never be felt."
- **Phone in the hand** (§1.2; §2.2–2.3): the six questions (structure, origin, function, qualities, continuity, boundary). "When does the phone stop?" is a modern chariot.
- **Cup** (Interlude; §13.3; Conclusion test): "'cup' was never a jewel waiting to be strung". Seen as a cup from the start. The closing exercise.
- **Indra's net** (Interlude): shows interconnection as still substantialist ("Each jewel… is still a jewel in its own right").
- **Rainbow** (§3.1): unfindability in an object already suspected of being insubstantial.
- **Chariot**, Candrakīrti's sevenfold analysis (§3.2; Figure 2): unfindable, yet "as designated it rings / carries passengers". Also applied to the person (§9.4).
- **Match and flame** (Interlude; §3.4): no edge of production; "no fire waiting inside it".
- **Fire and fuel** (MMK 10; §4.4): co-definition; neither is established first.
- **Extinguished fire**, Vacchagotta (§3.6): "That does not apply". The question of whereabouts fails.
- **Lute music**, Lalitavistara (§3.6): came from nowhere, went nowhere.
- **Lamp switched off** (§3.6): where did the light go?
- **Sound of a passing car; a thought arising** (§3.3): whereabouts not found in perception or conception.
- **"Does this moment arise or cease?"** (§3.5): stalls the mind. The question "is not answered; it is released."
- **Bell** (§3.5; §6.3; §6.5): change met without a thing that changes; silence as a positive appearance.
- **Seed and sprout, balance beam** (Śālistamba; §3.7): neither permanence nor annihilation, "nothing carried through".
- **Long/short, parent/child, buyer/seller** (§4.3): co-definition is ordinary.
- **Clapping** (§4.10): the sound is neither the hands nor other than them. Meeting without fusion.
- **Seamless** (§4.11): "no seam is found… It does not mean one continuous cloth."
- **Firewood and ash** (Dōgen; §5.5; §9.2; Figure 7): before and after cut off; positions designated.
- **Silence; the empty chair** (§6.5–6.6): absence is formed and functional.
- **Sip of tea** (§7.5): no taster behind the tasting.
- **Going under anaesthesia; waking; dreaming / lucid dream** (§8.3–8.5): time and self as designated. Dream is vivid and unfound. Lucid recognition loses "only its grip".
- **Candle through the night; Nāgasena's lamp** (§8.8; §9.2): neither the same nor another.
- **Old photograph** (§9.3): "yes, that's me" is valid with nothing underneath. **Melody learned by heart** (§9.5): memory and character.
- **The Dress; duck-rabbit; reading; scarf under a sodium lamp** (Ch 10): seeing-as. "never an undesignated floor."
- **Eddington's two tables** (§10.7): no table is the real one.
- **Rope and snake** (§10.8; §13.6): a mistaken versus a correct convention; illustrates the three criteria.

## 6. Practice and realisation

The book is mainly argument. It sends the reader to *The Transition from Anatta to Emptiness* for the contemplative path: "how it came to be lived, from the realization of anatta to emptiness—is told separately" (Intro). It gives no stage model (no I AM, non-dual or anatta stages). It does commit to the following:

- **Testing in experience is integral.** "The analysis is not complete when it has been followed. It is complete when the thing in the hand is seen as vivid, functional and unfindable at once" (Intro). Most chapters include a "lived example".
- **The target is the innate habit, not theories.** "Refuting the theories leaves the innate sense untouched… its real subject is the habit" (§1.2). The habit "retreats to wherever it has not yet been examined" (Intro), so the analysis must be repeated in each domain.
- **Identifying the object of negation is step one** (Tsongkhapa, §1.3).
- **Insight as not-finding.** Mahāmudrā looking (where a thought comes from, stays, goes): "Its finding is the not-finding" (§3.6).
- **Release, not answer.** "The question is not answered; it is released. This is non-arising as it is actually tasted: not the denial of change, but change met without a thing that changes" (§3.5).
- **The taste is positive but not a ground.** "nothing is lost from the sting; it is, if anything, nearer and more vivid than before—met without a barrier… This is not a hidden fullness discovered beneath emptiness, and it is not offered as proof" (§5.5). Conclusion: "appearance, vivid and unowned, exactly where it is."
- **Concordant vs discordant** (Mipham's second model) is the experiential side: "Nothing different appears in the two cases; what differs is whether appearance is misread" (§5.5).
- **Felt solidity loosens.** Designation is felt as solid "Because there is no gap in which the designating could be noticed" (§13.3). When seen through: "Appearance becomes luminous and dream-like—not faint, not unreal, but light, like the world of a lucid dream" (§13.3).
- **No special state to cultivate.** The waking glimpse "is not offered as proof that there is no self, still less as a special state to cultivate" (§8.4). The cup exercise: "The point of the exercise is not to arrive at a feeling of emptiness, which would be one more thing to hold, but to notice that nothing was lost in the looking" (Conclusion).
- **Freedom is the loosening of grip, not a removal.** "What changes is their grip. An appearance known to depend on conditions can be met, worked with and changed, because nothing in it is fixed" (Conclusion). "a habit loosens, a grievance ends, a person changes."
- **Death and grief**: "the fear loses its object, while love, sorrow and care remain exactly where they were" (§8.7).
- **Forgetting the self** (Dōgen): what is forgotten is "the self as a spectator standing apart". This is not a merger (§12.8).
- **Responsibility and growth**: a fixed essence "could not be transformed by practice" (§9.7). Emptiness is what makes practice possible.
- The book has no explicit treatment of effort or effortlessness.

## 8. Tensions

**A. Possible conflicts with the nine-point canon**

1. **"Luminous" applied to appearance** (canon 1–2). §13.3: "What changes is the felt solidity. Appearance becomes luminous and dream-like—not faint, not unreal, but light, like the world of a lucid dream." §7.10 reads luminosity as "a description of appearance itself… that appearing is vivid and unobstructed." The book never calls *emptiness* luminous, and §5.5 says "'authentic' names a way of meeting appearance, not a luminous reality hidden behind it." Still, "appearance becomes luminous" makes luminosity something that happens to appearance, rather than, as canon 2 puts it, a mode of seeing. Wording to harmonise.
2. **Openness and plasticity stated as features of phenomena** (canon 2, 3). §15.6: "Quantum phenomena display… the emptiness of phenomena, seen here from the side of its function, and which may be called their plasticity." "The substantialist mind cannot let this openness simply be." "the openness has been relocated rather than understood." Here openness and plasticity are attributed to phenomena themselves, not to a mode of seeing. Canon 2 says openness is "not [a property] of emptiness".
3. **Emptiness as the explanation of appearing and function** (canon 3, 5). §15.6: "Phenomena can appear in such varied, dynamic and context-dependent ways precisely because they are empty." §14.3: "the power of what is dependent and empty, and therefore free to act." §5.5: "Because the coldness forms only where ice, hand… come together, it cannot be something that exists from its own side". That last runs in the safe direction (dependence shows emptiness). The first two make emptiness the *reason why* phenomena appear and act. Canon 3 says openness "does not explain why appearing occurs". The book itself warns against this exact move in the next sentence: "To place the openness in a mathematical space behind them is to explain emptiness by giving it a substrate". So the tension is one of phrasing. MMK 24.14 is glossed with *yujyate*, "fits / works", which is the safer reading and could govern.
4. **"Seeing that is the freedom"** (Conclusion, final line). Read with canon 5 and 8, this could suggest freedom is produced by an act of seeing. Elsewhere freedom is "freedom from needing one" and the loosening of grip, which fits canon 5. Minor.
5. **"Surprisingly positive" taste** (§5.5, §7.10, Conclusion). It is carefully hedged ("not a hidden fullness… not offered as proof"). It is consistent with canon 9 as written, but the phrase could be read implicatively. Worth a governing line.
6. **"Unceasing" / ma 'gags pa** (canon 1). §3.5 introduces the Dzogchen term as "unceasing (*ma 'gags pa*), the word means unobstructed, not enduring". The Glossary heads it as "Unobstructed (Tib. *ma 'gags pa*)". The literal sense is "unceasing". The book re-glosses it, which is defensible, but the English key term differs between text and glossary. In §7.10 the "display" is "unobstructed (ma 'gags pa)… nothing blocks its showing". This is a description of appearing, not of emptiness, so it is fine with canon 1.
7. **Conventional division** (canon 7). Fully consistent: boundaries "not thereby abolished" (§4.8); "Conventional truth is valid as mutual dependence and as convention" (§5.2). No conflict.
8. **Practice and effort** (canon 4, 8). The book is silent on effort and effortlessness and on clear knowing. There is no conflict, but also no coverage. The cup exercise's warning against "a feeling of emptiness, which would be one more thing to hold" parallels canon 8.

**B. Internal tensions and terminology inconsistencies**

1. **Two accounts of what constrains designation.** §13.2: "What keeps the designation from being arbitrary is not a pattern established from its own side, but its reliability in use." §13.8: "The constraint that keeps designation from being arbitrary comes from the vividness itself." The two can be reconciled (both are co-defined), but the source of constraint is named differently. The "vividness" version risks reading as a given floor, which the same section denies.
2. **Co-arising / co-definition / designated together / co-designation / mutual dependence / relational co-arising / relational event.** The Intro says these all mean only "designated together". Ch 10, however, calls perception "a relational event in the conventional sense", and §11.2 is headed "Mapping the 4Es onto Relational Co-Arising". §4.6 licenses this ("conventional descriptions… not a new substrate"), but the many names for one idea should be fixed.
3. **"Dependent arising" vs "dependent appearing".** Dependent appearing is used in §6.8–6.9 and §7.4 with no glossary entry. It needs a fixed sense.
4. **"Intrinsic nature" / "inherent existence" / "own-nature" / "existence from its own side" / "essence" / "independent essence" / "ontological solidity".** These are declared synonyms (Intro). "Intrinsic" dominates the argument chapters and "inherent" the Buddhist ones.
5. **"Non-dual."** It is used in §7.4's heading and the Part III/Conclusion summary ("Subject and object co-arise non-dually") without definition. Elsewhere the book avoids "non-dual" in favour of "neither one nor two".
6. **"Relation is primary"** is used as a corrective (§4.5), then disowned. It is consistent, but readers of other books may meet it unqualified.
7. **"Conditions come together / align."** "When conditions come together, appearances are simply present" (§3.4), and "Phenomena appear vividly when conditions come together" (§6.8), yet "'Conditions align' is itself a way of speaking" (§3.4). Consistent with canon 6, but the phrase recurs unmarked.
8. **"Plasticity" and "openness"** appear only in Ch 15, as functional synonyms for emptiness ("the emptiness of phenomena… which may be called their plasticity"). Other terms of the book (unfindability) are not used there.
9. **Spelling**: "Shōbōgenzō" (text) vs "Shōbō Genzō" (references). Oxford -ize spellings throughout (recognize, realization, conceptualization), which differ from plain British -ise if the canon prefers that. "Mipham" in text, "Mipam" in the Duckworth title.
10. **"Ultimately"** is sometimes used adverbially as if naming a level ("completely unfindable ultimately", §7.4; "ultimately, both are empty", §8.6), while the book denies levels. Conventional usage, but the potential for a level reading should be noted.

## 9. Candidate canon points

1. **Intrinsic nature is impossible, not merely missing.** The object of negation is inherent existence and nothing else. The argument is by consequence: any proposal of something standing on its own is already a picking out by contrast and designation. It needs no premises about being of its own.

2. **Dependence is designated too.** Dependent arising demonstrates emptiness and is then not left standing as an account of what reality is. Dependence is not a universal law, a network or a field. Interconnection between things is not dependent arising.

3. **Relation is not the new ground.** Relation and relata are designated together, within the framework that distinguishes them. "Relation is primary" is a corrective to be dropped once the habit of self-standing things loosens. Structure, field, net, phenomenon and process are designations, not substrates.

4. **The two truths are non-dimensional.** Conventional and ultimate are two aspects of one appearance, not two levels. There is no backstage beneath appearance and no higher realm above it. The ultimate is the unfindability of this very cup.

5. **Appearance demonstrates emptiness.** The forming and functioning of appearance is how emptiness is shown, not a residue left after negation. What existed from its own side could not be felt, act, combine or change. Phrasing should keep the direction "dependence shows emptiness", not "emptiness explains appearing".

6. **The extremes share one premise.** Arising, ceasing, permanence, annihilation, coming, going, identity and difference all ask where a thing is. When nothing with whereabouts is found, all eight lose their object together. The middle way is not a midpoint.

7. **Nothingness is substantialism in reverse.** A void needs a prior something to be annihilated, and "nothing" is only identified against something. Absence is a relative, designated appearance. Emptiness is never a nothing beneath things.

8. **Designation is not a sticker.** Designation is in the appearing, mingled with vividness, with no undesignated floor and no conceptual top coat. The basis of designation is itself designated. Neither concept nor appearance is prior: "There is no down."

9. **Designation is constrained.** Conventions are correct or mistaken by whether they hold up under further experience (Tsongkhapa's three criteria). The rope is not a snake, and a shadow is not a bridge. This is why the view is not relativism or nominalism.

10. **Consciousness is designated with its objects.** It is not a container, a producer, a witness or a luminous substance behind experience. Luminosity, rightly read, describes appearing as vivid and unobstructed, and is as empty as anything else. The Hard Problem is dissolved, not solved.

11. **Persons are mere I's.** No invariant bearer persists. Continuity is designated across what is neither identical nor wholly different. The mere I is not a thinner self left over; it acts, keeps promises and bears responsibility because it is merely designated.

12. **Function needs no anchor.** Efficacy is not an intrinsic power. Key, lock, hand and opening are designated together, and an anchor would stop function. Social and physical facts function in the same way, with no brute layer.

13. **Emptiness must not be given a home.** Hilbert space, potentia, a wave of possibilities and a "seamless field" are refined substrates for emptiness. "For whom emptiness is possible, everything is possible" means everything fits and works (*yujyate*). It does not mean a reservoir of possibilities.

14. **Boundaries are designated, neither abolished nor merged.** No seam is found, yet distinctions work. "Seamless" does not mean one cloth. Monism and holism are substantialism enlarged.

15. **Freedom is loosened grip, not a found state.** Nothing is taken from appearance. What goes is the felt solidity and the weight with which things are held. A feeling of emptiness held as an attainment is "one more thing to hold".

## 11. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity

(The coordinator asked for this section to be numbered 11. There is no section 10. Searches covered the full docx text.)

**Search result in brief.** The book never uses *rigpa*, *'od gsal*, *prabhāsvara*, "luminous clarity", rangtong/shentong (*rang stong*/*gzhan stong*), Longchenpa, *kadag* or *lhundrub*. "Nyingma" appears only in a reference title (Duckworth, *Mipam on Buddha-Nature: The Ground of the Nyingma Tradition*). "Dzogchen" appears once (§3.5). Mipham appears in §5.5, §13.3 and the Glossary. "Clear light" appears once (§7.10). On luminosity the book commits to one position: it is a description of appearing as vivid and unobstructed, and it is empty. **No sentence leans shentong.** Every mention of awareness or luminosity as a ground is put forward in order to be refuted.

### (a) Nature of mind vs nature of phenomena

The book has no separate "nature of mind" doctrine. Mind is given the same analysis, and the same emptiness, as everything else:
- §2.5: "Nāgārjuna states it plainly—there is nothing that is not dependently arisen, and therefore nothing that is not empty (MMK 24.19)." The candidates for an exception explicitly include mind: "Consider the strongest candidates for an exception: a creator, an absolute, a pure awareness that underlies all experience, a final particle, or the unconditioned itself. Each is proposed by contrast with what it is said not to be."
- Intro, How to Read: "Each chapter takes a candidate for what might stand on its own—a substance, a relation, a moment, a mind, a self, a power, a particle—and applies the same test".
- §3.1: "Ordinary phenomena—such as a table, an organism, or a mind—differ only in that we are more accustomed to treating them as solid, standalone entities."
- §5.3: "When we examine any phenomenon—whether a physical object, a mental state, or a law of nature—in search of an independent core that exists from its own side, no such core can be found."
- §5.5 (end): "What is true of the coldness is true of the phone in your hand, the page you are reading, and the one who reads it."
- §7.4: "The feeling of awareness, the presence of sensory qualities and the coherence of mind are thoroughly real and functional conventionally and completely unfindable ultimately."
- §12.10 (Candrakīrti vs Mind-Only): "Ultimately, neither mind nor object is found; to deny objects while keeping mind is to make mind the last substance (Madhyamakāvatāra 6.45–97)… the dreamed mountain and the dreaming mind appear together and are equally unfound on waking."
- Conclusion, What Has Not Been Claimed: "It has not argued that relation, process, structure, experience or awareness is what reality is really made of; each of these candidates was examined and found to be designated, like everything else."
- Part III Overview: "conscious experience is designated together with its objects, neither prior to them nor produced by them."

### (b) Luminosity, clarity, awareness, presence, vividness

**Luminosity and clear light, the one dedicated passage (§7.10, "Objection: What of the Luminous Mind?"), quoted in full:**
> "Buddhist readers may raise a different concern. The tradition itself calls the mind luminous—'luminous, monks, is this mind', in an early discourse (Aṅguttara Nikāya 1.51–52)—and later traditions speak of clear light and of awareness as the nature of mind. Is the book denying something its own sources affirm?
> It denies only one reading of them. If luminosity is taken as a radiant substance behind experience—an awareness that exists from its own side, in which appearances arise and pass—then it is one more ground, and the analysis of this book applies to it as to any other. But luminosity can be read otherwise: as saying simply that appearing is vivid and unobstructed, that nothing in the nature of things prevents the cold from stinging or the tea from tasting. Read in this way, luminosity is not a container but a description of appearance itself. The Tibetan tradition calls such display unobstructed (ma 'gags pa), which means not that something lasts for ever, but that nothing blocks its showing. It is the same taste this book has called surprisingly positive, and it is as empty as everything else."

How it is framed: *as ground* (rejected); *as container/witness* (rejected); *as a description of appearing, vivid and unobstructed* (accepted); *as empty* (explicitly). The phrase "nothing in the nature of things prevents the cold from stinging" is a negative ("nothing blocks") rather than a property, which fits the canon.

**Other luminosity passages:**
- §13.3: "When this is seen through, nothing vivid is lost… What changes is the felt solidity. Appearance becomes luminous and dream-like—not faint, not unreal, but light, like the world of a lucid dream, in which everything remains vivid and works by its own logic while being known, all the while, as dream… This is the concordant experience of Mipham's second model in Chapter 5: the same appearances, no longer misread." Framed as how appearance is met when reification is seen through, i.e. a mode of meeting. The verb "becomes" makes it sound like a change in appearance itself (flagged in §8 A1).
- §5.5 (Mipham): "This second model allows the vividness to be spoken of positively without being made into a positive ground. Concordant experience is itself empty: 'authentic' names a way of meeting appearance, not a luminous reality hidden behind it." This explicitly denies luminosity as a hidden reality. It is the clearest statement that luminosity is a mode of meeting.
- §3.6: "Nothing luminous travelled down the cable." (Ordinary use.)

**Awareness:**
- Interlude, Emptiness: "or it is a hidden essence, a vast spacious ground from which all things arise, sometimes equated with pure awareness. The first is nihilism, the second a new substance with a quieter name." This is an explicit rejection of awareness-as-ground.
- §2.5: "a pure awareness that underlies all experience" is listed as a candidate exception and refuted (see (a)).
- §8.1: "This binary view relies on a fundamental misconception: that awareness is an invariant, self-contained entity that is either 'turned on' (present) or 'turned off' (annihilated)."
- §8.2: processes continue in sleep "not as a deeper layer persisting beneath awareness, but as other aspects of the same dependent appearance."
- §7.3: "Consciousness here is not a standing light that is switched on and then directed at objects… No consciousness is described that is not of something, through something." "The Hard Problem asks how a subjective light is kindled in objective matter. The Madhyamaka asks first whether a light, a matter or a kindling can be found apart from the others, and finds none."
- §7.4: consciousness "is not a container… not a hidden producer, a homunculus standing behind experience… not found apart from its objects". This rejects the witness.
- §7.5 (tea): "not found as a separate light in which the tea appears"; "there is no separate taster standing behind the tasting."
- §7.9 (cogito): "Its indubitability is the indubitability of vivid appearance… not the certainty of a ground."
- §12.8 (Dōgen): what is forgotten is "the self as a spectator standing apart". §12.6: "there is no unconditioned spectator inside the head watching experience unfold."

**Presence and vividness** (the book's main positive vocabulary, always paired with unfindability):
- Intro: "that double fact—vivid presence and complete unfindability".
- §3.1: "vivid appearance does not imply ontological solidity."
- §3.4: "When conditions come together, appearances are simply present… There is simply the flame, present, as they are present."
- §3.9: "the world no longer asked to say where it is, and found, without that demand, to be fully present."
- §10.6: "Perception is the direct presence of what appears, designated together with the one who perceives".
- §5.5: "appearance, vivid and unowned, exactly where it is"; "This is not a hidden fullness discovered beneath emptiness".
- §13.3: "Designation and vividness are therefore mingled through and through, with no seam between them and no hierarchy."
- §13.8: "The constraint that keeps designation from being arbitrary comes from the vividness itself." Here vividness does constraining work, which is the nearest the book comes to giving vividness a ground-like role. The same section adds "Neither is the ground of the other" (flagged in §8 B1).
- Conclusion, The Taste: "What remains is appearance, vivid and unowned, exactly where it is—not a final picture of reality, and not a hidden fullness beneath it, but the ordinary world met without reification."

**Clarity**: never used as a technical term. The only occurrence is "with unusual clarity" (§15.6, ordinary use).

### (c) Rangtong, shentong, Nyingma, Dzogchen, Mipham, Longchenpa, kadag/lhundrub

- **Dzogchen** (§3.5), the only mention: "Where the Dzogchen tradition speaks of appearance as unceasing (ma 'gags pa), the word means unobstructed, not enduring: the display is unborn and unimpeded at once, emptiness and appearance not two. It does not name a stream that lasts." This reads Dzogchen's term through MMK 7 (no abiding). It fits the author's stance that Dzogchen takes the MMK as its view. "emptiness and appearance not two" is the nearest the book comes to Dzogchen union language.
- **Mipham**: Intro, "Mipham's two models of the two truths… help to show how the negation and the vividness belong together". §5.5: the full two-models passage (concordant/discordant; "Concordant experience is itself empty"). §13.3: lucid-dream taste = "concordant experience of Mipham's second model". Glossary entry "Concordant and discordant experience". Sources: Duckworth 2008, 2010; Karma Phuntsho 2005. Mipham is used only for the two truths and never for buddha-nature or for a ground.
- **Nyingma**: only in the Duckworth reference title.
- **Rangtong / shentong / Longchenpa / kadag / lhundrub / rigpa / 'od gsal / prabhāsvara / tathāgatagarbha / buddha-nature**: **absent**. The book's lineage is stated as Prāsaṅgika (Nāgārjuna–Candrakīrti–Tsongkhapa, with Mipham and Dōgen). That is a rangtong reading in substance, but the label is never used.
- **Shentong-leaning sentences**: **none found.** Every candidate is put up only to be refuted: "pure awareness that underlies all experience" (§2.5), "a vast spacious ground… pure awareness" (Interlude), "a radiant substance behind experience" (§7.10). Nothing is called "empty of other". The phrase "not a luminous reality hidden behind it" (§5.5) is an explicit anti-shentong safeguard. The only sentences to watch are phrasing issues, not shentong: "Appearance becomes luminous" (§13.3); vividness as the source of constraint (§13.8); and "the same taste this book has called surprisingly positive" (§7.10), which is positive language kept non-implicative by "as empty as everything else".

**Implication for the canon.** The book supplies a clean line that fits the stated stance: "Luminosity is not a container but a description of appearance itself… unobstructed… as empty as everything else" (§7.10), together with "'authentic' names a way of meeting appearance, not a luminous reality hidden behind it" (§5.5). It could be adopted as a governing point: **Luminosity describes appearing, not a ground.** Luminosity names that appearing is vivid and unobstructed (*ma 'gags pa*): nothing blocks its showing. It is not a radiant awareness behind experience, it does not last, and it is as empty as everything else. Mind and phenomena are empty in the same way (MMK 24.19).
