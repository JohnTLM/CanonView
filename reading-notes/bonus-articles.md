## A. The Logic of Dependency

### 1. Source
- File: `logic-of-dependency-article.md`, id `1tOQZJ-ETY3P-Fs0DWIg4Snrg8nJY2NFr`. Header: "Bonus article · The Logical Necessity of a Substrateless World". Byline "By John Tan (Thusness)". It links to the artifact (`#logic`) and two videos.
- Structure. The headings are:
  - Two Logics, Two Starting Points: sets substance against dependency.
  - Do We Actually Think Substantially?: six places where substantial thinking shows (space, time, grammar, development, emotion, the question itself).
  - What 'Logic' Means Here: logic means prasaṅga consequence and non-contradiction.
  - Change Without a Changer: there is change, but no carrier underneath it.
  - The Eight Extremes Turned Over: the eight extremes in a table.
  - Identity: From Essence to Criteria: identity rests on criteria, not essence.
  - Conventional, Not 'Merely' Conventional: convention is fully valid.
  - Continuity Without a Carrier: Nāgasena's lamp, and the melody.
  - Objection: Isn't Energy the Substrate?: the same text as article B.
  - Objection: Potentiality and Plasticity: no store of potentials.
  - A Different Path: Conditions and Openness: the path reorganised around three recognitions.
  - The Two Logics Side by Side: a summary table.
  - Closing: a return to the candle.
- The text still uses book framing: "this chapter", "previous chapters", and cross-references to Chapters 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 9, 13, 15 and the Interlude.

### 2. Core theses
1. There are two logics with different starting points. Substance "starts with things, and then asks what happens to them". Dependency "asks how anything is identified as a thing in the first place" (Two Logics).
2. Substantialist reasoning is real. It is "not a constant belief but a default that switches on at particular places: when we explain, when we name, when we feel threatened, when we lose" (Do We…).
3. Space and time are the base pictures, and the logic of dependency "has to reach space and time" or the substantialist picture "survives intact underneath it".
4. "Logic" means "the necessity of a consequence (prasaṅga)". It is not inductive and it relies on non-contradiction. "Logic has not been suspended. The question had nothing to be about" (What 'Logic' Means).
5. The fourfold refusals "refuse a presupposition". They are not true contradictions. This is shown with the king of France analogy.
6. The conclusion is "a non-implicative negation (prasajya-pratiṣedha) that removes inherent existence and installs nothing in its place".
7. Change and the changer are "designated together within a framework of before and after". Dependency "does not replace the subject with a better subject, such as a process, a flow or an energy" (Change Without a Changer).
8. The eight extremes "are eight ways of asking where a thing is". "Dependence and unfindability are one fact, seen from the side of convention and from the side of analysis" (Eight Extremes).
9. Identity is "how it is reliably picked out", and "not arbitrary". "It is the reality of a designation that works" (Identity).
10. "Conventional truth is the only kind of validity there is, and it is fully valid." "Ultimate truth is not a higher world. It is the emptiness of this one… There is one appearance, seen two ways" (Conventional).
11. Continuity is "designated on a succession that is neither identical nor wholly different". "Continuity without a carrier is more reliable than the nihilist expects, and more open than the substantialist can allow."
12. Energy is a conserved quantity. It is "a designated regularity of the highest reliability. It is not a passenger."
13. Potentiality: "'Could' is a designation made within a framework of conditions, not a thing waiting backstage." "Appearances are open because they depend, and constrained for the same reason."
14. "A plasticity independent of conditions would itself be a kind of own-nature… that is the idealist extreme."
15. On the path: "Conditions are what work". "Openness is not a virtue added on top. It is what being dependent already means." "There is nothing to hold, and no one apart from the holding."
16. "Openness, too, is empty. It is not something one has. It is the absence of what one was holding."

### 3. Key terms
- **logic of substance / logic of dependency**: the two patterns of inference.
- **prasaṅga**: consequence.
- **prasajya-pratiṣedha**: non-implicative negation.
- **lhan skyes**: innate grasping.
- **psychological essentialism**: the laboratory description of innate grasping.
- **eight extremes**: four pairs.
- **whereabouts**: a position in time, space or identity that belongs to a thing from its own side.
- **passenger / carrier**: the reified bearer of change.
- **criteria**: of identity.
- **Tsongkhapa's three criteria**: of conventional validity.
- **mere I**: credited to Chapter 9.
- **designated regularity**: applied to energy.
- **yujyate**: "fits" or "works" (MMK 24.14).
- **unobstructed (ma 'gags pa)**: "Nothing blocks the display, and there is no store behind it".
- **openness**: "what being dependent already means".

### 4. Rejected views
- Substrate or carrier for change: process, flow and energy are each a "better subject".
- Space as container and time as river: Newton's absolute space and time, and Kant.
- "Beyond logic" readings of Madhyamaka: "A view that claims to transcend logic can say anything, and so says nothing."
- "Merely" conventional, that is, convention as demotion.
- A second, deeper truth.
- Disconnected flashes, which is nihilism about continuity.
- Energy as substance.
- Potentiality as a reservoir. This is "a substrate for emptiness".
- Unlimited plasticity, the idealist extreme.
- Path as a traveller's journey.
- Openness or emptiness made into a new held view (MMK 13.8).

### 5. Teaching devices
- Candle and flame: frames the article and shows change without a changer.
- Room or stage and river: space and time as substance.
- King of France: presupposition failure.
- Walker and walking.
- Lamp switched off and on: "the light went nowhere… came from nowhere".
- Seed and sprout, and rice yields rice.
- Cracked phone, ship of Theseus, childhood photograph: identity by criteria.
- Snake and rope.
- Nāgasena's lamp, and a melody known by heart: continuity.
- Moving ball and runner, and the match: energy.
- The dress, duck-rabbit, particle and wave, salt and water: plasticity and its limits.
- Anger "lifted out like a stone".
- Being falsely accused or praised (Tsongkhapa).
- Raccoon painted as a skunk (Keil).
- General anaesthetic.

### 6. Practice and realisation
- The test is immediate: "look for the something… The looking finds more change, vivid and exact, and no changer behind it."
- Practice arranges conditions. It is not an attack on fixed defects. "Practice works because nothing is fixed" (MMK 24.16–20).
- The practitioner "is the mere I, designated on the practising".
- The last trap is holding openness. The article names no stages (I AM and so on).

### 7. Sources relied on
- MMK 1.5, 2 and 2.18–20, 5, 10.1, 13.8, 15.10, 18.10, 19, 24.14, 24.16–20, 27.
- SN 12.15; MN 72; Milindapañha (lamp); Visuddhimagga XVI.90.
- Tsongkhapa, Great Treatise vol. 3 (object of negation), and his three criteria.
- Western sources: Newton's Principia Scholium; Kant; Lakoff & Johnson; Núñez & Sweetser; Spelke; Keil; Gelman; Feynman vol. 1 ch. 4; Noether 1918; Aristotle; Heisenberg's potentia.

### 8. Tensions
- **Openness as explanation.** "Openness is not a virtue added on top. It is what being dependent already means." This fits canon 3. However, "Practice works because nothing is fixed" and "Appearances are open because they depend" use "because" to explain. That is acceptable under canon 3 ("open because it is dependent"), but no wording should drift further toward openness explaining appearing.
- **Leftover book framing.** "This chapter", "previous chapters" and "Chapter 15 called a substrate for emptiness" all appear in a standalone article. These chapter numbers need checking against Book 1. They do not match the Part IV reconciliation book's numbering, where Ch. 13 is *Analysis and the Dividing Mind* and Ch. 15 is *Non-Dividing Seeing*. Tsongkhapa's three criteria are cited to "Chapter 13", but in the reconciliation book the criteria chapter is Ch. 12.
- **"Unobstructed (ma 'gags pa)".** Article E uses *thogs med* for "unobstructedness". See section I.
- "The innate grasping (lhan skyes)" is cited to Chapter 1 without diacritics. That is consistent with Wylie.
- No conflict with the nine canon points was found otherwise. Its negation is explicitly non-implicative.

### 9. Candidate canon points
1. **Substance and dependency are two logics, not two beliefs.** Substance begins with things and asks what happens to them. Dependency asks how anything is identified as a thing at all. The same words (change, cause, identity, path) lead the two into different worlds.
2. **Madhyamaka logic is consequence, not transcendence of logic.** It borrows the opponent's premises and shows that they cannot be stated without undermining themselves. It relies on non-contradiction throughout. The fourfold refusals refuse a presupposition, the bearer. They are not contradictions.
3. **Change without a changer.** Change and changer are designated together within before and after. No better subject (process, flow, energy) replaces the old one.
4. **Dependence and unfindability are one fact.** The eight negations describe dependent arising itself. They are not a second property added to it.
5. **Identity is criteria, not essence.** Sameness is "the same by which criteria, for which purpose". It is correctable and not arbitrary.
6. **Convention is the only validity, and it is full validity.** Ultimate truth is the emptiness of this world, not a higher one: one appearance, seen two ways.
7. **Continuity without a carrier.** It is designated across moments that are neither identical nor wholly different. Nothing is lost and nothing is fixed.
8. **No substrate of potentials.** "Could" is a designation within conditions. Appearances are exactly as plastic as their conditions allow.

### 10. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity
- **(a) Mind versus phenomena.** The article does not discuss the "nature of mind". It applies one emptiness to object, self and time together: "Ultimate truth is not a higher world. It is the emptiness of this one: the fact that the conventional table, the conventional self and the conventional hour are not found from their own side" (Conventional). The practitioner is also the "mere I, designated on the practising" (A Different Path). This matches MMK 24.19 implicitly; the verse is not cited.
- **(b) Luminosity, clarity, presence, vividness.** None of these words is used as a doctrinal term. The related passages are:
  - "The looking finds more change, vivid and exact, and no changer behind it" (Change Without a Changer). Here vividness is a mode of appearing.
  - "an 'I' appears, vivid and solid" (Do We…, In emotion). Here "vivid and solid" describes the reified self.
  - "The Dzogchen word unobstructed (ma 'gags pa) fits better than 'potential'. Nothing blocks the display, and there is no store behind it from which the display comes" (Potentiality). The display is unobstructed, with no ground behind it.
  - Closing: "the burning never anything apart from the wax, the air and the one who watches". The watcher is listed among the conditions, not set behind them.
- **(c) Traditions.** Dzogchen is named once (ma 'gags pa). Tsongkhapa is the main Tibetan authority. Mipham, Longchenpa, Gorampa, Nyingma, rangtong, shentong, kadag and lhun grub do not appear.
- **Shentong lean.** None. Rejecting "a plasticity independent of conditions… the idealist extreme" also guards against a mind-only reading.

## B. Isn't Energy the Substrate?

### 1. Source
- File: `isnt-energy-the-substrate-article.md`, id `1N8EKigetaqt2_SyKQH82Wy_hQ78OIJiA`. Header: "Bonus article · The Logical Necessity of a Substrateless World". It links to the artifact (`#energy`) and two videos.
- Structure: four paragraphs, no subheadings. It ends "*From* The Logic of Dependency".
- The body is word-for-word the section "Objection: Isn't Energy the Substrate?" in article A.

### 2. Core theses
1. Energy is not "a fluid poured from one container into another. It is a quantity, a number computed in different ways for different forms, whose total for a closed system comes out the same before and after."
2. Physics "has no picture of energy as blobs of anything, only a calculation that keeps balancing" (Feynman).
3. The amount of energy is frame-dependent: "no amount belongs to the ball by itself."
4. Conservation follows from time-symmetry (Noether): it is "a feature of how regularities hang together, not a store of stuff."
5. In an expanding universe "there is no well-defined total… A true substance could not simply fail to add up."
6. "'Where did the energy come from, and where did it go?' is the extreme of coming and going asked of a number."
7. "Conservation is a designated regularity of the highest reliability. It is not a passenger."

### 3. Key terms
- **substrate / carrier / passenger**.
- **designated regularity**.
- **closed system; frame of reference; symmetry (Noether)**.
- **extreme of coming and going**.
- **convention**: "among the most reliable conventions there are".

### 4. Rejected views
- Energy as the modern successor to substance, the thing that persists through change. It is answered from physics (quantity, frame-dependence, symmetry, cosmological non-conservation) and from Madhyamaka (coming and going).

### 5. Teaching devices
Chemical into heat into motion; mass into light; the moving ball and the runner keeping pace; the match head and the flame.

### 6. Practice and realisation
Not addressed.

### 7. Sources relied on
- Feynman Lectures vol. 1 ch. 4.
- Noether's theorem (1918).
- The eight extremes, implicitly from the MMK homage.

### 8. Tensions
- "From the side of this book" survives in a standalone article. It should say "this article", or name Book 1.
- Duplication: when article A changes, B must change too. It is a verbatim excerpt.
- No canon conflict.

### 9. Candidate canon points
1. **Energy is a conserved quantity, not a substance.** Its conservation is a designated regularity of the highest reliability. It does not carry anything through change.
2. **Coming and going asked of a number is still an extreme.** "Where did the energy go?" presupposes whereabouts, and none is found. The conventional sentence remains correct.

### 10. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity
Not addressed. The article is entirely about energy in physics. It has no passages on mind, luminosity or the traditions, and no shentong lean.

## C. Death and Continuity

### 1. Source
- File: `death-and-continuity-article.md`, id `1G0ALgBEeAujEpm9f0B6QPne1iKpN1Y7E`. Header: "Bonus article · The Logical Necessity of a Substrateless World". Subtitle: "Sleep, anaesthesia and death read without an invariant bearer, through Dōgen's firewood and ash and Tsongkhapa's mere I." It links to the artifact (`#death`) and two videos.
- Structure:
  - Untitled opening: three paragraphs (Dōgen; anaesthesia; the candle and the mere I).
  - What the Fear Is Tracking: the fear presupposes a possession and a possessor.
  - Why Experience Needs No Carrier: luminosity is sufficient.
  - Seeing It Again and Again: repeated looking at how appearances form (bell, ice and fire).
  - Continuity and the Habit: the habit makes continuity look like a possession, and "the designation is the continuity".
  - What Changes in the Dying: what the view removes and what it does not.
  - The Candle, and the Weight of Watching: closing.

### 2. Core theses
1. Dōgen: firewood and ash "each abides fully in its own position. Life does not become death, as winter does not become spring" (opening).
2. Under anaesthesia "no river of time flowed by itself". The self on waking is "fully functional, and nowhere to be found as a standing spectator".
3. The person "is the mere I, designated on changing body and mind, which keeps its promises and can still change."
4. The dread of death "presupposes two things: that experience is a possession, and that a possessor stands behind it" (Fear).
5. "The logic of dependency does not simply deny the possessor. It shows that experience does not need one" (Why Experience…).
6. Luminosity "is not a substance behind the display. It is the knowing clarity of the display."
7. "Dependent designation accounts for the reliability of an appearance and its correction by conditions. Luminosity accounts for its vividness. Its passing is accounted for by the conditions changing. Nothing else is needed."
8. "Death is not the loss of a stored experience by a stored self. It is the ending of a set of conditions for a particular vividness."
9. "The conviction is not reached once and kept. It is reached by looking, repeatedly, at how appearances form" (Seeing…).
10. "The vividness has never been in the object as an own-nature. It forms in dependence, and it is complete as formed."
11. "The framework is seen to be added, not found."
12. "The designation is the continuity, as far as anything is continuous." "This is not an illusion laid over a real flow" (Continuity and the Habit).
13. "Nor does the vividness persist. What appears is new at each moment."
14. The view "does not promise that anything of the person continues as the person, and a book that promised that would be claiming a carrier after all" (Dying).
15. "Because nothing is being held, nothing is deferred." Luminosity "is available now, including to the one who is dying."

### 3. Key terms
- **mere I**: Tsongkhapa.
- **invariant bearer; carrier; possessor/possession; holder; standing spectator**.
- **luminosity**: "the knowing clarity of the display". It is credited to "the Dzogchen tradition".
- **vividness**.
- **dependent designation**.
- **own-nature**.
- **inherent framework**.
- **continuity**: designated.
- **logic of dependency**: linking to article A.
- No Sanskrit or Tibetan terms appear, even for luminosity (no *'od gsal*).

### 4. Rejected views
- A possessor of experience, and experience as a possession.
- Every carrier: "a soul, a stream of consciousness, a neural core, a store of potentialities or a conserved energy". Each "is itself an object of seeing… so it is designated like everything else and would need a carrier of its own" (a regress argument).
- A river of time.
- A hidden real flow under the designation of continuity.
- Promises of personal survival "as the person".
- Cold as an own-nature in ice.

### 5. Teaching devices
- Dōgen's firewood and ash: each moment is in its own position.
- The anaesthetic.
- The candle through the night (Nāgasena implied) and "the weight of watching".
- The struck bell: a sound formed from conditions, vivid in itself.
- Ice and fire: vividness without own-nature.
- The melody.
- "The taste of this afternoon and the light in this room".

### 6. Practice and realisation
- Repeated seeing loosens the assumption. The change "is in how the habit is met each time, not in a substance that has been improved".
- The new understanding is "one more appearance". "Nothing persists from the old understanding to the new one, yet the new one is clearer."
- There is no stage language. Practice is noticing "a pattern that does not change when attention is applied to it".

### 7. Sources relied on
- Dōgen (firewood and ash, from Genjōkōan, not named).
- Tsongkhapa (mere I).
- "The Dzogchen tradition" (luminosity), unnamed.
- Implicitly Nāgasena (flame).

### 8. Tensions
- **Luminosity given explanatory and exempt status.** "Luminosity accounts for its vividness" and "This is why luminosity is sufficient". Also: "Luminosity is not such an object. It is the clarity by which an appearance is known". Every other candidate is said to be "designated like everything else". Luminosity alone is exempted from being an object or designation. This sits close to awareness as ground (canon 6: "No foundation, no hierarchy", and canon 9). It also conflicts with the claim in articles A, E and F that even designation, the analysis and emptiness are themselves empty. The article never says luminosity is itself empty or dependent. Suggest adding that luminosity is itself empty, and is not a factor alongside conditions.
- **"Clear in itself" / "the ringing's own".** "it is vivid because it is clear in itself" and "the vividness is seen to be the ringing's own, not borrowed". The "in itself" and "own" wording clashes with the article's own "the vividness has never been in the object as an own-nature". Article F warns: "the word 'own,' which is the easiest place for a self to return."
- **Vividness persists or not.** "Nor does the vividness persist" (Continuity) is fine. But the closing "the vividness of this moment, has never needed a bearer to be complete" combined with "luminosity… is available now" can read as a standing luminosity. This is minor.
- **"It is the fact of appearance, undivided, lit and passing."** This is acceptable, but "undivided" could be read as a non-dual ground if it is detached from canon 7.
- **Typography.** Curly apostrophes ("fear’s", "tune’s", "flame’s") are mixed with straight ones in other articles. This is minor.
- **Terminology.** "Luminosity" here is the clarity of the display. In E and F, luminosity is what self-knowing "is" (Mipham, the flow's luminosity). In G, vividness is explained by emptiness instead. See section I.

### 9. Candidate canon points
1. **Experience needs no possessor.** The fear of death presupposes a possession and a possessor. Remove both and the dread has no subject and no object, though pain and grief remain real conditions.
2. **Every proposed carrier is itself a designated appearance.** Soul, stream, neural core, potential, energy: each appears, is named and is picked out by criteria. So each would need a carrier of its own.
3. **Continuity is the designation, not a flow under it.** The designation is not an illusion over a hidden stream. It is the continuity, as far as anything is continuous.
4. **Vividness is formed, not owned.** Cold is not in the ice. Vividness forms in dependence and is complete as formed, new at each moment.
5. **No promise of survival as the person.** Promising that would be claiming a carrier. What the view removes is the dread of a holding taken away, not grief or pain.
6. **Understanding changes without anything carried over.** The clearer understanding is one more appearance. The change is in how the habit is met.
7. **Luminosity is the knowing clarity of the display, not a substance behind it.** This is proposed only with the addition that luminosity is itself empty and not an exempt ground (see Tensions).

### 10. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity
- **(a) Mind versus phenomena.** The article never says whether mind and phenomena share one emptiness, and it does not cite MMK 24.19. The person is "the mere I, designated on changing body and mind" (opening). Mind is treated through luminosity rather than through emptiness.
- **(b) Luminosity and vividness.** Every passage, quoted exactly:
  - "It is valid because it works, and it is vivid because it is clear in itself. That clarity is what the Dzogchen tradition points to with the word luminosity. It is not a substance behind the display. It is the knowing clarity of the display, the way a sound is heard as sound and a cold is felt as cold." (Why Experience Needs No Carrier)
  - "This is why luminosity is sufficient. Every candidate for a carrier… is itself an object of seeing… Luminosity is not such an object. It is the clarity by which an appearance is known as it appears. Dependent designation accounts for the reliability of an appearance and its correction by conditions. Luminosity accounts for its vividness. Its passing is accounted for by the conditions changing. Nothing else is needed." (same section)
  - "A reader who tries to name what luminosity requires beyond these will find that each attempt turns the clarity into an object, and each object is again a designated appearance." (same section)
  - "Keep looking, and the vividness is seen to be the ringing's own, not borrowed from a stored essence of sound waiting in the bell." (Seeing It Again and Again)
  - "The point is that the vividness has never been in the object as an own-nature. It forms in dependence, and it is complete as formed." (same section)
  - "Nor does the vividness persist. What appears is new at each moment" (Continuity and the Habit).
  - "The luminosity of this appearance, its clarity, its colour and the sound in the next room, is available now, including to the one who is dying." (What Changes in the Dying)
  - "It is the fact of appearance, undivided, lit and passing. The light was never something the candle owned" (The Candle).
  - "what remains, the vividness of this moment, has never needed a bearer to be complete" (closing line).
- **How luminosity is framed.**
  - Mostly as a mode of appearing: "of the display", "of this appearance". It is denied to be a substance. This fits the Nyingma reading.
  - It is never said to be empty or dependent.
  - It is given an explanatory role ("accounts for its vividness") alongside dependent designation.
  - It is explicitly exempted from object status ("Luminosity is not such an object") while every other candidate "is designated like everything else".
  - It is not framed as a witness. "Clear in itself" and "the ringing's own" give it a self-standing flavour.
- **(c) Traditions.** "The Dzogchen tradition" is named, with no text or teacher. Dōgen and Tsongkhapa appear. Mipham, Longchenpa, Gorampa, Nyingma, rangtong, shentong, kadag, lhun grub, 'od gsal and prabhāsvara do not.
- **Shentong lean (flag).** Moderate. The trio "luminosity is sufficient" / "Luminosity is not such an object" / "clear in itself" makes luminosity the one item that is not designated, not said to be empty, and doing explanatory work. That is the shape of a self-standing ground, though the article never says "truly existing" or "empty of other". Suggested remedy, in Nyingma terms: state that luminosity is itself empty (inseparable clarity and emptiness), is the clarity of each appearance and not a factor beside conditions, and that "not such an object" means not an object of seeing, not exempt from emptiness.

## D. Dependent Designation and the Taste of Presence

### 1. Source
- File: `dependent-designation-and-the-taste-of-presence-article.md`, id `1MJcOx72JWzF71VUHbTWddD4TEpByuqeH`. Header: "Bonus article · The Transition from Anatta to Emptiness". Subtitle: "An essay on the logic of dependence, from the chariot to the immediacy of appearing." It links to the artifact (`#presence`) and two videos.
- Structure:
  - §1 The chariot, before the logic: the chariot argument "carried out in the body".
  - §2 The logic stated plainly: dependent origination as a relation, not a list.
  - §3 Dependence within appearance: redness, and the Sautrāntika objection.
  - §4 Where dependence alone does not reach: the network itself becoming a ground.
  - §5 Abstraction, reification, and svabhāva: svabhāva is a stance.
  - §6 The gap and its absence: immediacy; Longchenpa; the caution.
  - §7 Analysis as practice: dpyad sgom and gzhag sgom.
  - §8 What the essay can and cannot do: prompts, not certification.

### 2. Core theses
1. "Nothing has been denied… What has disappeared is the expectation that there would be some chariot-itself" (§1).
2. The logic of dependent origination is "a relation between factors… Nothing in the statement requires a first term, a fixed substance, or an independent agent" (§2).
3. A dependently designated unit's designation "is real in the sense that it works. It is not real in the sense of being a foundation" (§2).
4. Redness is not the wavelength, the cones or the seeing, "Yet the redness is not apart from them" (§3).
5. "A complete map of interdependence can itself become a standing-behind… The network has become a ground" (§4).
6. "What the taste of presence adds is a change of location rather than of content" (§4).
7. "The chariot shows that the whole is not found beyond the parts. The taste shows that the knower is not found beyond the knowing. These are one finding seen from two sides" (§4).
8. A ground comes from a two-step operation: abstraction, which is useful, then reification. "Only this second step creates the standing-behind" (§5).
9. "Svabhāva, then, is not located in the content of an experience… It is a matter of how the appearing is held… The difference lies entirely in the stance" (§5).
10. "The taste of presence cannot be described as a foundation the analysis finally reaches… its immediacy is that of something relating, not of something standing on its own" (§5).
11. "The gap was never holding anything up" (§6).
12. "Vividness here is not intensity added to ordinary perception. It is the absence of the mediation that normally makes perception feel distant" (§6).
13. "If the gap is a construction, its absence is a stopping rather than an accomplishment" (§6).
14. Caution: "'No gap, no ground' can become a new ground… The immediacy should be described as something that appears when the standing-behind is not being maintained, not as a state the reader can now be sure of" (§6).
15. "Analysis is not a preliminary that yields a conclusion to be accepted. It is an activity of attention" (§7).
16. "A text that attempts to certify realization, or to measure the reader's progress, has already built a new ground" (§8).

### 3. Key terms
- **dependent designation**.

### 8. Tensions
- **"What remains is the immediacy of appearing itself"** (§6). "What remains" phrasing risks implicative negation (canon 9), as if the negation left a positive residue. ### 9. Candidate canon points
1. **Svabhāva is a stance, not a content.** Red, sound or presence can be equally vivid held either way. The difference lies in how the appearing is held: as from its own side and as foundation, or as relating.
2. **A ground is a reified abstraction.** Abstraction is useful and conventional. Only reification, taking the common feature as a thing beneath the particulars, creates the standing-behind.
3. **Interdependence surveyed from above is a new ground.** The network becomes a ground when a vantage is kept. Dependence must be present in the appearing, not viewed over it.
4. **Whole not beyond parts; knower not beyond knowing.** The chariot and the taste of presence are one finding seen from two sides.
5. **The gap was never holding anything up.** Immediacy is a stopping of construction, not an accomplishment and not a bridge built. Vividness is the absence of mediation, not added intensity.
6. **No text certifies realisation.** Writing can prompt and set traps. Measuring the reader's progress builds a new ground.

### 10. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity
- **(a) Mind versus phenomena.** One emptiness applies to object and knower: "The chariot shows that the whole is not found beyond the parts. The taste shows that the knower is not found beyond the knowing. These are one finding seen from two sides." (§4) This matches MMK 24.19 in substance; the verse is not cited. The phrase "nature of mind" is not used.
- **(b) Presence, the taste of presence, vividness and immediacy.**
  - "What the taste of presence adds is a change of location rather than of content. The interdependence is not seen from above but is present in the appearing itself." (§4)
  - "Svabhāva, then, is not located in the content of an experience. Redness, sound, and the feeling of presence can be exactly as vivid whether or not they are held as svabhāva." (§5)
  - "For this reason the taste of presence cannot be described as a foundation the analysis finally reaches. Appearance seen without a standing-behind is dependent through and through, and its immediacy is that of something relating, not of something standing on its own." (§5)
  - "every taste of presence is already filtered through a gap" (§6).
  - "there is no longer a witness positioned apart from what appears, and no ground that appearances must be checked against. What remains is the immediacy of appearing itself." (§6)
  - "Vividness here is not intensity added to ordinary perception. It is the absence of the mediation that normally makes perception feel distant. The chariot's emptiness applies to the vivid as much as to the dull. The immediacy is not a quality that makes appearances more real." (§6)
- **How presence is framed.** Explicitly as empty and dependent ("dependent through and through"), and as a mode of appearing ("how they are not being held at a distance"). It is denied as a foundation and denied as a witness. The words luminosity, clarity and rigpa are not used.
- **(c) Traditions.** Longchenpa ("self-liberation of thought", §6). "Madhyamaka and Dzogchen both take the first view" (§6). dpyad sgom and gzhag sgom are cited without naming a school. Nyingma, Mipham, Gorampa, rangtong, shentong, kadag and lhun grub do not appear.
- **Shentong lean.** None. This is the most explicitly rangtong-compatible treatment of presence among the articles. The only soft spot is "What remains is the immediacy of appearing itself" (§6), which risks a residue reading (canon 9), not shentong.

### 9. Candidate canon points
1. **Two modes, one result.** Dividing and non-dividing cognition, rightly understood, point to the same seeing. Neither must be forced, and neither is the final truth of what is seen.
2. **The dividing mind is a formation, not a discovery.** The MMK uses analysis to expose analysis, and the parts are designated too.
3. **Dependent designation is the middle way itself.** It is not a compromise between existence and non-existence (MMK 24.18).
4. **Emptiness is the nature of mind and phenomena alike.** It is not confined to mind (MMK 24.19). It is not a further product of conditions and not a reality behind them.
5. **Dependent arising and spontaneous presence are two descriptions of one appearing.** The first is analytic, the second experiential. "Spontaneous" never means "uncaused source".
6. **Liberation changes how what exists is held, not what exists.** Nirvāṇa is not another place (MMK 25).
7. **Suppression is not the decisive step.** Forced stillness becomes a new object of holding. Thought is recognised as already unfixed, and in that recognition stops fixing.
8. **The two truths must be held together.** Apart, the conventional becomes really causing things and the ultimate becomes an empty ground. Joined, they dissolve each other's solidity.
9. **The witness is the flow's own clarity, not a subject.** If the witness is a separate knower, it is another svabhāva.

### 9. Candidate canon points
1. **"Beyond cause and effect" is not an exception to dependent arising.** It sets aside the intrinsic producer and keeps conditional alignment. It is not a place where causes do not operate.
2. **Three senses of cause.** The intrinsic producer is rejected. Conditional alignment is conventionally valid. The designated relatum is "the whole status of the cause".
3. **The producer-or-nothing dilemma shares one false premise.** Both horns take "cause" to name an item. Remove the item and the question of origin loses its object.
4. **The demand for an ultimate "why" is the last reification.** It searches for permission to stop searching. It is not answered. It stops applying.
5. **Analysis ends itself.** It answers a fixing habit when one is present and is complete when that habit has no object. It is not a standing condition of mind.
6. **Analysis and recognition are not two stages.** The dividing mode is the habit analysis answers. The non-dividing mode is appearing when that habit is not operating.
7. **No undesignated floor.** Something is always seen as something (duck-rabbit, reading). Designation is the forming.
8. **Appearing has no owner.** "Self-appearing" means only "not borrowed from another place". "Own" must never be read as possession.
9. **The synthesis is presented as synthesis.** The defensible claim is that both traditions identify the substantialist grasp as the problem and converge on undoing it, not that they name one nature.

### 10. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity
- **(a) Mind versus phenomena.**
  - Spontaneous presence section: "The second asks about the nature of what appears. That nature is emptiness, which is not confined to mind. There is not one phenomenon that has not arisen dependently, and so there is not one phenomenon that is not empty (MMK 24.19)."
  - This carries the same correction as E §10, but it lacks E's explicit sentence "so it is the nature of mind and phenomena alike".
  - It says "Every appearance has an answer", where E says "Every appearance, mental or material, has an answer". The two parallel texts should be aligned.
- **(b) Awareness, luminosity, ground, taste and vividness.**
  - Two traditions: "Dzogchen speaks of rang rig, self-cognizing awareness. The difficulty is that a nature that is not produced but is the ground of appearances sounds very close to the svabhāva that Madhyamaka refuses."
  - Same section (Mipham): "Self-knowing is not a second act performed on the flow of awareness. It is what the flow's luminosity is."
  - Same section: "Mipham went further and argued that rigpa's luminosity is not a svabhāva, because it is not established as an independent object. It is known only self-cognizingly and depends on nothing outside itself."
  - Same section: "Longchenpa, for his part, is explicit that the ground is empty and not a subtle existent standing behind appearances. Later commentators have argued at length that appearance-emptiness inseparability and the primordial purity and spontaneous presence of Dzogchen are identical in what they refer to."
  - Same section: "The equivalence is still contested. Some lineages hold that Dzogchen is its own resultant vehicle".
  - Taste of pacified reification: "The gap between observer and observed was a designation laid over immediate experience. It was never found in the experience itself. Once the designation stops, experience has a vividness that is hard to describe. Colours are vivid, sounds are immediate, and thoughts lose their heavy ownership."
  - Same section: "If the witness is a separate subject standing behind experience, it becomes one more svabhāva. If it is the vivid, unobstructed quality of the flow, nobody is performing the tasting and seeing."
  - Insubstantial self-appearing: "It is self-appearing only in a thin sense: the alignment of conditions is the appearing, as a flame's light is the burning and not a second thing given off by it."
  - Spontaneous presence: "It names what dependent origination looks like from the inside".
- **How these are framed.**
  - Luminosity is a quality of the flow, as in E.
  - Dzogchen's ground is "empty and not a subtle existent" (Longchenpa). This is the clearest anti-shentong statement in the set.
  - Vividness is what is tasted when the reifying designation stops.
  - The witness is denied as a subject.
- **(c) Traditions.** Named: Dzogchen, Mipham, Gorampa, Longchenpa, rang rig, rigpa, lhun grub, "primordial purity" (kadag, in English only; the Tibetan is not given), "spontaneous presence", "appearance-emptiness inseparability", zhig pa and rang grol. Not named: Nyingma, rangtong, shentong, 'od gsal and prabhāsvara.
- **Shentong lean (flags).**
  1. Mipham's rigpa "depends on nothing outside itself". This is the same flag as E.
  2. "the ground is empty". This is Longchenpa's ground (gzhi) and is explicitly not a subtle existent, so it is rangtong-compatible. However, it uses "ground" of the Dzogchen basis, which canon 1 forbids for emptiness. A gloss such as "the basis (gzhi), which is primordially pure (kadag)" would avoid a collision.
  3. "Once the designation stops, experience has a vividness…" and "immediate experience" as what designation is "laid over". These suggest an undesignated experiential floor, which contradicts the same article's "Nothing is ever found beneath the seeing as an undesignated floor" (The two modes). "Designation" is used in two senses here: reifying overlay and conventional forming. That should be marked. Otherwise "immediate experience" reads as a pure given, a shentong-like or Sautrāntika residue.
  4. "from the inside" (Spontaneous presence). This is an interior-viewpoint metaphor.

### 8. Tensions
- **"Emptiness is what allows them to appear and to act at all"** and **"It is part of why they follow"** (§2). Emptiness is given an enabling and explanatory role, against canon 1 and canon 3 (it "does not explain why appearing occurs"). Compare E §4 "condition of possibility".
- **"Habit is not a private failing. It is the common ground on which appearances take shape"** (§4). This calls habit a "ground", against canon 6 ("Conditions are not a ground beneath appearances"). It also contradicts the same section's "Habit does not sit behind appearances as a hidden cause." Suggest "common patterning".
- **"The water itself has not changed"** (§4). This posits a water-itself beneath the three impressions and contradicts the next sentence: "no single version is the water as it truly is".
- **"their solidity came from the activity of the dreaming mind"** (§2). Unqualified, this leans toward a mind-only or idealist reading. Article A explicitly rejects "the idealist extreme".
- **"Karma and habitual conditionings make up the impressions"** (§4). "Make up" could read as karma producing appearances. The text says habit is in the web, but the wording leans causal and idealist.
- **"solid" in two senses.** It is reified solidity ("the solidity lay in the habit of grasping"; "so that their solidity loosens") and also functional solidity to be affirmed ("vivid and solid in its working"). Elsewhere (A, Emotion) "vivid and solid" describes the reified "I". This needs a fixed usage.
- **Rovelli and Barad.** Saying "relation comes before the relata" contrasts with §1's own "arise together without priority". The contrast is intended, but the phrase could be misread as endorsed.
- **Editorial residue.** The final note to "check the Rovelli and Barad paragraph… before publication" is left in a published PDF. A hyphenation artefact also appears ("non- relational").
- **Metadata.** The byline "@JohnTan" differs from "John Tan (Thusness)". It uses American spelling ("Recognizing", "recognize").

### 9. Candidate canon points
1. **Two mirrored errors.** Reification takes solidity to require an own side. Its mirror takes emptiness to mean flimsiness. Both assume solidity and emptiness exclude each other.
2. **Empty is not flimsy, and not harmless.** Empty appearances are vivid and workable. They injure and heal, and their consequences are fully real.
3. **Relations are as empty as relata.** Relational physics still seeks relation as a determinate ground. Madhyamaka asks whether that ground can be found. Both are relational, but only one leaves the ground empty.
4. **Function needs only designated patterns within conditions.** Nothing must be "the true bridge" for the bridge to bear weight.
5. **Conditions are patterns too.** No bottom layer produces impressions from outside the web (MMK ch. 1).
6. **Habit is patterning, not a hidden cause.** Karma and habit shape how appearances show up as solid and separate. They belong to the same dependent web. No version of an appearance is the thing as it truly is.

### 10. Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity
- **(a) Mind versus phenomena.** Emptiness is applied to phenomena and to the self behind words: "we imagine a fixed 'self' behind the words that really means them… Yet the light works, and the kindness is felt, without any such ground being found" (§3). Mind itself is never said to be empty. Mind appears as the shaper of appearances:
  - "their solidity came from the activity of the dreaming mind" (§2).
  - "What appears to each mind is shaped by the tendencies it has accumulated, and those tendencies are themselves designated within the same web" (§4).
  - "The water that appears is shaped by the one who perceives it" (§4).
  
  MMK 24.19 is not cited. The lean here is toward mind-only or idealism (Cittamātra-style), not shentong. The canon point that emptiness is the nature of mind and phenomena alike should be added explicitly.
- **(b) Vividness.** No luminosity, clarity, awareness or presence vocabulary is used. Vividness is a property of patterns:
  - "They can be as vivid and solid in their working as anything we know" (§2).
  - "The coin is vivid" (§2).
  - "the patterns that make up the coin or the apple are vivid" (§2).
  - "the designation of a vivid pattern arising within a set of conditions" (§3).
  - "They are vivid, workable, and empty" (Closing).
  
  Vividness is framed as a feature of empty appearances, not as a ground or witness.
- **(c) Traditions.** Only Madhyamaka, Nāgārjuna and the MMK are named. The three perceptions of water (human, fish, hungry ghost) are a traditional image associated with Yogācāra and later Tibetan sources, and are unattributed here.
- **Shentong lean.** None. The idealist lean is noted above and in §8.

### I.4 Combined candidate canon points (proposed additions to the nine)
10. **Two logics.** The logic of substance begins with things and asks what happens to them. The logic of dependency asks how anything is identified as a thing at all. The same words lead the two into different worlds. The habit of substance is a default that switches on when we explain, name, feel threatened or lose. It is not a belief held all the time.

11. **Consequence, not transcendence of logic.** Madhyamaka holds the opponent to non-contradiction and shows that a self-standing thing cannot be proposed without undermining itself. Refusals such as "neither existent nor non-existent" refuse a presupposed bearer. They are not true contradictions.

12. **Change, identity and continuity without a carrier.** Change and changer are designated together. Identity is a matter of criteria for a purpose, correctable and not arbitrary. Continuity is designated across moments that are neither identical nor wholly different. The designation is the continuity, not a cover over a hidden flow. No better subject (process, flow, energy, potential, a stream of consciousness) replaces the old one.

13. **Designation is the whole status, not a veneer.** A cause is a designated relatum, and the designation "is the whole status of the cause". There is no undesignated floor beneath seeing: something is always seen as something, and designation is the forming. To call a thing designated is not to demote it. Convention is the only validity, and it is full validity.

14. **Three senses of cause.** Intrinsic production is rejected (MMK 1.1). Conditional alignment holds conventionally and does real work. The cause as designated relatum keeps alignment from sliding back into production. "Beyond cause and effect" sets aside only the first sense. It is not a place where causes do not operate.

15. **The last why is the last reification.** The demand for why appearing springs forth at all is not answered by a spontaneous ground. It loses its object and stops applying. "Spontaneous" (*lhun grub*) never means "uncaused source".

16. **Svabhāva is a stance, not a content.** The same red, sound or sense of presence can be held as from its own side and as foundation, or as relating. Grounds arise when a useful abstraction is reified. A network of interdependence surveyed from a vantage is itself a new ground.

17. **Emptiness is the nature of mind and phenomena alike, and does not enable or establish anything.** It is not confined to mind (MMK 24.19). It is what dependent arising amounts to when examined: "the way appearance is". It is not its condition of possibility, its producer or its establisher. Wording such as "established by emptiness", "what allows them to appear" or "vivid because empty" is to be avoided.

18. **Empty is not flimsy and not harmless.** There are two mirrored errors. One holds that the solid must be solid from its own side. The other holds that the empty must be faint. Empty appearances are vivid and workable, and their consequences (injury, grief, healing) are fully real.

19. **Relations are as empty as relata.** Mutual dependence is not a hidden structure tying things together. Relational accounts that take relation as the final determinate ground are reliable designations within their domains. They are not the last word on what appears.

20. **Vividness is formed, not owned, and needs no further ground.** The vividness of an appearance forms in dependence and is complete as formed, new at each moment. Luminosity names the knowing clarity of appearing. It is not a substance, not an exempt object and not a factor beside conditions, and it is itself empty.

21. **No owner, no witness behind the flow.** "Self-appearing" means only "not borrowed from another place". "Own" never means possession. If the witness is a separate subject, it is another svabhāva. As "the flow knowing itself", nobody performs the seeing. Experience needs no possessor, and death is not the loss of a held experience by a holder.

22. **Analysis ends itself; analysis and recognition are not two stages.** Analysis answers a fixing habit when one is present and is complete when the habit has no object. The non-dividing mode is how appearing is when fixing is not operating. Phrases such as "what remains" are avoided, because nothing positive is left behind (canon 9).

23. **Suppression is not the decisive step.** Forced stillness becomes a new object of holding: grasping the absence of grasping. Thought is recognised as already unfixed, and in that recognition stops fixing.

24. **Habit and karma are patterning, not a hidden cause or ground.** Accumulated tendencies shape how appearances show up as solid and separate. They belong to the same dependent web. No version of an appearance is the thing "as it truly is", and changing habit changes how the world meets us.

25. **The synthesis is named as synthesis, and no text certifies realisation.** The defensible claim is that Madhyamaka and Dzogchen agree that the substantialist grasp is the problem and converge on undoing it. Identity of referent is an argued synthesis. Writing can prompt and trap. A text that measures the reader's progress has built a new ground.

### I.5 Nature of mind and phenomena; luminous clarity (across the articles)

**(a) Same emptiness for mind and phenomena?**
- **Explicit.** E §10 (corrected): "so it is the nature of mind and phenomena alike". F (Spontaneous presence): "which is not confined to mind" plus MMK 24.19. F lacks E's "mind and phenomena alike" sentence and E's "mental or material". The two parallel passages should be aligned on E's wording.
- **In substance.** D §4: "the knower is not found beyond the knowing. These are one finding seen from two sides." A (Conventional): table, self and hour are all "not found from their own side".
- **Silent or uneven.**
  - C treats mind through luminosity and never says that luminosity or mind is empty.
  - G treats mind as the shaper of appearances ("the activity of the dreaming mind") and never says mind is empty.
  - B is not applicable.
- **Old mind-only wording.** It does not appear in any file read. E §10 already has the corrected text.

**(b) How luminosity, awareness, presence and vividness are framed**

| Article | Word(s) | Framed as | Said to be empty? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "vivid and exact"; ma 'gags pa "unobstructed" display | mode of appearing; no store behind | implicitly (everything) |
| C | luminosity = "the knowing clarity of the display"; "sufficient"; "accounts for its vividness"; "not such an object" | mode of the display, but explanatory and exempt from designation | **no** |
| D | taste of presence; immediacy; vividness = "absence of the mediation" | mode of appearing; "dependent through and through"; not foundation; not witness | **yes** |
| E | rang rig; rigpa's luminosity; "what the flow's luminosity is"; "vivid because it is empty"; natural state | quality of the flow; inseparable from emptiness; witness denied as subject; Mipham's rigpa "depends on nothing outside itself" | yes (vivid and empty at once), except the Mipham line |
| F | same as E, plus Longchenpa's "the ground is empty"; "primordial purity and spontaneous presence"; vividness "once the designation stops" | quality of the flow; Dzogchen basis empty; taste after the overlay stops | yes, except the Mipham line and the "immediate experience" floor |
| G | "vivid pattern" | property of empty appearances | yes |

- None of the eight files uses the phrases "luminous clarity", 'od gsal or prabhāsvara. "Luminosity" appears only in C, E and F.
- No file says that luminosity is itself empty in so many words. E §12 comes closest ("vivid and as empty at once").

**(c) Tradition references**
- **Dzogchen** is named in A, C, D, E and F.
- **Mipham** is named in E and F, for the lamp and for "rigpa's luminosity is not a svabhāva".
- **Gorampa** is named in E and F, only as a stricter Madhyamaka critic.
- **Longchenpa** is named in D (self-liberation of thought) and F (the ground is empty).
- **lhun grub** appears in E and F.
- **kadag** appears only in English, as "primordial purity", in F.
- **Nyingma, rangtong and shentong** are named nowhere. The author's stance (a rangtong reading of the MMK; Nyingma Dzogchen on luminous clarity, taking the MMK as its view) is therefore implicit throughout and never stated. F's "Some lineages hold that Dzogchen is its own resultant vehicle" is the only nod to the lineage question.

**Shentong-leaning sentences (consolidated)**
1. **C (Why Experience Needs No Carrier).** "Luminosity is not such an object", "This is why luminosity is sufficient", "Luminosity accounts for its vividness", and "it is vivid because it is clear in itself". Luminosity is the only item not called designated or empty, and it does explanatory work. This is the strongest lean in the set.
2. **E §7 and F (Two traditions).** Rigpa "depends on nothing outside itself". Unqualified, this reads as self-established awareness. It needs Mipham's sense (self-known, not known by another cognition) and the statement that rigpa is empty.
3. **F (Taste of pacified reification).** "a designation laid over immediate experience… Once the designation stops, experience has a vividness". This implies an undesignated given beneath designation, against F's own "no undesignated floor".
4. **E §7 and §9.** "a natural state that does not need to be made" and "recognized, not produced". These are acceptable in Nyingma terms if glossed as empty and not an uncaused base (canon 5).
5. **E §12 and F.** "the flow knowing itself" and "the flow of awareness". "Flow" can become a reified stream. C's rejection of "a stream of consciousness" as a carrier is the remedy.
6. **F.** "the ground is empty" (Longchenpa). This is doctrinally anti-shentong, but uses the word "ground" for the Dzogchen basis, against canon 1's wording.

No sentence anywhere says "empty of other" or calls luminosity truly existing.

**Additional combined candidate canon points**

26. **One emptiness for mind and phenomena.** Emptiness is the nature of mind and phenomena alike (MMK 24.19). There is no phenomenon, mental or material, that is not dependently arisen and so not empty. The nature of mind is not a second, special emptiness, and mind is not exempt as the shaper of appearances.

27. **Luminous clarity is the clarity of appearing, and it is empty.** Following Nyingma Dzogchen, which takes the MMK as its view, luminosity ('od gsal) names the knowing clarity of each appearance, inseparable from its emptiness. It is not a substance, ground, witness or factor beside conditions. It is not "empty of other" and not truly existing. "Not an object" means not an object of seeing; it does not mean exempt from emptiness.

28. **Rigpa's self-knowing is not independence.** When Mipham says rigpa is self-cognizing and "depends on nothing outside itself", this means it is not known by a second cognition (the lamp). It does not mean an independently established awareness. Rigpa is not a svabhāva because it is empty.

29. **The Dzogchen basis is primordially pure.** Where Longchenpa speaks of the ground or basis (gzhi), it is empty (kadag) and "not a subtle existent standing behind appearances". To keep canon 1 intact, articles say "basis, primordially pure" and do not call emptiness "the ground".

30. **No undesignated given.** The taste of presence or of pacified reification is not a pure experience under designation. What stops is reifying designation. Conventional designation is the forming itself. Vividness is the absence of mediation and holding, not a floor uncovered.
