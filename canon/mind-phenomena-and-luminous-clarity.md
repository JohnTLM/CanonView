# Canon View

Governs all four books and the bonus articles, together with `nature-canonical-view.md`.

**Stance.** The view is a rangtong reading of Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* (MMK): everything, mind included,
is empty of its own nature, and emptiness is a non-implicative negation. On luminous clarity the view follows
Nyingma Dzogchen, which takes the MMK as its view. Luminosity is therefore read through emptiness, never as a
ground that is "empty of other".

Sources are abbreviated as follows.
- **SW**: *The Logical Necessity of a Substrateless World*
- **AE**: *The Transition from Anatta to Emptiness*
- **MW**: *Nāgārjuna's Middle Way*
- **OP**: *The Observer and the Taste of Presence*
- **Art.**: bonus article

## Part I. The view: phenomena, mind included, emptiness and nirvāṇa

Phenomena (*dharma*) are whatever can be singled out, mental or material; mind is one of them (point 14). Where the
canon keeps the pairing "mind and phenomena", it quotes a source (the nature of mind, *sems nyid*, beside the nature
of phenomena, *chos nyid*) and marks mind as included, never as set apart.

1. **One emptiness for all phenomena, mind included.** Emptiness is "the nature of mind and phenomena alike" (*Two Modes*, §10). There is no
   phenomenon, mental or material, that has not arisen dependently, and so none that is not empty (MMK 24.19). The
   nature of mind is not a second or special emptiness.
   *SW §2.5; OP Ch 18 ("it is not confined to mind"); MW 24.19 ("every cup, thought and breath"); Art.
   Two Modes, One Seeing §10.*

2. **Empty of own nature, nothing more.** Emptiness is the absence of intrinsic nature (svabhāva). It is the
   absence of any fixing, not a hidden essence, a spacious ground or a pure awareness. Only own-nature is negated:
   "not found" does not mean "does not happen".
   *SW Interlude ("a new substance with a quieter name"); MW 24.11, 24.18; OP Ch 14, glossary "Emptiness".*

3. **Emptiness is itself empty.** It cannot become a new ground, a substrate of emptiness or a "luminous reality
   hidden behind" appearances. Negation installs nothing positive.
   *MW Ch 13, 24.18; OP Ch 18; SW §5.5; canon 1, 9.*

4. **Mind is tested like everything else and is not exempt.** A substance, a relation, a moment, a mind, a self:
   each meets the same search, and none is found standing on its own. Mind is not the ground of dependence: "Does
   the red depend on the mind, and is the mind then the ground? That question leads back to the witness."
   *SW Intro, §5.3; OP Ch 2.*

5. **Knower and known are designated together.** Consciousness is not a standing light, a container, a homunculus
   or a witness behind experience. It is always "of something, through something". "Knowing, too, is part of what
   depends." The knower is not found beyond the knowing.
   *SW §7.3–7.5; MW 5.6; AE Flower; Art. Dependent Designation §4.*

6. **Neither idealism nor physicalism.** To deny objects while keeping mind makes mind the last substance. "The
   dreamed mountain and the dreaming mind appear together and are equally unfound on waking." Mind-Only, idealism,
   panpsychism and consciousness-as-fundamental are rejected for the same reason as matter-as-substance.
   *SW §12.10 (Candrakīrti); OP Ch 6, Ch 19; AE Science.*

7. **Two truths, one appearance.** The two truths are two aspects of one appearance, not two levels. What is
   vivid and functional conventionally is unfindable ultimately. Conventional functioning is full validity, not a
   veneer. Ultimate truth is "not a higher world, but the emptiness of the conventional one".

   *Explanation.*
   - **Non-dimensional.** There is no deeper level beneath appearances (no backstage, substrate or ground) and no
     higher level above them. The two truths are "non-dimensional".
   - **Conventional truth has standards.** Tsongkhapa's three criteria sort sound conventions from mistaken ones:
     known to ordinary cognition, not overturned by further ordinary cognition, and not contradicted by analysis of the
     ultimate. The snake in the rope fails the second; the inherently existing rope fails the third; the rope as
     designated passes all three.
   - **Each needs the other.** Without relying on the conventional, the ultimate cannot be taught (MMK 24.10). Taken
     apart, each becomes a fixed object: the conventional turns into solid things really causing one another, and the
     ultimate into an empty ground behind them. Joined, they dissolve each other's solidity.
   - **Speaking positively without a ground.** Mipham's second model, concordant and discordant experience, lets
     vividness be affirmed without being made a ground: "'authentic' names a way of meeting appearance, not a luminous
     reality hidden behind it".
   - **Wording.** "Ultimately" used as an adverb ("unfindable ultimately") is conventional usage and names no level.
     The ultimate, once singled out, is itself designated (points 14–15).

   *SW §5 (incl. §5.5), §7.4; MW 24.8–10; OP Ch 13, glossary "Ultimate truth"; Art. The Logic of Dependency
   ("Conventional, Not 'Merely' Conventional"); Art. Two Modes, One Seeing §11; nature point 7.*

8. **Emptiness enables nothing and explains nothing.** Emptiness is what dependent arising amounts to when
   examined. It is not a condition of possibility, a producer or an establisher. Avoid phrasings such as "because
   they are empty, they can arise", "established by emptiness" and "vivid because empty".
   *Canon 3, 5; Art. Across the articles, point 17.*

9. **Designation is the whole status, not a label on top.** A thing and its designation arise together; the thing
   is not there first, waiting to be named. "There is no undesignated layer on which labels are placed": something
   is always seen as something. Calling a thing designated does not demote it. Convention is the only validity, and
   it is full validity.
   *SW Intro ("A Working Vocabulary"), Ch 13; AE Note on Method; Art. Beyond Cause and Effect ("the whole status of
   the cause"; "never… an undesignated floor").*

10. **Relations are as empty as the things related.** Relation, structure, field or network does not become the new
    foundation once things are seen to depend. "Relation and relata are designated together… neither is prior, and
    neither is a ground." Relational accounts in physics are reliable designations within their domains, not the
    last word on what appears.
    *SW Intro ("Two Opponents"), Ch 4; Art. Insubstantial Appearances §1.*

11. **Change without a changer, continuity without a carrier.** Change and changer are designated together, and no
    process, flow, energy or stream replaces the old subject. Continuity is designated across moments that are
    neither the same nor wholly different: the flame at midnight is "neither the flame at dusk nor an unrelated
    flame". The designation is the continuity, not a cover over a hidden flow.
    *MW 18.10; SW Ch 9; Art. The Logic of Dependency; Art. Death and Continuity; OP Ch 9, 11.*

12. **Function without essence.** Nothing acts from its own side, and nothing needs to. A key opens a lock only with
    the lock, the hand and the turning: "the 'power to open' is not found apart from them". A thing with its nature
    from its own side could not respond, combine or change.
    *SW Ch 14; MW 24.14, 24.16–20; Art. Insubstantial Appearances §3.*

13. **Empty is not flimsy.** Empty appearances are vivid and workable, "as vivid and solid in their working as
    anything we know". Their effects are fully real: a car with no essence still injures, and grief and healing
    still happen. Two mirrored errors are refused: that the solid must be solid from its own side, and that the
    empty must be faint.
    *Art. Insubstantial Appearances §2; SW §13.3; AE The Taste, Not the Argument.*

14. **Nothing singled out is exempt.** Whatever can be identified, characterized or singled out in any way, by
    perception, by concept or as "the ultimate", is dependently designated, so it exists only conventionally and is
    empty: luminosity, emptiness and nirvāṇa included. These verbs all name one act, picking out as this rather than
    that. Nothing unidentified is left over beyond it, which would be implicative negation returning.
    *MMK 5 (characteristic and characterized), 13.8, 22.11, 24.18–19, 25.19–20; SW Intro ("A Working Vocabulary").
    The sources outside the author's books cited for this point in conversation (Tsongkhapa on emptiness existing
    conventionally; Mipham's categorized and uncategorized ultimate) are unverified and are not cited here.*

15. **"Beyond identification" means nothing further to identify, never an ineffable something.** When the ultimate
    is called free of the four extremes, inexpressible or beyond concepts, this names designation and grasping coming
    to rest, with nothing found and nothing installed. It does not name a reality that exists but escapes designation.
    The phrase is itself a pointer, a designation, and what it points to is not an item standing outside emptiness.
    Read the other way, as an ineffable reality that concepts merely fail to reach, it would be an ultimate exempt from
    emptiness: implicative negation, and shentong under another name.

    *Explanation.*
    - **The two kinds of ultimate.** Some Tibetan commentators distinguish a categorized ultimate from an uncategorized
      one. The categorized ultimate is emptiness as a non-implicative negation, an absence known by reasoning; it is
      still held as a negation. The uncategorized ultimate is free of all four extremes, the negation included: the
      negation released, as MMK 13.8 asks. The canon accepts the distinction in this sense only. The categorized
      ultimate is what point 14 already covers (identified, so designated and empty), and the uncategorized ultimate
      is not a second object but the end of holding the first.
    - **How it relates to the rest of the canon.** The canon's theory points make the non-implicative negation the
      view and guard it with "emptiness is itself empty" (points 2–3). The step of releasing the negation is carried by
      the practice points: recognition is not achievement, and non-dividing seeing is appearing when no fixing occurs
      (nature points 4, 8; luminous clarity point 8). This point names that step in the theory as well.
    - **The test.** Any wording that leaves something real behind once all identification has stopped fails this
      point and point 14. "Nothing unidentified is left over" is the safeguard.

    *Sources to verify before citing in a book.* Gorampa (1429–1489) and Mipham (1846–1912) are read in the secondary
    literature as holding the categorized/uncategorized distinction. Gorampa is read as dividing the two truths by the
    apprehending mind, treating the conceivable emptiness as only nominal, and criticising Tsongkhapa for making the
    mere negation final; Gelug critics answer that his "inexpressible" ultimate risks becoming an absolute. Mipham is
    read as agreeing that the mere negation is not final while keeping conventional valid cognition firmly in place.
    None of this is yet checked against primary texts. Works to check: Cabezón and Dargyay, *Freedom from Extremes*
    (2007, on Gorampa); Pettit, *Mipham's Beacon of Certainty* (1999); Duckworth (2008) and Karma Phuntsho (2005) on
    Mipham. Do not attribute either position in the books until it is confirmed (OP verification log).

16. **Nirvāṇa is this same appearing with fixing ended.** It is not produced by practice and not an uncaused ground
    that was always there (nature point 5). It is not another place or a level above the world: there is not the
    slightest difference between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa (MMK 25.19–20). Liberation "is not a change in what exists. It is
    a change in how what exists is held." Nothing is added and nothing is removed; what ends is proliferation, which
    "stops in emptiness" (MMK 18.5). No one holds it: "'nirvāṇa will be mine' is great grasping", and no refined,
    selfless self survives to enjoy it. As anything singled out, nirvāṇa is designated and empty (point 14).

    *Explanation.*
    - **Neither existent nor non-existent.** MMK 25 refuses to make nirvāṇa a thing, an absence, both or neither. The
      canon reads this as the same refusal it makes everywhere: the question presupposes a bearer with a status of its
      own, and none is found.
    - **Recognised, not attained.** Freedom is recognised, not produced (nature point 8). Where the books keep the
      verse's word "attained" (MW's rendering of 24.10), it means conventionally realised along a path, never an
      achievement that produces freedom.
    - **The path keeps its place.** The ultimate is taught only through convention (MMK 24.10). Practice, path and
      fruit stay fully valid conventionally; what practice undoes is the habit of fixing, which takes clear knowing.
    - **The taste is ordinary.** "Seen is just seen, heard is just heard": nothing needs to be produced to take the place
      of the habit that was seen through.

    *MMK 16, 18.5, 24.10, 25; MW Ch 16, 18, 25; Art. Two Modes, One Seeing §6; OP Ch 21, 23; nature points 5, 8.*

17. **Dependent arising: alignment without a producer, and no last "why".** "Cause" has three senses, and only one is
    refused. Intrinsic production, a cause that exists on its own and delivers an effect, is rejected (MMK 1.1).
    Conditional alignment, factors that let a result appear when assembled, holds conventionally and does real work.
    "Cause" as a designated relatum, the element attention picks out when it sequences a process, keeps alignment from
    sliding back into production: the designation "is the whole status of the cause". The demand for why appearances
    spring forth at all is the last reification. It is not answered by a spontaneous ground; it loses its object and
    stops applying. "No why and because" marks that point and does not contradict dependent arising.

    *Explanation.*
    - **One fact, two sides.** Dependent arising and emptiness are one fact, seen from the side of convention and from
      the side of analysis (MMK 24.18; point 8). The eight negations describe dependent arising itself: "If a sprout
      could be found arising from its seed, it would have an arising of its own, and it would not need to depend."
    - **Dependent arising dismantles itself.** "Dependence, too, is designated." Dependent arising is not a final truth
      standing on its own side, and it does not become a network or field beneath things (point 10).
    - **"Beyond cause and effect".** The phrase sets aside only the first sense of cause. It is not a place where causes
      do not operate, and "spontaneous" (*lhun grub*) never means "uncaused source" (Part II point 5).
    - **The conventional account stands.** The twelve links describe how suffering runs while ignorance is present and
      ends when ignorance ends (MMK 26).

    *MMK 1.1, 24.18, 26; SW Thesis in Brief; AE The Last Refuge of Ontology; MW Ch 1, 26; OP Ch 17; Art. Beyond Cause
    and Effect ("Two claims held together", "The fourth assumption"); Art. The Logic of Dependency ("The Eight Extremes
    Turned Over").*

18. **Anatta is a genuine and thorough insight, but not the whole view.** The observer is fully seen through: no
    seer behind seeing, no hearer behind hearing, no owner behind experience. "In reference to the seen, there will be
    only the seen." Anatta alone does not extend that unfindability to phenomena, conditions and dependence itself.
    Emptiness does. Until it does, the taste of no-self can sit inside a view that is still substantialist:
    desynchronization. The canon does not call anatta "complete".

    *Explanation.*
    - **No one has disappeared.** Seeing, hearing and thinking are found, vivid and working; only a viewer standing
      apart from them is not. "This does not mean that nobody is seeing, and it does not mean that a person has
      disappeared." Experience is less mediated, not blank.
    - **The person remains as the mere I.** Tsongkhapa's mere I (*nga tsam*) is designated on the aggregates,
      identical with none of them and found nowhere apart from them, yet it remembers, acts and keeps promises
      (MMK 18.1, 27).
    - **Not even an egoless one.** Liberation "does not produce a refined self that has the quality of selflessness"
      (MMK 18.3). "No-self" is itself only a designation (18.6): a corrective, medicine, not a new truth to hold.
    - **The last refuge.** After anatta, "Presence quietly becomes the new observer… awareness itself becomes the new
      Self". The canon refuses this (Part II point 9): awareness and presence are as empty as the self they replaced.
    - **Appearing belongs to no one.** "Self-appearing" means only "not borrowed from another place", never possession:
      it is "what anattā looks like when no owner is being sought".
    - **The felt self.** *The Observer* Chapter 3 offers, as the author's own claim for inspection, that the seer-seen
      split is felt in the body as contraction, and that contraction is felt as a self.

    *MMK 18.1, 18.3, 18.6, 23, 27; SW Ch 9; AE Note on Method, The Starting Point, The Collapse of the Observer, The
    Great Desynchronization; OP Ch 1, 2, 3, 21; Art. Beyond Cause and Effect ("Insubstantial self-appearing"); Udāna
    1.10 (Ṭhānissaro); points 4, 5, 11, 16; Part II points 7, 9.*

19. **Elaboration stops in emptiness because what it spins around was never there.** Elaboration (*prapañca*) is
    "the mind's spinning-out of 'is', 'is not', 'it came', 'it went' around something it takes to be there". When
    the something is not found, "the spinning stops by itself. That stopping is called peace." Emptiness is not a new
    object that replaces the old ones: "It is the seeing that the objects of elaboration were never there as
    elaborated."

    *Explanation.*
    - **The root of suffering.** Liberation is the ending of action and afflictions; these come from conceptual
      construction (*vikalpa*: "pleasant", "mine", "I am wronged"), and that comes from elaboration, which stops in
      emptiness (MMK 18.5).
    - **What emptiness is for.** Its purpose is to quiet elaboration (24.7, with Candrakīrti). The work opens and
      closes on "the quieting of elaborations, peace" (homage; 25.24). Nirvāṇa is "the quieting of all
      taking-as-objects, the quieting of elaborations" (point 16).
    - **Even "empty" can be elaboration.** "Taken as a description of what he really is, even 'empty' becomes one
      more elaboration" (22.11); "those who elaborate about the Buddha… are struck down by elaboration" (22.15).
      Whatever is singled out and held as there is elaborated again (point 14).
    - **Already free, not freed.** Freedom from elaboration (*niṣprapañca*) is not "a temporary meditative suspension
      of conceptuality", nor the property of a non-conceptual state. It is "the very nature of dependent arising
      itself": "Dependent arising has never possessed the elaborations projected upon it." Analysis removes the
      projection and adds nothing.
    - **Thought is not the elaboration.** Freedom "is not the possession of any mode of cognition". Correct conceptual
      understanding "simply ceases to project elaboration onto that which has always been free" (Part II point 13).
    - **A quiet mind is not the end of it.** It is a bypass when "the cessation of conceptual proliferation is
      mistaken for the completion of wisdom". Stillness with the habit of fixing intact has not ended elaboration.
    - **Peace is not dim.** "When elaboration stops in emptiness (18.5), nothing goes dark, and nothing new is
      installed to shine." Reality is described by what is absent from it: own-nature, elaboration, plurality (18.9).
    - **Wording to watch.** AE Ye Shes calls dependent arising "primordially open, non-abiding, effortless" with an
      "inexhaustible capacity for the manifestation of appearances". The passage itself says these are "not positive
      realities inferred from emptiness"; quoted without that clause, "openness" and "capacity" read as a ground.

    *MMK homage, 11.6, 18.5, 18.7, 18.9, 22.11, 22.15, 24.7, 25.24; MW Ch 1, 11, 18, 22, 24, 25 and glossary; AE Ye
    Shes, Relentless Negation; OP glossary "Elaboration"; Art. Two Modes, One Seeing §6; points 14, 16; Part II
    points 8, 13.*

20. **Karma and rebirth are affirmed conventionally, with nothing carried and nothing cut off.** Actions bear fruit,
    in this life and beyond it, and one life conditions the next, "as one flame lights another". Both hold as
    designated, as everything holds. What is refused is the traveller, the self that would walk unchanged from life
    to life, "and its shadow, the self that would be wiped out at death".

    *Explanation.*
    - **Karma works because it is empty.** Agent, action and fruit are empty, "and this is exactly why they work". A
      karma with its own nature "could never be made, never change and never end"; it would ripen again and again
      (17.21–25). "Only an empty karma can deliver it."
    - **No bridge between deed and fruit.** "The link between action and result is real and reliable, as the link
      between rice seed and rice is. It is designated across the moments, not carried by something that travels
      through them": a melody known by heart, with no tune stored between playings (point 11).
    - **Like a magician's creation, not like nothing.** A magically made person makes another, which really acts
      (17.31–32). A mirage "is seen, and travellers turn toward it"; a dream "can make the dreamer weep". Karma is
      "fully working, and nowhere found standing by itself" (17.33).
    - **Rebirth is not a journey.** "The tradition affirms rebirth, but not as a journey. What is denied is the
      passenger": no soul, no subtle consciousness, and no "stream of consciousness flowing on like water through a
      series of pipes: a thing in all but name". The Buddha rebuked Sāti for holding that the same consciousness
      wanders on (MN 38).
    - **Neither the same nor another.** Nāgasena's lamps: the flame of the last watch is neither the flame of the
      first nor a different one; a lamp lit from a lamp, "nothing passes over, yet the second flame is not
      unconnected with the first" (Milindapañha).
    - **No link found at the joint.** If the last moment has ceased, nothing connects; if not, two lives run at once;
      if one is born exactly as the other dies, they are two (21.18–21). The continuum "is not denied as a
      convention, but it is not found as a thing that flows".
    - **Everything that matters is kept.** "Kind acts tend toward happiness and cruel acts toward suffering. Practice
      changes a person. One is responsible for what one has done." The mere I "keeps its promises and meets the
      results of what it has done" (point 18). "None of this denies past lives, future lives, or the moral weight of
      what we do."
    - **The maker of saṃsāra is not-knowing, not a person.** The twelve links keep karma, rebirth and the path;
      "what is left out is only the owner" (26.9–10). Karma comes from afflictions, afflictions from conceptual
      construction, and that from elaboration (18.5, point 19). The same chain "can be seen in a single afternoon".
    - **Affirmed, not proven by emptiness.** Emptiness removes one objection, that rebirth needs a carrier, since
      nothing ever has one. It does not show that rebirth happens: "Removing a bad argument against something is not
      the same as supplying a good argument for it." Whether a given case occurs is a matter of evidence (AE
      At the Edges of Science, "Ordinary Magic", Stevenson). The canon affirms rebirth as the tradition teaches it, held conventionally, not as
      a conclusion drawn from emptiness.

    *MMK 8.12, 17, 18.5, 21.18–21, 26, 27.11–20, 27.30; MW Ch 17, 21, 26, 27; SW Ch 1 ("Rebirth as a traveller"), Ch 8
    ("Doesn't This Evade the Question of Rebirth?"); AE At the Edges of Science ("Ordinary Magic"); Art. The Logic of Dependency ("Continuity
    Without a Carrier"), Death and Continuity; points 11, 16, 18, 19.*

## Part II. Luminous clarity

1. **Luminosity is the knowing clarity of appearing itself.** It is "not a light behind appearances, nor an
   awareness standing apart to illuminate them. It is the vividness of the event itself." A sound is heard as
   sound; the knowing and the known are not two.
   *AE Total Exertion; OP Ch 2; SW §7.10.*

2. **Luminosity is empty.** Read as a radiant substance behind experience, an awareness existing from its own
   side, luminosity "is one more ground". Read rightly, it "is not a container but a description of appearance
   itself… and it is as empty as everything else". Clarity and emptiness are inseparable. Neither owns the other.
   *SW §7.10.*

3. **Unobstructed, not enduring.** *Ma 'gags pa* means that nothing blocks the showing. It does not mean a stream
   that lasts, and there is no store behind the display from which it comes.
   *SW §3.5; OP Ch 8; Art. Logic of Dependency.*

4. **The basis is primordially pure (*ka dag*).** Following Longchenpa, the basis (*gzhi*) is called a ground
   only from a relative perspective. In the true nature of things it is empty of being a ground. It is "not a
   subtle existent thing" and not an uncaused source. Say "the basis, primordially pure", not "emptiness is the
   ground".
   *OP Note on Method, Ch 19; AE Resonances.*

5. **Spontaneous presence (*lhun grub*) is dependent arising without a producer.** It names "appearing that owes
   its completeness to nothing hidden". It is not an uncaused ground. Spontaneous presence and dependent arising are
   the same nature under two descriptions, and neither is higher.

   *Explanation.*
   - **Two questions kept apart.** The first asks how an appearance comes about: every appearance, mental or material,
     arises from causes and conditions, and the MMK's analysis works at that level. The second asks about the nature of
     what appears: that nature is emptiness, which is not produced, so "why?" does not apply to it. Keeping the two
     apart is what lets "spontaneous" and dependent arising agree. "Spontaneous" never means "uncaused source"
     (Part I point 17).
   - **Two descriptions, not interchangeable, neither higher.** Dependent arising is the analytic description, the
     appearing as described while dividing is at work. Spontaneous presence is the experiential description, the same
     appearing when the reifying habit stops: "already complete", not waiting for construction. The two are not
     interchangeable, and neither is higher than the other.
   - **Not an ingredient on top.** Spontaneous presence "does not name an additional ingredient sitting on top of
     emptiness". It is "not produced by effort, and so it is not a state one gets more of by trying harder".
   - **The subtlest hiding place.** It can become "the last and subtlest place substantialism can hide": a producer
     granted one final exemption. Point 15's test applies.
   - **Named as synthesis.** That the two are one nature under two descriptions is the author's reading; some lineages
     hold Dzogchen as its own vehicle (point 11).

   *AE Note on Method, Relentless Negation, Beyond Cause and Effect; OP Note on Method, Ch 18, 23; Art. Two Modes, One
   Seeing §10; Art. Beyond Cause and Effect ("Spontaneous presence").*

6. **Display has nothing behind it.** Thoughts are the display (*rtsal*), not obstacles in front of the nature of
   mind. Display "must not be heard as the display of something standing behind it… Display here means only this:
   dependent arising, vivid and unobstructed, with nothing behind it." Recognised as already unfixed, thought
   self-releases (*rang grol*).
   *AE Ye Shes.*

7. **Self-knowing is not a self-standing knower.** *Rang rig* and rigpa do not posit a witness or an awareness
   that is independently established. "If the witness is a separate subject standing behind experience, it
   becomes one more *svabhāva*." The Yogācāra self-awareness critiqued by Candrakīrti is not adopted.
   *OP Ch 19, Ch 21.*

8. **Vividness is the absence of mediation.** It is "not intensity added to ordinary perception… not a quality
   that makes appearances more real". It is not an undesignated floor uncovered when designation stops. What
   stops is reifying, and appearances are seen without being held at a distance.
   *OP Ch 20; Art. Across the articles, point 30.*

9. **The taste is affirmed; the reification is refused.** Pure awareness, luminous presence and mirror-like
   clarity are genuine tastes: "neither mistaken nor fabricated". The error is the last refuge, where "the self
   may dissolve, while awareness itself becomes the new Self… luminosity quietly assumes the role once occupied
   by substance".
   *AE Starting Point, Spiritual Bypass.*

10. **Ye shes is prajñā completed.** Gnosis is not a second attainment or a separate faculty. It is "the same
    clarity, no longer needing an object apart from itself to be clear about".
    *AE Ye Shes.*

11. **The Madhyamaka–Dzogchen union is named as the author's synthesis.** Reasoning and direct recognition
    converge on one nature from two directions. The books present this as a Madhyamaka reading of Dzogchen, not as
    Dzogchen's self-understanding in every lineage. Some lineages treat Dzogchen as its own resultant vehicle.
    *AE Resonances; OP Note on Method.*

12. **Rigpa is the knowing of the basis, not an awareness that is the basis.** In Dzogchen, rigpa (*vidyā*,
    "knowledge") is the knowledge of the basis, and some sources call it the pristine awareness that is the basis
    itself. The canon takes it in the first sense only: recognising appearing as unfixed, dependent and empty. It is
    not a witness, not a self-standing knower and not a ground. "Knowing clarity" is the books' gloss, not a
    translation.

    *Explanation.*
    - **Its opposite.** Non-recognition (*ma rigpa*) is not a hidden nature going unseen: appearing "is taken as fixed,
      and its unfixed, dependent character goes unrecognised. Nothing hidden is waiting behind it to be found."
    - **Self-knowing without a second act.** Against Candrakīrti's critique of self-awareness (the blade that cannot
      cut itself, *Madhyamakāvatāra* ch. 6), the Buddhist logicians' lamp (Dharmakīrti, Prajñākaragupta) reads
      self-knowing as "not a second act performed on the flow of awareness"; read so, "like the flow it is empty, not
      an independent awareness". The canon accepts this only with that last clause (point 7).
    - **Recognised, not built up.** Recognition (*ngo shes*) is decisive and often identified with awakening. It is
      not "a state built up through effort, or a result gained by practice", and it still takes clear knowing.
    - **Display and self-release.** Thoughts are rigpa's display (*rtsal*), with nothing behind it; recognised as
      unfixed, they release themselves (*rang grol*) (point 6).
    - **Designated and empty.** Rigpa, once singled out, is designated and empty like everything else (Part I
      points 14–15). Ye shes is prajñā completed, not a second faculty (point 10).
    - **Not to be reintroduced** (OP verification log): Mipham's lamp reply; "rigpa's luminosity is not a svabhāva
      because it is not an independent object"; the rtogs pa / ngo shes distinction.

    *OP Note on Method (fourth departure), Ch 3, 19, 23, glossary "Rigpa", "Rang rig", "Recognition"; AE Ye Shes;
    Art. Two Modes, One Seeing §7 and Beyond Cause and Effect (as corrected, live site version 63). On translating
    rigpa, the OP glossary cites Ācārya Malcolm Smith.*

13. **Self-release: a thought frees itself because nothing stands behind it.** Thoughts are display (*rtsal*), not
    a veil in front of the nature of mind. Recognised as unfixed, a thought releases itself as it arises (*rang
    grol*), "like a drawing on water, which needs no eraser". Unrecognised, the same display is taken as solid and
    binds. "The difference is never the thought. It is whether the thought is read as a thing standing on its own."

    *Explanation.*
    - **The word.** The canon says self-release (*rang grol*), not "self-liberation" (Part III).
    - **Not suppression.** Suppression pushes thoughts down and can make a stillness that "becomes one more fixed
      state": "grasping the absence of grasping". Self-release lets thoughts dissolve because they are not followed
      or fixed. Madhyamaka says the same from its side: Candrakīrti accepts śamatha as a conventional means, but the
      decisive step is analysis. Practice is not a campaign against thinking.
    - **A stopping, not an achievement.** "In not being followed they do not become a wall." The gap between knower
      and known is constructed, so its absence is a stopping, available wherever the construction is not running.
    - **"Self" means no outside remedy, not a self.** No antidote is applied from outside. There is no self-standing
      knower doing the releasing (point 7), and nothing real is set free. As with nirvāṇa (Part I point 16),
      liberation is "not a change in what exists. It is a change in how what exists is held": the ending of the act
      of fixing.
    - **Empty too.** Self-release, once singled out, is designated and empty (Part I point 14). It is not a new state
      to possess: "'No gap, no ground' can become a new ground."
    - **Source caution** (OP verification log). Longchenpa's *Sems nyid rang grol* is cited for its theme only. No
      passage on thoughts was confirmed in the Guenther or Thondup translations, so the description of thoughts not
      being followed is the book's own.

    *AE Ye Shes ("Thought Is Not the Veil"); OP Ch 19, Ch 20, glossary "Rang grol"; Art. Two Modes, One Seeing §6–8.*

14. **There is no background awareness: clarity is the appearing itself.** "The sound does not need a listener
    behind it in order to be clear. The visible does not require a background awareness in order to be vivid. The
    clarity is the manifesting itself, not something it possesses." The taste of presence is genuine; making it the
    Self, the ground or an ever-present witness is the subtlest form of the same fixing. (The books do not use the
    phrase "I AM"; they treat it as the witness, the observer, Presence and background awareness.)

    *Explanation.*
    - **The witness is looked for and not found.** "You will find a tree and a seeing. You will not find a viewer
      standing apart from both." Even "the sense of a someone who hears… is itself something that appears… It is not
      the hearer."
    - **Nobody has disappeared.** "This does not mean that nobody is seeing, and it does not mean that a person has
      disappeared… The one who sees is not found apart from the seeing." The person remains as the mere I (Part I
      point 18).
    - **No blank either.** When the witness is not found, "experience does not go blank… The tree is more vivid, not
      less." What goes is the distance, not the vividness (point 8).
    - **The taste is real; the reification is the error.** Pure awareness, luminous presence, mirror-like clarity and
      boundless consciousness are "neither mistaken nor fabricated". But "the observer may disappear, while Presence
      quietly becomes the new observer… The centre has disappeared, yet the background remains", and it is hard to
      see "because it feels so complete" (point 9).
    - **The decisive question.** "Does Presence itself inherently exist? … If the self can be examined, why not
      awareness? … If inherent existence cannot be granted to the individual, on what basis should it be granted to
      consciousness itself?" (Part I points 4, 14).
    - **Not an invariant that switches on and off.** Deep sleep and anaesthesia are "quieter configurations of
      appearance", not an awareness "turned off". "If an entity were truly invariant and unconditioned, it could not
      interact with, perceive, or be affected by changing conditions."
    - **Not a ground or a witness under another name.** Luminosity is "not a light behind appearances, nor an awareness
      standing apart to illuminate them" (point 1). Rigpa is the knowing of the basis, not an awareness that is the
      basis (point 12). "If the witness is a separate subject standing behind experience, it becomes one more
      *svabhāva*" (point 7). Knower and known are designated together (Part I point 5).
    - **Losing the background is not yet the whole view.** In total exertion "the phenomenology is authentic, but the
      interpretation remains substantialist": the background can return as one "field of luminous immediacy", as
      process or as a seamless whole. It ends when emptiness extends the same unfindability to appearances,
      conditions and dependence itself (Part I point 18).

    *OP Ch 1, Ch 2, Ch 21; AE The Starting Point, The Taste of Total Exertion ("Luminous Clarity Without Background
    Awareness"); SW Ch 8 ("The Fallacy of the 'Ontological Off-Switch'", "Sleep and Anaesthesia"), Ch 9 ("The Problem
    of the Invariant Bearer"); points 1, 7, 8, 9, 12; Part I points 4, 5, 14, 18.*

**Emptiness used as an explanation (Part I, point 8)**
- SW §15.6 "precisely because they are empty"
- AE "Radiance is immediate precisely because it is completely empty"
- MW Ch 24 "That openness is emptiness"; Ch 26 "it is why the world can work"
- Art. Two Modes §4 "condition of possibility"; §13 "established by emptiness"
- Art. Insubstantial Appearances §2 "Emptiness is what allows them to appear"

**Mind left unemptied**
- Art. *Insubstantial Appearances* treats mind as the shaper of appearances ("the activity of the dreaming mind")
  and never says that mind is empty.
- Art. *Death and Continuity* treats mind through luminosity alone.

**Rebirth left neutral rather than affirmed (point 20)**

Added 2026-10-22; fixed on the site in version 70. These passages refuse a carrier, so they do not conflict with emptiness, but they stop
short of the canon's conventional affirmation of rebirth.
- SW Ch 8, "Doesn't This Evade the Question of Rebirth?": continuity beyond death is "paradigm-relative"; "Within the
  Buddhist paradigm, karmic continuity is designated across lives… a physical paradigm traces continuity through matter
  and energy. What this book claims is only what holds in every framework."
- Art. *Death and Continuity*: the view "does not promise that anything of the person continues as the person"; death
  is "the ending of a set of conditions for a particular vividness". Both read as agnostic. Better: nothing continues
  *as a carrier*, while the conditioning of one life by the next is affirmed conventionally.

**Wording that hints at a hidden presence**
- OP Ch 3 "the nature of appearing is there, and it goes unrecognised"
- OP Ch 23 "the habit that obscures it"
- SW §13.3 "Appearance becomes luminous". Better: it is met as luminous.

**Stance not stated anywhere**
- No text uses the words rangtong, shentong, 'od gsal or prabhāsvara, and Nyingma appears almost nowhere.
- MW is entirely silent on luminosity.
- OP anchors its Madhyamaka in Candrakīrti and Tsongkhapa and leaves the question between Gorampa and Mipham open.

- the rtogs pa / ngo shes distinction
